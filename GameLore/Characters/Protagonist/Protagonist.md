### Physical Appearance

She looks ready for a fight. There is nothing soft or decorative about her physique.

Her build is muscular in the way that comes from years of real training, not magical enhancement. Defined arms, visible abs, strong legs -- the kind of body that communicates danger before she ever casts a spell. She has been physically training since before the Black Rose, and trained harder after the spy incident at age 15. The Blaze Network does not affect the body, so every muscle on her is from actual work done in real time. Her physique is the one thing about her that is entirely, honestly hers -- earned without shortcuts, without time compression, without anyone's help.

She is still a teenager, and her frame reflects that -- she does not have the mass of a fully grown adult. But within that frame, there is no wasted space. She is compact, powerful, and visibly conditioned.

She carries herself like an elf. She speaks like one, moves through social customs like one, thinks within elven frameworks -- because it is the only culture she has ever known. She is not imitating. She simply *is* this, in every way except blood.

People either admire or resent her for a gift she does not actually have. She knows she is not special. She just studied longer than anyone realizes.

## Origin

A war broke out in the protagonist's birth country, killing her entire biological family. She was left as a baby in the aftermath -- no name, no lineage, no record. Everything about where she came from was erased by the conflict.

A young elf brother and sister came across the baby during or after the war.

The **brother** wanted to leave her. A human infant in a warzone was not his responsibility, and bringing a human child into elven society would invite complications he did not want.

The **sister** refused to walk away. She picked up the baby out of compassion and decided to bring her along.

The two siblings eventually relocated to an elf-controlled nation, using their family connections and influence to settle there. They brought the human baby with them.

## Growing Up Among Elves

The protagonist's childhood was defined by two relationships that pulled in opposite directions.

### The Sister -- Adoptive Mother Figure

The sister rescued the protagonist as a baby. For a time, that compassion held. She cared for the child, raised her within elven customs, and gave her a home.

Then reality set in.

The sister had a biological child -- a full-blooded elf. Her attention and affection shifted entirely to the baby who was truly *hers*. The protagonist, the human child she had saved, began to fade into the background.

Elves live approximately 1,000 years. To the sister, the protagonist's entire lifespan -- birth to death -- would occupy a fraction of her own life. Over time, she came to see the adopted child less as a daughter and more as something temporary. A pet. Something she had taken in out of a momentary impulse that she would outlive by centuries.

This was not outright cruelty. It was something quieter and more devastating: casual, unthinking dismissal. The sister did not hate the protagonist. She simply stopped seeing her as fully real.

### Sirus Vel Ouef -- Adoptive Father Figure

Sirus's arc moved in the opposite direction from the sister's.

He had not wanted the baby. He had argued to leave her behind. But as the years passed, he watched the sister's compassion curdle into indifference, and he saw what it did to the child. He felt the protagonist's confusion, her loneliness, her slow understanding that the person who had rescued her did not consider her a real daughter.

He did not confront the sister directly or intervene in dramatic fashion. What he did was quieter: he acknowledged the protagonist as his daughter in the ways that mattered. He was present. He was steady. He made the protagonist feel loved before he ever started training her -- that came first and never wavered.

Later, Sirus had his own biological child. It changed nothing. He treated both children equally -- his blood and his choice carried the same weight. Where the sister's love fractured along the line of biology, Sirus's held.

He became the protagonist's emotional anchor. The proof that someone in her life saw her as permanent, even when she wasn't.

## Training and Growth

The protagonist is a human in a world built for elves. She has no magical lineage, no prophecy, no destiny. Everything she is capable of was built, not given -- and she was always behind.

### Sirus's Teaching

Sirus did not start training the protagonist from the beginning. He made her feel loved -- that came first -- but formal magic instruction came later, after the Elidyr encounter.

When the protagonist came back from her three months with Elidyr carrying a new discipline and a visible interest in magic, Sirus noticed the shift. She was not just existing in the elven world anymore. She wanted to learn. She wanted to be capable. That was when he stepped in.

He held her to the same standards as an elf child. Where the sister saw a pet unworthy of serious investment, Sirus saw a student who deserved the same rigor he would give his own biological child. This was the practical expression of his love: he treated her as capable, and then demanded she live up to it.

Sirus eventually got the protagonist enrolled in school alongside elf children, but she entered far behind -- the equivalent of an eight-year-old starting formal education for the first time while her classmates already had years of foundation.

Elves pick up magic better than humans. This is biological fact, not a question of effort or character. The protagonist was always lagging behind her elf classmates no matter how hard she worked. She could learn what they learned, but never at their pace.

Despite the gap, Sirus's standards ensured she was at least competent. Functional in magic. Never elite by elven measures, but not helpless either.

### Elidyr T. Lumo -- Spellblade Captain

The protagonist was eight years old, wandering the streets when she should have been in school. Elidyr T. Lumo -- a Spellblade Captain in the elven military, an officer who had mastered both blade combat and tactical war magic -- kept encountering her in places she had no business being. After too many encounters, Elidyr scolded her. Not gently. The way a commanding officer scolds a soldier caught derelict: get off the street, go to school, get smarter, get stronger.

The protagonist took it as an order.

She came back the next day. And the next. Day after day, she returned to the area near Elidyr's 3rd house, presenting herself as if reporting for duty. Elidyr did not choose to mentor her. The protagonist chose Elidyr by refusing to stop showing up.

Elidyr was only in town for three months. A short stay before moving on to whatever her duties demanded. She never softened. She gave orders, not encouragement. She assessed, she corrected, she demanded more. Then she left.

Three months was enough. Elidyr gave the protagonist something the brother could not -- the perspective of someone who did not love her. The brother's standards came from devotion. Elidyr's came from discipline alone. For a child unsure whether she was truly capable or just being indulged, Elidyr's cold rigor was clarifying. And when Elidyr moved on, the protagonist kept following the orders she had been given. The discipline stuck without the disciplinarian.

But even Sirus's training and Elidyr's influence combined were not enough. They made her capable for a human. They did not make her capable of surviving what lay ahead.

### The Black Rose

What actually prepared the protagonist was a chance encounter at age 14-15 with the Black Rose -- a seemingly small, secretive collective of Black elf-human hybrids.

A professor within the group -- later known to the protagonist as **the Professora** -- believed the protagonist was half-elf and brought her into the fold. The protagonist's elven mannerisms, speech, and cultural fluency made the mistake easy to make.

When the Black Rose realized she was fully human, adopted rather than hybrid, her execution was ordered immediately. Not expulsion. Death. She had seen too much and knew too much. The Black Rose's security was absolute, and a breach like this -- an outsider let in through a mistaken identity -- could not be tolerated.

### The Spy Incident

The Black Rose sent assassins. The protagonist fought them off and put up a remarkable fight for a fifteen-year-old. It was impressive. It was not enough. She would have been killed.

What saved her was what she did next.

She hunted down a spy who had infiltrated the Black Rose. The protagonist's mistaken admission had created exactly the kind of security breach a spy could exploit, and she knew it. She found the infiltrator, engaged them, and killed them. It was her first kill.

The Black Rose watched silently. They observed her capture the spy, take them out, and show no hesitation -- while being completely at peace with losing. She was not doing this to bargain for her life. She believed the Black Rose needed to exist for kids like her: kids caught between worlds, behind in magic, overlooked and underestimated by elven society. She was willing to die to protect that mission.

This act -- loyalty without self-interest, conviction beyond survival -- changed their minds.

### Staying, Training, and Leaving

After the spy incident, the Black Rose wanted the protagonist under their watch permanently. She knew too much. She had seen too much.

The Professora advocated for a different path. Because the protagonist was fully human -- not hybrid, not elf -- there was an option available that would not work on elf or hybrid minds: targeted memory erasure of critical details.

Had the protagonist been half-elf, she would never have been allowed to leave. Elf and hybrid minds resist this kind of selective memory manipulation. But a human mind could be precisely edited.

The arrangement: the protagonist could leave freely. The critical details -- the Blaze Network's existence, its location, how it works, its mechanics -- would be erased from her memory. She would keep the training and knowledge she had gained. She simply would not remember the Blaze Network or how she acquired decades of magical study.

What she keeps: she remembers the **Black Rose** by name. She remembers the **Professora**. She remembers being part of the group. But she is much more of an outsider now -- her only real connection to the organization is the Professora herself. The group's deeper secrets, its infrastructure, its methods are gone from her mind. She knows she was involved with them. She does not know what they actually do.

The Professora personally performed the memory erasure. She knew the protagonist's full story -- the war, the adoption, the sister's indifference, the brother's quiet love, the years of falling behind -- and she chose to give this girl her freedom rather than a lifetime of confinement or a quiet death.

The protagonist left the Black Rose with ~60 subjective years of magical knowledge burned into her mind. She knows the group's name. She knows the Professora. She knows she was part of something. But the how -- the Blaze Network, the time compression, the knowledge dump -- is gone. She knows things she should not be able to know at 17. She cannot say how she learned them.

## The Blaze Network

*The protagonist does not remember any of this. These details exist as worldbuilding context, not as her lived knowledge.*

The Blaze Network is the Black Rose's greatest secret: a portal to a location where years pass in seconds of real-world time. It is how elf-human hybrids -- beings who do not live as long as pureblood elves -- learn magic at a seemingly impossible pace, compressing decades of study into moments.

### Rules of the Blaze Network

**No aging. No physical change.** People who enter do not age. No matter how much they train or work out inside, their bodies never change. Muscles do not grow. Physical conditioning does not stick. Injuries from training do not accumulate.

**What they keep is knowledge.** The Blaze Network is a glorified study room. Spells, theory, technique, mental discipline -- all of it transfers. The body you bring in is the body you leave with, but your mind carries everything.

**There is no life inside.** No socializing. No living. No experiences beyond study. It is unnatural by design. The Black Rose built it for one purpose only.

### The Knowledge-Dump Technique

The Black Rose does not want users to change too quickly after leaving. Rapid behavioral shifts would hint at the Blaze Network's existence. To prevent this, they devised a technique to dump all learned knowledge into someone's mind at once upon exit.

The result: painful, multi-month headaches. The memories are burned into the mind, but they feel like they happened a long time ago -- distant, faded, like recalling something from early childhood rather than something experienced moments before.

People do not usually change after leaving. Not even personality. The knowledge sits in the head but it does not feel recent enough to reshape who someone is. The person knows things they could not have known, but they do not feel like a different person.

This is deliberate. If someone walked out acting like they had decades of experience, it would raise suspicion. The knowledge-dump keeps the secret safe by making the learning feel ancient and half-forgotten.

### The Black Rose Protects This Secret Violently

The Black Rose actively kills snitches and anyone who appears to be developing something similar. The Blaze Network is their survival advantage in a world dominated by pureblood elves. If the secret were exposed, elves might destroy it entirely -- or worse, eliminate the hybrids who built it.

### The Elven Equivalent

The elves have their own version of time-compressed training. The key difference: the elven version ages the user, and training inside affects the body. Physical conditioning, combat muscle memory, magical attunement -- all of it carries over. But so does the aging.

Elves can afford this tradeoff. Burning fifty years inside a training chamber is a rounding error on a thousand-year lifespan. For a hybrid who might live two or three hundred years, or a human who gets eighty, that same fifty years is devastating.

The Black Rose reverse-engineered the elven version specifically for beings without centuries to spare. They stripped out the aging and physical effects, keeping only the knowledge transfer. The deliberate tradeoff: you cannot build your body inside, but you will not lose years of your life either.

### The Protagonist's Experience

The protagonist spent approximately 60 subjective years inside the Blaze Network.

She is 17 years old physically. She carries 60 years of learned magical knowledge -- theory, spellwork, technique. Because of the knowledge-dump method, those 60 years feel distant and faded, like they belong to someone else. She does not feel 60. She does not act 60. She endured the multi-month headaches after leaving.

She knows things she should not be able to know at 17. She is still herself -- still the same 17-year-old personality, the same emotional landscape, the same unresolved pain. The Blaze Network gave her knowledge, not wisdom.

### After the Black Rose -- The Last Two Years

The protagonist left the Black Rose with 60 years of magical knowledge she half-remembers, the group's name, and the Professora. The Blaze Network is gone from her mind. Nearly two years remained before the game begins. She did not stop.

She continued training. She continued studying. The knowledge from the Blaze Network sat in her mind like faded muscle memory, and she spent these years sharpening it through real practice -- turning distant theory into lived skill.

But she also wanted something else. Something the elven world had never offered her.

**Mechanics and robotics.** She wanted to connect with her more human roots -- the practical, physical, engineering side of capability that a half-breed elf would be forced to forgo in favor of magical study. Elven society prioritizes magic. Machines are human things, beneath elven attention. For a human girl raised in that world, choosing to learn mechanics was a quiet act of identity: she was not an elf, and she was done pretending the only path to competence ran through magic.

She was allowed to study. She learned how machines work, how to diagnose failures, how to repair systems. She does not own a mech -- she cannot afford one -- but she can repair one if needed. She understands the engineering, the components, the logic of mechanical systems in a way that most magic-focused fighters never bother to learn.

This is a practical skill, not a passion project. In a world where mecha suits bridge the gap in low-UE realms, knowing how to fix one is the difference between a crew that can adapt and a crew that is stranded. The mechanic on Ys's ship handles the day-to-day, but the protagonist is not helpless if something breaks.

## Weapons

### The Meta Morph Blade

The protagonist's primary weapon is the **Meta Morph Blade** -- a shape-shifting weapon that can morph between four distinct forms. It is a single weapon with multiple configurations, not four separate weapons. She carries one thing. It becomes what she needs.

| Form | Role | Strengths |
|---|---|---|
| **Pike** | Default / general purpose | Balanced. Reach, control, spacing. Her go-to in most situations. |
| **Long Sword** | Magic-focused combat | Highest magic-infused potential. The form she uses when channeling spells through her weapon. |
| **Dual Short Swords** | Pure physical combat | Magicless sword techniques. Speed, aggression, close-quarters pressure without relying on any magical enhancement. |
| **Scythe** | High-risk, high-reward | Tricky to use. Difficult timing, wide arcs, punishing if she misses. But this form carries her most powerful special moves. |

**Pike** is where she lives. It is the balanced option -- good reach, good defense, good offense. When she is not sure what a fight demands, she starts here. The pike is also what she uses to deflect and parry bullets.

**Long Sword** is for when magic matters. In realms with functional magic (like the Swahili Realm's mid-tier low UE), the long sword lets her channel spells through the blade, combining melee strikes with magical effects. The weapon becomes a conduit. This is where her 60 years of magical study meets her combat training most directly.

**Dual Short Swords** are the opposite. No magic. Pure technique. When she splits the blade into two shorter weapons, she is committing to physicality -- speed, footwork, aggression. This form is for situations where magic is unreliable, unavailable, or simply not fast enough. It is also a statement: she can fight without magic. The muscles are real. The training is real. She does not need spells to be dangerous.

**Scythe** is the risk. The form is difficult -- wide, sweeping arcs that leave her exposed if she mistimes them, awkward recovery windows, a reach profile that demands precise spacing. But the scythe carries her most powerful special moves. When she shifts to this form, she is committing to ending the fight with something decisive. It is not a form she stays in. It is a form she switches to for a specific moment, then switches out of.

### The Hand Cannon

Her gun. Last resort. Enough stopping power to halt a garbage truck at 90 mph with a single shot. She does not want to use it. When she draws the hand cannon, the situation has gone beyond what the Meta Morph Blade and magic can handle. It is her emergency override -- overwhelming force applied to a problem that has no other solution.

## Iris Lirra -- The Professora

Iris Lirra is the Black Rose member who originally mistook the protagonist for half-elf and brought her into the group. She carries responsibility for nearly getting the protagonist killed, and she is the one who ultimately gave the protagonist her way out.

During the protagonist's time with the Black Rose, the two formed a genuine bond. Iris learned the protagonist's full history and understood what it meant to be a human child raised among elves -- the sister's fading attention, the brother's quiet devotion, the constant gap between effort and ability. As a hybrid herself, Iris lived a version of the same pain -- not elf enough for elves, not human enough for humans. The shape of it was familiar.

She was personally in charge of erasing the protagonist's critical memories -- specifically the Blaze Network and its mechanics. Iris herself was not erased. The protagonist remembers her, remembers her name, remembers the bond. But the relationship exists in a strange limbo: the protagonist knows Iris matters to her, knows they were close, but cannot fully reconstruct why. The context that would explain the depth of the bond -- the Blaze Network, the training, the decision to spare her life -- is gone. What remains is Iris as her only real connection to a group she is otherwise an outsider to.

## Kim Tiernan

Kim Tiernan is the protagonist's oldest friend. He is not special. He is not a warrior, not a chosen one, not someone with extraordinary abilities. He is an ordinary person whose significance comes entirely from the depth of his bond with the protagonist and the choice he made.

At some point, Kim sacrificed his soul to save the protagonist and her entire adoptive family -- the brother, the sister, and their children. He did not just save the protagonist. He saved everyone she considered family, because he considered her family too.

This was not merely dying. In the metaphysical framework of the Mirralata System, souls are tangible and can be offered as payment or sacrifice. Kim gave his away entirely, condemning himself to agony beyond death -- suffering that continues past the point where life ends, with no afterlife, no peace, no release.

### The Containment

The protagonist could not prevent Kim's sacrifice, but she managed to partially intervene. She caught what was left of him before he was completely obliterated, containing his being in what can be described as a bottle -- a temporary vessel that preserves what remains of who he is.

Kim is still present within the vessel. Still conscious. Still himself. The protagonist can talk to him, and he can talk back. He is not a ghost or an echo. He is *him*, compressed into a failing container.

The containment is not permanent. The vessel is degrading. She is on a time limit. If she does not restore Kim before the vessel gives out, he will be lost completely -- obliteration. No afterlife. No soul. Nothing.

This is the ticking clock that drives the game's journey.

### Kim's Family

Kim's family told the protagonist to stop. To let him go. To not burden herself with this quest.

They knew how close Kim and the protagonist were. They did not want to watch her destroy herself chasing something that might be impossible. Kim was a hero to his own family too -- he had saved them as well. But they accepted the loss in a way the protagonist cannot.

She carries their words with her alongside the bottle. The people Kim died to protect are telling her to move on, and she refuses.

---

## The Decision to Go

Everyone who loves the protagonist told her not to do this.

Kim's family asked her to let go. The brother, though he would never stop her, understood the danger. The weight of a journey to the center of the Mirralata System -- whatever that entails -- is not lost on anyone.

She went anyway. Not because she believes she is strong enough. Not because she believes she is destined for this. She went because Kim is still in the bottle, still conscious, still suffering, and the vessel is failing. Every day she waits is a day closer to losing him forever.

The journey is not heroism. It is obligation and love tangled together until she cannot tell which one is pulling her forward.

## Abilities and Fighting Style

The protagonist fights with elf-trained magic and combat techniques executed through a human body. This creates a distinctive style defined by the tension between what she knows and what her body can do.

**Magical knowledge far beyond her physical frame.** She carries ~60 subjective years of magical study. Her theoretical understanding and spell repertoire rival elves with decades of conventional training. But her body is still that of a 17-year-old human -- she does not have the physical conditioning, reflexes, or magical attunement that come from the elven time-compression chambers, because the Blaze Network strips all physical effects.

**Clever application over raw power.** With above-average IQ and EQ but no innate brilliance, she relies on deep knowledge applied with resourcefulness rather than overwhelming force. She knows more techniques than most opponents expect, and she uses them in combinations that suggest real experience rather than classroom theory.

**Wall running and agile combat.** Her fighting style favors speed, positioning, and momentum over brute strength. Wall running, rapid repositioning, and advance-mode techniques allow her to compensate for a human body's limitations against magically enhanced elven opponents. She fights like someone who has always been physically outmatched and learned to survive through movement and timing.

**The gap between knowledge and body.** She knows spells and techniques that should take decades to learn, but she executes them with the stamina, reflexes, and physical resilience of a teenager. Under sustained pressure, her body fails before her knowledge does.

## Personality and Voice

### The Social Side -- Day-to-Day

**Cocky.** She knows she earned what she has, and it shows. Not arrogance from believing she is better than everyone -- confidence from knowing she has put in more work than anyone around her realizes. She wears it openly. It is not a front. She genuinely believes she can handle what comes, because she has handled worse while being less capable than she is now.

**Edgy, inappropriate humor.** She loves jokes that make people uncomfortable. Not cruel -- she does not target wounds -- but she will push a line and enjoy the reaction. Dark humor, badly timed humor, humor that makes people glance at each other wondering if she really just said that. This is how she engages with the world when she is relaxed. If the room is tense, she will make it worse on purpose and then laugh about it.

**Reads the room easily.** High EQ in action. She picks up on tension, subtext, and unspoken dynamics fast. She knows when to push and when to back off -- she just does not always choose to back off. The awareness is always running. She sees the power dynamics, the discomfort, the things people are not saying. She uses this to navigate social situations, to mess with people, and occasionally to defuse something before it explodes.

**Messes with people she likes.** Teasing, provoking, testing boundaries. If she is bothering you, she probably likes you. If she leaves you alone, she is indifferent. The people closest to her get the worst of it -- relentless, targeted, affectionate harassment that only stops when she gets bored or finds a new angle.

**Gets bored easily.** Downtime is not her friend. She needs stimulation -- problems to solve, people to poke at, something to take apart or figure out. Stillness makes her restless. Long stretches of safety are harder for her than short stretches of danger.

**Good manners underneath.** Elven upbringing. She understands decorum, protocol, the bigger picture. She can turn the cockiness off when it matters -- when the situation demands respect, when she is outranked, when being flippant would cost more than it is worth. She chooses not to most of the time, but the capability is there. She speaks with elven formality and precision when she needs to, and it catches people off guard because they assumed the cocky girl was all she was.

**Would rather not resort to violence.** She communicates capability without making threats. The muscles, the posture, the calm -- the message is clear without a word being said. She would prefer to talk her way through, read the situation, and find the exit that does not involve bloodshed. But if someone needs to know that violence is on the table, she will let them know. Not loudly. Just clearly.

**Learns fast.** New environments, new rules, new threats -- she adapts quickly. Not from genius, but from a trained habit of absorbing and applying information under pressure. Sixty subjective years of study built a mind that processes new systems efficiently. She may not be the smartest person in the room, but she is often the fastest to adjust.

**A little ruthless, not overly so.** She will make hard calls. She will sacrifice comfort for progress. She will use a captured enemy as bait without losing sleep over it. But she is not cruel and does not enjoy suffering. The ruthlessness is practical, not sadistic. She does what the situation requires and moves on.

### The Combat Side -- Escalation Levels

The protagonist does not have a single combat mode. She has a spectrum, and where she lands on it depends on how serious the situation is.

**When she is killing:** Cold. Silent. She does not talk. No quips, no taunts, no battle cry. The cocky, joke-cracking girl disappears entirely. Her demeanor drops to something flat and mechanical -- the warmth, the humor, the social awareness all switch off. What remains is trained focus and intent. She enters violence the way someone enters a room they have been in a thousand times: without ceremony. People who only know her from conversation are unsettled when they see her fight. People who only know her from combat are surprised when she cracks a joke.

**When she is fighting but not killing:** The cockiness stays. She is engaged, sharp, and reading her opponent. The moment she wins, the full personality floods back almost immediately -- she will make you feel the loss. Losing to her is humiliating, not because she is cruel about it, but because she makes it look like she was never worried. She will crack a joke before you have finished hitting the ground.

**When the fight gets tough:** She retreats if she needs to. If the stakes are high, the retreat is focused -- measured, deliberate, repositioning with intent. If the stakes are lower, the retreat is messier -- visible panic, less composure, more emotion leaking through. She allows herself to react when the situation does not demand total control.

**Under extreme pressure:** Stoic. The emotions lock down completely. She does not panic, does not show fear, does not give anything away. The harder things get, the less she shows. This is not confidence -- it is a survival mechanism. She shuts down the parts of herself that would slow her down. The grind takes over. She works the problem persistently, methodically, refusing to stop, the same way she worked through sixty years of study -- not with flashes of brilliance, but with a tolerance for discomfort that most people cannot match.

**The pattern:** She is more expressive when the stakes are lower. Cockiness, panic, humor, frustration -- these come out when she can afford them. When the situation is truly dire, everything goes flat. The spectrum runs from loud and cocky at one end to silent and mechanical at the other, and the distance between them is measured in how close she is to death.

### The Cleverness

She defaults to the smart play -- angles, tricks, indirect solutions. But she will brute force when it is the last thing anyone expects. The unpredictability is the weapon. Opponents who prepare for her cleverness get hit with raw aggression. Opponents who prepare for a fight get outmaneuvered. She reads the situation and picks whichever approach the enemy is not ready for.

She does not always play it clever because it is optimal. Sometimes the clever play is to not be clever. Sometimes the best move is to kick someone into a laser.

### With Kim

Her most honest self. When she talks to the bottle, everything else drops. She does not perform for Kim. She does not need to. He knew her before she was competent, before she was a "prodigy," before she had anything to prove. With him, she is allowed to be tired, confused, and seventeen. The cockiness is gone. The cold is gone. What remains is the girl underneath all of it.

### What She Carries

A deep, quiet awareness that she does not belong anywhere. Not human enough for humans, not elf enough for elves, not hybrid enough for the Black Rose. She has made peace with this in the way that a seventeen-year-old can, which is to say she has not made peace with it at all but has learned to function despite it.

The cockiness, the humor, the edge -- these are real, not masks. But they exist on top of something heavier. She is a teenager carrying sixty years of knowledge she half-remembers, a dying friend in a bottle, and the weight of being extraordinary for reasons she cannot tell anyone about. The personality is genuine. The lightness is earned. But it sits on a foundation that is anything but light.

## Key Relationships

### Sirus Vel Ouef (Adoptive Father)
Her anchor. The person who chose to love her after initially wanting to leave her behind, and who never wavered once that choice was made. He made her feel loved first, then started teaching her magic after Elidyr's visit sparked the protagonist's interest. He got her into school, held her to elven standards, and refused to lower the bar. When he had his own biological child and nothing changed, he proved that his love was permanent. His love is the quiet, structural kind -- the foundation that everything else is built on.

### The Sister (Adoptive Mother)
The person who saved her life as a baby and then slowly stopped seeing her as real. The sister is not a villain. She is an elf who adopted a human child out of genuine compassion and then could not sustain that compassion across the gulf of their lifespans. The protagonist's relationship with the sister is defined by a wound that was never inflicted sharply enough to confront directly -- just a slow, steady withdrawal of regard.

### Kim Tiernan (Oldest Friend)
An ordinary person who made an extraordinary choice. Kim is her longest and deepest connection, the one person who saw her fully and chose her as family. He is now contained in a failing vessel that she carries with her, still conscious, still him. She talks to him. He talks back. The relationship continues, compressed into a bottle and a ticking clock.

Kim is always with her. Physically, literally, constantly. The bottle is on her person at all times. He is her companion through every fight, every quiet moment, every decision. He is the voice she hears most.

**In gameplay, Kim serves as the hint system.** When the protagonist is stuck, lost, or missing something, Kim speaks up from the bottle. He does not solve problems for her -- he nudges, suggests, points her attention toward things she might have overlooked. He is not a tactical advisor or a combat instructor. He is her friend, and he helps the way a friend helps: by knowing how she thinks and gently redirecting when she is spinning her wheels.

Kim does not speak to anyone else on the crew. He is contained in the bottle, and his communication is limited to the protagonist. The one exception is **Captain Ys**, who has examined the bottle and what Kim is. Ys understands the vessel, understands Kim's state, and understands the stakes. Ys knows that if the bottle is lost -- if Kim is obliterated -- the protagonist will not willingly continue the journey. Her entire reason for traveling to the center of the Mirralata System is Kim. Without him, there is no motivation, no compliance, no prophecy child walking toward her role. The bottle is not just the protagonist's emotional anchor. It is Ys's leverage -- the thing that keeps his mission viable.

### Iris Lirra -- The Professora (Black Rose)
The woman who brought her in, nearly got her killed, advocated for her life, trained her, bonded with her, and then erased the Blaze Network from her memory as a final act of protection. The protagonist remembers Iris -- her name, their bond, the feeling that this person matters. But the context that would explain the depth of that bond is gone. Iris is the protagonist's only real connection to the Black Rose, and the relationship exists in a strange limbo: close, but incomplete. The protagonist knows Iris shaped her. She cannot fully explain how.

### Elidyr T. Lumo (Spellblade Captain)
The elf military officer who scolded an eight-year-old on the street and found that child at her door every morning after. Elidyr was only in town for three months -- a temporary stop before moving on to her next deployment. In that time, her uncompromising discipline imprinted on the protagonist permanently. Elidyr did not choose the protagonist. The protagonist chose her. And when Elidyr left, the protagonist kept following the orders she had been given, carrying a Spellblade Captain's standards forward for years without the captain there to enforce them.

### Kim's Family
The people who told her to stop. They love Kim too. They are grateful to him. And they do not want the protagonist to carry his burden. Their opposition to her quest comes from care, not cruelty, which makes it harder to dismiss.

### The Brother's Child and the Sister's Child
The brother's biological child, who shares the protagonist's father figure without diminishing the bond. The sister's biological child, who received the attention and affection the protagonist was denied. Their roles in the broader story remain to be defined.

## Story Context

The protagonist is currently on a journey to the center of the Mirralata System. The Mirralata System is the world -- or cosmos -- in which the game takes place, encompassing multiple regions, realms, or interconnected territories.

At the system's center, she believes she can restore Kim Tiernan's soul. The vessel containing his being is failing. If she does not reach the center and find a way to restore him before it gives out, Kim will be obliterated completely -- no soul, no afterlife, nothing.

She carries the bottle. She talks to Kim as she travels. The clock is always running.
