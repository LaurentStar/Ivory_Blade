## Origin

Sketch's power is making Brod's drawings become real. Tablet is what happens when that power is applied to something that is not a drawing.

Brod had a regular tablet -- a standard digital device, nothing special. At some point, Sketch's magical antics brought it to life. Not deliberately, not as part of a plan. Sketch did something, the magic did something, and the tablet became a girl. Whether this was an accident, an experiment, or Sketch trying to give Brod a gift that got out of hand is unclear. The result is a sentient robot with a humanoid form who has decided -- or was designed -- to protect Brod Tsumi.

This makes Tablet fundamentally different from Brod's drawing-cast creations. His drawings are temporary -- brought to life by Sketch's energy, sustained for as long as Sketch can manage, then gone. Tablet is permanent. She did not fade. She did not dissolve. Whatever Sketch did to the tablet, it stuck.

---

## Appearance

Tablet has a humanoid female form -- a robot girl. She does not look organic. There is something mechanical, digital, and constructed about her that makes it clear she is not a living creature in the traditional sense. She looks like what she is: a device that became a person.

The crew sees her and reads "tech." A piece of equipment that follows the mercenary around. A drone with legs. They do not question her existence because robot companions are not unusual enough to warrant suspicion in the Mirralata System. What they do not realize is that she is thinking, feeling, and choosing to be where she is.

---

## Personality

**Blunt.** Tablet does not soften her words. If she speaks -- which is rare -- it is direct, short, and without cushioning. She does not explain herself. She does not elaborate. She says what needs to be said and returns to silence. There is no hostility in it. There is simply nothing extra.

**Silent.** Her default state is quiet. She does not fill silence the way Sketch does. She does not narrate, comment, or react audibly to most situations. She is present, she is aware, and she says nothing. In a crew that includes Sketch's loudness, Kat's bubbly voice, and the protagonist's cocky commentary, Tablet is the absence of noise. She watches. She waits.

**Low emotion.** Her expression does not move much. She does not smile, frown, laugh, or show surprise in ways that are easy to read. She processes the world with a flatness that could be mistaken for indifference. It is not indifference. It is restraint -- or limitation -- or something in between that even she may not fully understand.

**More emotion near Tsumi.** The exception. When Brod is nearby, Tablet softens. Not dramatically -- she does not become chatty or expressive. But the flatness eases. She turns toward him. She responds faster. There is a difference in how she exists when he is in the room versus when he is not, and it is visible to anyone paying attention. She was made from his device, brought to life by magic tied to him, and she gravitates toward him the way a compass points north. It is not romantic. It is not maternal. It is a bond that does not have a clean label.

---

## Combat

**Lethal.** Tablet is a weapon. Where Brod is "not a good fighter," Tablet is exactly the opposite. She is fast, precise, and built to end threats. She does not hesitate, does not posture, does not warn. When Tablet engages, the fight is already over in her head -- she is just waiting for reality to catch up.

**Silent fighter.** She fights the way she exists: without noise. No battle cries, no taunts, no commentary. She closes distance, eliminates the threat, and returns to Brod's side. The protagonist is cold when she kills. Tablet is colder -- not because she is angry or ruthless, but because combat is a function she executes with mechanical efficiency.

**Brod's survival tool.** Brod is a mediocre fighter with powerful magic. Without Tablet, he is vulnerable in close-quarters situations, during ambushes, or when he does not have time to draw. Tablet fills that gap. She is the reason a 19-year-old mercenary who is "not a good fighter" has survived long enough to be a mercenary at all. She handles what he cannot. He draws. She kills. The division of labor is clean.

---

## Relationship with Brod

Tablet is Brod's bodyguard, but the relationship is not employer-employee. She chose this -- or was made for this -- and the distinction may not matter. She follows him. She protects him. She stands near him when nothing is happening and between him and danger when something is.

Brod treats Tablet like a companion, not a tool. He does not give her orders in the way a soldier commands a drone. He talks to her the way he talks to Sketch -- like a person. The difference is that Sketch talks back at volume, and Tablet responds with a nod or nothing at all.

The crew sees Tablet and sees Brod's tech. A robot that follows the mercenary around. They do not see a sentient being with preferences, feelings, and a specific attachment to one person. This is the same blindness they had about Sketch -- assuming that what looks like an object is just an object.

---

## Relationship with Sketch

Sketch made Tablet. Whether Sketch considers Tablet a creation, a sibling, a child, or an accident is an open question. What is clear is that they are connected -- both products of the same magic, both tied to Brod, both sentient beings that the world is inclined to dismiss as objects.

They are opposites in every visible way. Sketch is loud, Tablet is silent. Sketch is warm, Tablet is flat. Sketch pushes Brod into social situations, Tablet pulls him out of dangerous ones. Sketch is the voice, Tablet is the fist.

Together, they are Brod's complete support system: one handles his social survival, the other handles his physical survival. Without both, he is an anxious teenager with some swords and no confidence.

---

## The Secret

The crew knows Tablet exists. They see her. She walks around, stands near Brod, and occasionally does things. They assume she is a piece of tech -- a robot companion, a bodyguard drone, something he bought or built or was given. They do not know she is sentient.

This is different from Sketch's secret. Sketch is hidden entirely -- the crew does not know the sketchbook is alive until the emergency reveal. Tablet is hidden in plain sight. She is visible, acknowledged, and completely misunderstood. The crew interacts with her the way people interact with equipment: they talk around her, not to her. They do not ask her opinion. They do not consider that she has one.

Captain Ys and Kat know. They are aware of all sentient beings on the ship. They know Tablet is alive the same way they know Sketch is alive -- and they keep quiet about both for the same reasons.

## Open Questions

1. **Combat specifics** -- What does Tablet fight with? Built-in weapons, external armaments, her body itself? Is she armed or is she the armament?
2. **Durability** -- How tough is she? Can she take hits that would kill a person? Is she repairable if damaged?
3. **The "magical antics"** -- What exactly did Sketch do? Was it intentional? Does Sketch know how to do it again? Could Sketch bring other objects to life?
4. **Communication** -- Does Tablet speak at all, or is she purely nonverbal? Does she communicate with Brod through other means (gestures, data, a shared connection)?
5. **Autonomy** -- Does Tablet follow Brod because she chooses to, or because the magic that created her compels it? Does she have independent goals?
6. **Post-reveal dynamics** -- When Sketch's sentience is revealed to the crew, does Tablet's sentience come out at the same time? Or does it remain hidden longer?
