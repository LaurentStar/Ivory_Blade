## Appearance

Robert is tall. Noticeably tall -- slender and lanky in a way that makes her look like she would fold in a strong wind. She is built like someone who has spent her life working with tools, not weapons. Long arms, deft hands, a frame that is all reach and no mass.

Her hair is **pink**. Her skin carries a pink tone that reinforces the visual -- she is a distinctly colored woman in a crew of visually striking people, but where Ys is unsettling and Kat is alien, Robert just looks like someone who should be somewhere safer than here.

Nothing about her screams danger. She does not carry herself with the protagonist's coiled readiness or Brod's resigned awareness. She carries herself like someone who was on her way somewhere else entirely and ended up in a war.

---

## How She Joined

Robert was not recruited. She was not sought out. She was **riding a realm-traveling train** -- public cross-realm transit -- when a group of **bank robbers** blew past and disrupted the tracks.

The robbers were being chased by a **galaxy-sized worm guardian**. The worm is so massive that it will take **1.2 years** for it to fully pass through the realm. Until it clears, there is no travel along that route. No trains. No ships. Nothing moves through while the worm is in the way.

Rerouting is not simple either. The worm's passage through the Mirralata System **heavily disturbs** surrounding travel routes -- realm gates destabilize, navigation becomes unpredictable, and the dangers of cross-realm travel multiply. The official recommendation is to **not travel until the worm passes**. Most people would wait the 1.2 years out.

Robert was on her way to the **Vitoko Realm** -- a capital realm of sorts -- just to see family. Nothing urgent. Nothing dramatic. A visit. The kind of trip normal people take for normal reasons. And now she cannot get there because the entire region of space between her and her family has a worm the size of a galaxy sitting in it.

She ended up with the crew by circumstance rather than choice. She is a temporary passenger. The crew **plans to drop her off** at a safe point along their rerouted path, but rerouting through a disturbed Mirralata System is dangerous, and every stop they make seems to almost kill them.

In the meantime, she is useful. Ys normally handles the ship's mechanical needs himself, but Robert is a skilled substitute. She frees him to focus on navigation and command. Her presence on the crew is practical -- she earns her keep by keeping the vessel operational until they find somewhere to leave her.

She has no stake in the prophecy. No connection to the Mirralata System's center. No personal mission driving her forward. She just wants to see her family in Vitoko. She is on this ship because a galaxy-sized worm blocked her commute and these people had a ship willing to go a different way. The plan is to drop her off. The plan keeps not happening because the crew keeps almost dying.

## Personality

**Scared.** Robert is afraid of almost everything the crew encounters. She is not pretending. She is not hiding courage underneath. She is genuinely, visibly, consistently terrified by the violence, the monsters, the military forces, and the sheer scale of danger that surrounds the crew at all times. Cave animals send her sealing herself inside the ship. The Tiger Unit captain grabbing her by the throat is the worst moment of her life. She does not acclimate to danger. Each new threat is as frightening as the first.

**Not paralyzed by fear.** This is the part people miss. Robert is scared. She is not useless. When the volcanic activity hit in Chapter 2, she flew the ship to safety. When the protagonist needed her, she scraped together a magic signal she barely knew how to cast just to call for help. When Brod's drawing-cast symbols were ready on the ship's laser in Chapter 4, she pulled the trigger and fired the shot that took down the tiger captain. She does what needs to be done while being terrified the entire time. The fear never leaves. She acts through it, not past it.

**Intelligent.** Robert understands how things are built. Machines, systems, mechanisms -- she sees structure where other people see parts. This is not formal training -- it is survival. **Every vehicle Robert has ever ridden breaks down.** Every one. Trains, ships, transports -- something always fails. It is either a curse or the worst luck in the Mirralata System, but the flip side is that she is always there when it happens, so she learned to fix it. A lifetime of breakdowns turned her into an engineer. The fact that Ys's ship keeps taking damage is not surprising to her. It is Tuesday.

In the puzzle room of Chapter 3, she contributed meaningfully alongside the protagonist and Brod. The protagonist brought magic and tech knowledge. Brod brought creativity. Robert brought an **engineering mind** -- an understanding of how things connect, how systems interlock, how mechanisms work at a structural level. She may not know magic, but she knows construction. In a room full of ancient machinery, that mattered.

**Nearly magicless.** Robert has almost no magical ability. In a crew where magic is the primary tool -- the protagonist channels it through combat, Kat uses it for support and scanning, Brod casts through drawings -- Robert is the one who cannot participate in the magical layer of the world. The best she can manage is a rough, unstable distress signal. It is functional the way a child's first drawing is functional: you can tell what it is trying to be, but it is not good.

**Quiet.** She does not command attention. She does not crack jokes like IndigoLLiy or provoke people like Kat. She exists at a lower social volume than everyone else on the crew. This is not shyness -- it is the natural state of someone whose job is behind the scenes and who prefers it that way. She speaks when she has something to say. She does not perform.

**Normal.** Brod considers Robert one of the only **normal people on the crew**, alongside the mouse woman. She does not bully him. She does not provoke him. She is not mysterious, not dangerous, not carrying a secret. She is a mechanic on a ship. In a crew of ghosts, aliens, prophecy children, and sentient sketchbooks, Robert is the person who just works on engines. Brod gravitates toward her for the same reason anyone gravitates toward calm in chaos.

## Role on the Crew

### The Ship

Robert's primary value is keeping the crew's ship operational. She handles repairs, maintenance, and the mechanical upkeep that would otherwise fall to Ys. The ship has taken repeated damage throughout the game -- the initial attack that separated the crew, rough landings, gate entries, combat damage -- and Robert is the reason it keeps flying.

She is not just a wrench-turner. She understands the ship's systems at a deep level. When something breaks, she diagnoses the failure, identifies the fix, and executes it. This is skilled work, not grunt work.

### Piloting

Robert can fly the ship. She is not Ys's equal (his piloting aim is lethally precise) or Kat's equal (competent and experienced), but she can keep the ship in the air and navigate it to safety. She is the crew's **fourth pilot** -- behind Ys, Kat, and Brod -- but in emergencies, four is better than three.

In Chapter 2, she flew the ship to the top of a giant tower during volcanic activity. She did this alone, scared, with no combat support. The ship survived. She survived.

### The Puzzle Room

Chapter 3's puzzle room is Robert's defining moment outside of the ship. With Kat, Ys, and the mouse woman injured, the three people left standing are the protagonist, Brod, and Robert. They solve the room together.

This is where the crew sees Robert as more than the person who fixes things. Her engineering mind -- the ability to read mechanisms, trace systems, and understand how ancient architecture connects -- makes her a real contributor to a challenge that raw combat skill cannot solve. She is not a fighter. She is a thinker. And in that room, thinking was what mattered.

### The Shot

In Chapter 4, the protagonist kicks the tiger captain into the air. Brod's drawing-cast symbols are on the ship's laser. Robert fires.

The magically augmented blast hits the tiger captain. He is burned, broken, and beaten. He does not die -- he goes down and stays down. Robert took a shot at a living person for the first time in her life, and it brought down the most dangerous individual fighter in the game: a crew working together, with the scared mechanic pulling the trigger.

She did not volunteer for this. She was in position, the weapon was ready, and the moment came. She fired because it needed to happen. That is who Robert is: the person who does what needs to be done, not because she is brave, but because she is there and nobody else can do it.

## Key Relationships

### Captain Ys

Ys protects Robert. He goes out of his way to keep her out of combat situations -- adjusting plans, positioning her behind cover, making decisions that prioritize her safety alongside the mission. He does not explain this protectiveness. She is on his ship. That is enough.

Ys also uses his lighter side with Robert. The dry humor, the offhand remarks that break tension -- these go to the **outer circle** of the crew. Robert and the mouse woman get the jokes. Brod and Kat do not. This is not closeness. It is social management. But for Robert, who spends most of her time scared, the occasional dry remark from the ghost captain is the closest thing to normalcy she gets.

### IndigoLLiy (The Protagonist)

Neutral from both sides, but for different reasons. IndigoLLiy recognizes that Robert is not a fighter and does not expect her to be one. No teasing, no bullying, no special protectiveness -- just practical acknowledgment.

Robert sees IndigoLLiy as **a teen** -- or even a child. She is 17, after all. Robert can talk to her on the same wavelength, which is comforting -- IndigoLLiy is easy to communicate with in a way that some of the more intense crew members are not. But Robert does not get emotionally close. There is a natural distance that comes from seeing someone as younger, not as a peer. She is not dismissive -- she just does not attach.

When IndigoLLiy reaches the ship in Chapter 2 and finds Robert terrified and sealed inside, she clears the threat, confirms Robert is safe, and moves forward. The interaction is functional, not emotional. Robert is crew. She is alive. Next problem.

### Brod Tsumi

Brod considers Robert one of the only normal people on the crew. He gravitates toward her and the mouse woman as anchors of sanity. They do not bully him. They do not provoke him. They are just people.

In the puzzle room, Brod and Robert work together under the protagonist's lead. This is the first time their dynamic gets real screen time. Brod who considers her normal. Robert who has been terrified, protected, and underestimated for the entire game. They are peers in a way that neither of them is with anyone else on the crew.

---

## What Makes Her Important

Robert is the crew member who proves that capability is not limited to combat. In a story about a protagonist defined by fighting, a ghost captain defined by mystery, and a mercenary defined by magic -- Robert is defined by **competence in the space nobody else occupies**. Without her, the ship does not fly. Without her, the puzzle room does not get solved. Without her, the tiger captain does not go down.

She is scared the entire time. She does it anyway.

The story needs Robert because she is the human-scale reference point. When IndigoLLiy punches soldiers across a room and Ys shape-shifts through a platoon, Robert grounds the narrative by being someone who cannot do any of that and contributes anyway. She is the proof that this crew is not just a collection of fighters. It is a system, and every part of the system matters.
