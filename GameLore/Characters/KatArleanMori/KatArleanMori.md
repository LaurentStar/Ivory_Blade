## Appearance

Kat is an alien girl with rabbit anthropomorphic features. She is not a rabbit, not a hybrid, and not connected to the rabbit god referenced in the prophecy documents. She is an alien species that happens to look rabbit-like -- the visual similarity is coincidence, not design.

She stands at **4'9"** on **hind legs** -- taller than the protagonist (4'3") but shorter than Ys. Her skin is **blue** and her fur is **white.** The blue skin is distinctly alien -- she is never mistaken for a human at any range. The white fur covers her body over the blue skin.

**She is not a normal rabbit anthropomorph.** The rabbit-like furry creatures native to the realms stand on their feet like humans, have proper proportions, are capable of expressive facial movements, and speak at a higher pitch. Kat does not match any of this. Her proportions are **off** -- subtly wrong in ways that are hard to pinpoint but impossible to ignore. She stands on hind legs rather than human-like feet. Her face is stoic because her facial structure does not support the range of expressions that native rabbit anthropomorphs can produce. Her voice is **lower pitched** than what anyone would expect from a rabbit-like being.

These differences are the signal. Anyone from the Mirralata System who has seen actual rabbit anthropomorphs can tell that Kat is not one of them. She looks similar at a glance, but the proportions, the posture, the voice, the expressions -- all of it is wrong in the specific way that says "alien" rather than "different breed." She is from somewhere else entirely, and her body does not let anyone forget it for long.

She has fur, which creates a specific problem: **she does horribly in hot climates.** Heat makes her miserable. If forced to stay in a hot environment too long, she will shave her fur -- a drastic, uncomfortable measure that she resorts to out of survival rather than vanity. The shaved look is not something she enjoys, and the crew is advised not to comment on it.

## Personality

**Bubbly voice, stoic face.** Kat is warm, present, and energetic -- but you hear it more than you see it. Her face is naturally stoic. The bubbly personality comes through in her voice, her tone, her words, her body language -- but her expression rarely matches. She can be cracking a joke or flirting with Brod and her face looks the same as when she is scanning a battlefield. People who rely on reading faces struggle with Kat. People who listen to her voice understand her immediately. The crew has learned to use their ears more than their eyes to gauge her mood.

When she tries to be more human-like with her facial expressions, it is **terrifying.** Her happy face is scary looking. The features do not arrange themselves the way a human face would. The result is something that reads as a threat display rather than a smile. It scares the entire crew -- including Ys, who is a ghost-like entity that has seen enough to be calm about almost everything. When Kat tries to look happy, Ys looks away.

If she opens her mouth completely wide, the rabbit resemblance disappears entirely. What is underneath does not look like a cute animal anymore. It looks like a **vicious beast that could bite your head off** -- rows of something that should not be in a rabbit's mouth, a jaw that opens wider than anatomy should allow, the sudden reminder that this is an alien species that merely resembles a rabbit from the outside. The crew has seen this exactly enough times to know they never want to see it again.

**Sexually provocative.** She relentlessly provokes Brod -- flirtatious, forward, deliberately making him uncomfortable in ways she finds entertaining. She knows he tries not to react. She does it anyway. This is not cruelty. It is her version of engagement, and it lands differently on everyone. Brod cannot handle it. The protagonist probably finds it funny. Ys does not comment.

**Warm with the protagonist.** This is the detail that matters most. Kat knows the protagonist is meant to be a sacrifice at the end of the journey. She carries the same terrible knowledge as Ys. She handles it completely differently.

Where Ys builds a wall -- monotone, distant, refusing to invest emotionally in someone he is delivering to their death -- Kat opens up. She is bubbly and fun with the protagonist. She treats her like a person, not a delivery. She does not go cold. She does not withdraw.

Whether this is because Kat processes the knowledge differently than Ys, because she believes something can change, or because she has decided that the girl deserves kindness on the walk to the altar is her own quiet burden. She does not explain it to Ys, and he does not ask her to stop.

**Aware.** Kat and Ys are aware of all sentient beings on the ship. They knew about Sketch (Brod's sentient sketchbook) before anyone else did and chose not to mention it until it became operationally necessary. This suggests a level of perception or ability that goes beyond normal crew awareness -- whether this is a trait of Kat's species, something she shares with Ys specifically, or a capability they developed together is unclear.

**Second-in-command and strategist.** Kat is not just Ys's partner -- she is his second-in-command. She is the strategic mind on the crew. She analyzes situations, identifies options, and lays out the tactical picture. But she does not make the final call -- that is Ys. He listens to her assessments, weighs them against whatever he knows that nobody else does, and decides. The protagonist fills the third role in this chain: she improvises. Kat plans, Ys decides, and when the plan falls apart -- which it always does -- the protagonist adapts on the fly better than anyone else on the crew. When Ys is unavailable, Kat runs the crew. The bubbly, provocative, scandalously dressed alien girl is also the person who takes over when the captain is gone. She has a **special tone** -- a shift in voice and demeanor that drops the playfulness entirely. When that tone comes out, the crew listens. Brod in particular recognizes it immediately and becomes more obedient. He knows the difference between Kat messing around and Kat giving an order. The tone is how everyone on the crew knows the situation has changed.

**An exceptional liar.** Kat can lie without a single tell. Her stoic face works in her favor here -- there is no micro-expression to betray her, no flicker of guilt, no shift in body language. Her voice stays warm, steady, convincing. She can calmly promise someone help, reassure them that everything will be fine, and kill them in the same motion. The transition from comforting to lethal has no gap. The person being lied to dies believing they were safe.

**Nonchalant about killing.** Kat is more casual about taking lives than anyone else on the crew. The protagonist goes cold and silent when she kills. Ys is clinical. Brod is anxious about everything, killing included. Kat does not change. She can cut someone down with the scythe and be back to her bubbly self in the same breath. There is no mode switch, no visible weight, no ceremony. Killing is something that happens and then she moves on. Whether this comes from experience, her species, or something else about her nature is unclear -- but it is noticeable. The crew has seen it. Nobody has asked her about it.

**Not performing innocence.** Kat's bubbly exterior is real, not a mask. But she is not naive. She carries knowledge of the protagonist's sacrifice. She is partnered with a ghost-like entity whose true nature remains unknown. She is aware of sentient beings others cannot perceive. She is the second-in-command of a crew being hunted across a warzone. The warmth is genuine. The person generating it is more complicated than the warmth suggests.

## Relationship with Captain Ys

Kat is Ys's partner. Something like a wife.

Nobody on the crew knows this. They do not act affectionate in front of others. There is no visible warmth between them that would distinguish their relationship from any other captain-crew dynamic. She follows his orders like everyone else. He treats her with the same stern tone he uses on Brod. To the rest of the crew, she is another hire -- the alien girl with rabbit features who happens to be one of two people Ys seems to trust slightly more than the others.

The concealment is not dramatic. They are not hiding a scandal. They simply do not perform their relationship for an audience. Whatever exists between them exists in private, in glances that mean something only to them, in decisions Ys makes that happen to keep her safe without being traceable to favoritism.

Ys does not joke with Kat. She is too close for that. The humor he occasionally shows is reserved for the outer circle -- the mechanic, the mouse woman. With Kat, as with Brod, everything is serious. But the shape of the seriousness is different: with Brod it is mentorship. With Kat it is intimacy stripped of performance.

## Relationship with the Protagonist

This is the most loaded relationship on the ship.

Kat knows the protagonist is going to die. She is warm to her anyway. She is bubbly, fun, present -- the closest thing the protagonist has to a friend on the crew. Every joke, every light moment, every interaction where Kat treats the protagonist like a person who matters carries the invisible weight of what Kat knows.

The protagonist does not know about the sacrifice. She does not know why Kat is so warm. She probably assumes it is just Kat's personality -- which it is, but it is also a choice. A deliberate decision to give this girl something good on the way to something terrible.

The contrast between Kat's warmth and Ys's flatness toward the protagonist is one of the most telling dynamics on the ship. Two people who share the same terrible knowledge. One who shuts down to survive it. One who opens up despite it.

## Relationship with Brod

Kat sexually provokes Brod constantly. It is relentless, deliberate, and she enjoys watching him fail to not react. This is her dynamic with him -- playful harassment that he endures because telling her to stop would require the kind of social assertiveness he does not have.

Brod considers Kat one of the crew members who is not normal. The mouse woman and the mechanic are his safe harbors. Kat, along with the protagonist, is part of the chaos he is trying to survive socially.

## Relationship with Vennessa

Warm, and close to maternal, though Kat would never describe it that way and Vennessa would be embarrassed if anyone said it out loud.

The relationship runs through medicine. Vennessa is a civilian who does not fight, cannot take a hit, and gets hurt anyway, which means Kat has patched her up more times than anyone else on the crew. Kat is one of the only people who regularly touches her, and one of the only people who has time alone with her, because injuries create privacy that nothing else on this ship does.

**She uses Vennessa's name.** This is the single most important thing about the relationship and it is invisible unless you are looking for it. Indigo, Ys, Brod, and Kim all call her "the mouse" or "the mouse woman." Kat never has, in any scene, in any chapter. It is not a statement and Kat has never remarked on it -- it is just what she does, and it means Vennessa hears her own name from exactly one person aboard. **Do not write Kat saying "the mouse."** See `GameLore/Characters/Vennessa/Vennessa.md` § Naming.

She also **notices Vennessa's work**, which nobody else does. Vennessa's magic is force multiplication -- decoys, copies, barriers -- and it is invisible precisely when it succeeds. Kat is the crew's other support caster and understands the problem from the inside. In Ch3 6.2 she points out that Vennessa spent hours in a cell painting an entire army and not one person has mentioned it. She is right, and she is the only one who would notice.

**And she uses all of that goodwill to torment Brod.** Kat suggests, in a completely warm voice with a completely stoic face, that Vennessa and Brod are a thing. She does it because it gets a reaction out of him that direct flirtation does not, and because Vennessa reacting is funny to her. It is the same instinct as everything else she does to Brod, aimed through a third party.

This has a second effect Kat probably does intend: it forces Vennessa to have a reaction at all. Vennessa's default is one soft question and then letting it go. Kat is the only crew member who reliably breaks that.

## Appearance -- Clothing

Kat switches outfits constantly. Her default wardrobe leans **scandalous** -- revealing, attention-grabbing, the kind of clothing that makes Brod unable to look in her direction without short-circuiting. She dresses like someone who knows exactly what she looks like and enjoys the effect.

When the situation gets serious, the wardrobe changes instantly. She magically dresses into **full tactical gear** -- practical, armored, combat-ready. The scythe comes out. The DMR comes out. The scandalous alien girl is gone and what replaces her is a fighter who looks like she has done this before.

The switch is jarring. The crew goes from seeing Kat in something that would get her thrown out of a military briefing to seeing her in gear that belongs in one. The contrast is deliberate. She does not take most situations seriously because she does not need to. When she does need to, the transformation is immediate and complete.

## Combat

Kat does not get into fights often. She does not need to. The protagonist and Ys handle most of the crew's combat, and Kat is content to let them. But when she does fight, she is the **third-best fighter on the crew** -- behind the protagonist and Ys, ahead of everyone else by a clear margin.

### Weapons

**Scythe (primary).** A powerful scythe that she wields with skill. Magic-enhanced, lethal, and suited to her fighting style. With weapons in hand, she is the third most powerful member of the crew.

**Magic-powered DMR (secondary).** A high-power designated marksman rifle powered by magic. This is not a standard firearm -- it is enhanced, precise, and hits significantly harder than the guns the wolf squad carries. When she switches to tactical gear, the DMR comes out alongside the scythe.

### Hand-to-Hand

In unarmed combat, Kat is the **second best on the crew** -- and the gap between her and third place is enormous. Without weapons, she is behind only the protagonist. Her hand-to-hand ability is not a supplement to her weapons; it is a standalone capability that most fighters on the ship cannot approach.

### Magic -- Support Role

Kat's primary combat contribution is **magic support through status effects.** She leans support rather than direct damage, buffing the crew and debuffing enemies through magical statuses. This makes her more valuable as a force multiplier than as a solo combatant -- she makes the protagonist hit harder, makes Ys move faster, makes the crew survive longer.

Her magic is extremely well-used. She is a skilled caster, and her support capabilities are a significant part of why the crew survives encounters that should overwhelm them by numbers.

### Universal Energy Limitation

Kat **cannot go as low on the Universal Energy scale as Brod or the protagonist.** Her body and abilities depend on a higher baseline of UE than theirs do. In realms with lower Universal Energy, she begins to lose effectiveness -- her magic weakens, her physical capabilities degrade, and eventually she may not be able to function at all.

In the Swahili Realm (mid-tier low UE), she is still functional but operating closer to her floor than her ceiling. In realms lower than this, she would be a liability. This is a hard limit on where Kat can go in the series -- as the crew travels deeper toward the center of the Mirralata System and potentially passes through lower-UE realms, Kat's viability as a crew member is not guaranteed.

## Role on the Crew

Kat is Ys's hidden partner, the crew's social wildcard, its primary magic support in combat, its medic, and its intelligence source. She shares awareness of sentient beings on the ship with Ys. She carries knowledge of the prophecy and the protagonist's sacrifice.

### Medical

Kat provides medical attention to the crew alongside Ys. She is better at it than he is. When someone is hurt -- and on this crew, someone is always hurt -- Kat is the one patching them up. Ys can handle field medicine, but Kat is the real medic. After the protagonist's encounters with the wolf squad and especially after her near-death fight with the tiger captain, Kat is the reason she is still functional.

### Intelligence -- Environmental Scanning

Kat can **scan the environment** and pull up what functions as a wiki -- detailed information about people, places, creatures, and threats. She provides the crew with critical details about what they are facing before or during encounters.

This makes her the crew's primary source of tactical intelligence. When the crew arrives somewhere new, Kat is the one telling them what lives here, what the local threats are, who controls the territory, and what to watch for. When they encounter a new enemy, she is the one identifying what it is and what it can do.

In a crew where the protagonist fights, Ys commands, and Brod draws, Kat is the one who makes sure everyone knows what they are dealing with. Her scans and intel briefings are often the difference between walking into a situation prepared and walking into it blind.

### Piloting

Kat can fly the ship. She is not Ys's equal behind the controls -- his piloting aim is unnervingly sharp -- but she is a competent pilot and can take over when he is unavailable. She is not a mechanic. If something breaks, she cannot fix it. That is the mechanic's job, or the protagonist's in a pinch. Kat can keep the ship in the air but not keep it running.
