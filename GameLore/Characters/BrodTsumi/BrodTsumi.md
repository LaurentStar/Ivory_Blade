## Appearance

Brod looks like he would rather be somewhere else. His default expression is unimpressed -- one hand behind his head, posture loose, the look of someone who has been dragged into situations he did not ask for enough times that he has stopped being surprised by it.

He is a young Black teenage male with messy black hair that does what it wants. A small goatee gives him the faint impression of being older than he is, which is undercut immediately by everything else about him -- the slouch, the casual clothes, the look on his face that says he was napping twenty minutes ago and would like to get back to it.

His eyes are notably light -- pale, almost white. In a crew that includes a ghost captain with no pupils, Brod's eyes are less unsettling but still distinctive.

He does not dress like a mercenary. Gray jacket, red shirt, nothing armored or tactical. He looks like a teenager on his way to buy something from a shop, not someone who fights for money. This is either carelessness or a deliberate choice to avoid looking like what he is.

## Personality

**Anxious.** Brod does not believe in himself in any meaningful way. He is self-conscious enough that making an effort to connect with people feels like a risk he cannot justify. Left to his own devices, he does not talk to anyone. He draws. The flat expression and dry affect are not coolness -- they are the outermost layer of someone who is deeply unsure of his own worth and has decided that saying nothing is safer than saying the wrong thing. He is anxious in the way that reads as quiet rather than panicked -- an internal hum of doubt that never fully shuts off.

**An artist.** Before all of this -- before Ys, before mercenary work, before being hunted across realms -- Brod was an art student. He draws. Constantly. His sketchbook is always on him. When he is not fighting, not following orders, not being dragged into the next crisis, he is drawing. It is the one thing he does without hesitation or self-doubt. The drawing is also directly connected to his magic -- his drawing-casting technique is literal. He draws to cast.

**Dry.** He does not perform enthusiasm. If something is impressive, he might acknowledge it with a nod or a short sentence. He does not cheer. He does not hype. He responds to the absurd with the energy of someone watching it rain.

**Not particularly loyal.** Brod is here for the money. That is the arrangement. Ys pays, Brod fights. He does not follow the ghost out of devotion, shared purpose, or a deep belief in the mission. He follows because he really needs the paycheck and Ys is the one writing it. If someone offered him more money to walk away, the only thing keeping him on the ship would be whether the new offer also came with a ghost who could keep him alive long enough to spend it.

**Competent without ego.** Brod is a hired gun who is good at his job. He does not brag about it. He does not need to prove himself. He fights, he adapts, he handles his part. The protagonist is flashier, Ys is more mysterious, the mouse woman is a better mage -- Brod is the reliable one. The one who does his job without making it a performance.

**Constantly harassed.** The protagonist kinda bullies him. She messes with people she likes, and Brod is her favorite target -- cocky jokes at his expense, teasing him for his lack of confidence, pushing buttons she knows are there. The rabbit girl is worse in a different way: she is constantly sexually provoking him, flirtatious in a way that is deliberate and relentless. Brod tries not to react to either of them. He fails regularly. The flat affect cracks, the anxiety spikes, and then they do it again.

**Two normal people.** The only crew members Brod considers normal are the mouse woman and the mechanic. They do not bully him. They do not provoke him. They are just... people. The mouse woman is looking for her cat. The pink-haired mechanic keeps the ship running. Neither of them seems interested in making his life harder for entertainment. Brod gravitates toward them for the same reason anyone gravitates toward calm in a storm: they are the only parts of this crew that make sense to him.

**A magnet for chaos.** Brod does not seek out dangerous or magical situations. They find him. This is the pattern of his life -- an anxious, quiet artist who is constantly followed by chaotic situations he did not ask for. Ys's crew is the latest and most extreme iteration of this, but it is not the first. Brod exists in a state of resigned acceptance that the universe will not leave him alone, no matter how little he does to invite its attention.

**Young.** Under the mercenary exterior, he is still a teenager. The goatee and the flat affect make it easy to forget, but he is a kid. A kid who draws in a sketchbook, has no confidence, kills for money, and follows a ghost across realms because the alternative is worse.

## Combat

**Dual swords.** Brod's weapons are two swords -- a dual-wield setup. They are not just blades. The swords double as drawing tools: he uses them to trace ink-like symbols onto surfaces or directly in midair. The line between his weapons and his art supplies is nonexistent. He fights with the same instruments he draws with.

**Drawing-casting magic.** Brod's real usefulness comes from what he draws. He uses his dual swords to sketch on surfaces or in midair, and Sketch brings the drawings to life. What he creates depends on the situation:

- **In combat (no prep time):** Quick, sloppy spells. Fast symbols slashed in the air mid-fight -- functional but rough. These are his bread and butter when things are moving too fast to think. They work, but they are not elegant.
- **With prep time:** He draws helpers. Creatures, constructs, things that can fight alongside the crew or perform a task. Given a few minutes to sit down and actually draw, he can produce allies that significantly change a fight's odds. The quality of the drawing affects the quality of the result -- a rushed sketch produces a weak helper, a detailed drawing produces something formidable.
- **In non-combat situations:** He can draw items or creatures to solve problems. A tool the crew needs. A copy of someone for a distraction. A powerful dragon if the situation is desperate enough and he has the time and Sketch has the energy.

The ceiling on what he can create is high -- dragons, copies of people, complex constructs. The floor is low -- sloppy glyphs that fizzle or barely hold together. The variable is always time and Sketch's energy reserves. His casting is not as refined as the mouse woman's. She uses the same general technique and does it significantly better. Brod's drawings get the job done. Hers are art.

**Speed blitz.** Brod has one special move he spams relentlessly: a speed blitz where he rushes an enemy and cuts them down with both swords in rapid succession. Against weak enemies, it is a finishing move -- fast, decisive, over before they register he moved. Against strong enemies, it does nothing meaningful. He knows this. He does it anyway sometimes, out of habit or optimism, and it never works on anyone who matters. He prefers his drawing powers for real threats. The blitz is for cleanup.

**Pilot and minor mechanic.** Brod can fly the ship and make minor repairs. He is not Ys's equal at piloting and not the mechanic's equal at fixing things, but he can handle both in a pinch. Between Ys, Kat, and Brod, the crew has three people who can keep the ship moving if any one of them goes down.

**Not a good fighter.** Brod is a mercenary who is not particularly good at fighting. He can defend himself -- he is not helpless, and he will not go down without resistance -- but he is not someone you want leading a charge or holding a line. His value is in his drawing-casting, not his swordplay. The dual swords are tools for drawing first and weapons second. In a straight fight without magic, he loses to most serious opponents. Given the choice between rushing in with blades and standing back to sketch a spell, he picks the sketch every time because the sketch is where his actual capability lives.

## Sketch -- The Sketchbook

Brod's closest companion is not a person on the crew. It is his sketchbook -- a blue spiral-bound book called **Sketch** that is a sentient being with two forms: a book (default) and a humanoid girl with white hair, blue eyes, and a forward, playful personality that is the opposite of everything Brod is.

The crew does not know Sketch is real. They see Brod talking to a sketchbook and assume it is an imaginary friend, a quirk of the anxious artist. Captain Ys and the rabbit girl know -- they are aware of all sentient beings on the ship -- but they do not announce it until an emergency forces Ys to grab the sketchbook and yell at it to get to work or get thrown out. Sketch manifests. The secret is over.

Brod did not know Ys and the rabbit girl were aware of Sketch. The reveal is a shock on multiple levels: his private companion is now visible to the people who bully and provoke him, and the captain he works for has been keeping quiet about knowing his deepest secret the entire time.

## Tablet -- The Bodyguard

Brod's other companion is **Tablet** -- a sentient robot girl conjured by Sketch's magic using Brod's regular tablet device. Sketch's power normally brings drawings to life temporarily. Tablet is what happened when that magic was applied to a physical object instead -- and unlike the drawings, she did not fade. She is permanent.

Tablet is Brod's silent bodyguard and a lethal combatant. She does not talk much, shows minimal emotion, and fights with mechanical precision. Where Sketch handles Brod's social survival, Tablet handles his physical survival. She is the reason a mercenary who is "not a good fighter" has stayed alive long enough to be one.

The crew knows Tablet exists -- they see her, they acknowledge her -- but they assume she is a piece of tech. A robot companion, a bodyguard drone, equipment. They do not know she is sentient. She is hidden in plain sight. Captain Ys and Kat know the truth, same as with Sketch, and keep quiet about it.

Tablet shows more emotion when near Brod. The flatness eases, she responds faster, she turns toward him. She was made from his device, and she gravitates toward him in a way that has no clean label.

## Relationship with Captain Ys

Brod and Ys have known each other for a while. Their relationship predates this crew, this ship, and this mission.

From Brod's perspective, this journey is Ys getting him into yet another bout of craziness. He has lost count of how many times the ghost has pulled him into something dangerous, strange, or both. He follows because every previous time made him better -- he learned, he grew, he came out more capable. Ys knows this. It is exactly to Ys's liking.

Ys mentors Brod, but not gently. Stern orders, no praise, no explanations beyond what is necessary. The mentorship is in the doing. Ys puts Brod in situations that force growth and trusts him to figure it out. When Brod figures it out, Ys gives him the next problem.

Ys does not joke with Brod. The humor Ys shows to the mechanic and the mouse woman never extends to the mercenary. With Brod, everything is direct and serious. This is how Brod knows Ys respects him.

Brod is one of Ys's two closest connections on the crew (the other being the rabbit girl). What makes this relationship different from the rabbit girl's is its shape: Ys and Brod are mentor and mentee, not partners. The bond is built on shared experience and mutual understanding, not intimacy.

## Role on the Crew

Brod is the crew's most straightforward combatant after the protagonist. He is not the leader (Ys), not the specialist (the mechanic, the mouse woman), and not the mission's reason for existing (the protagonist). He is the hired gun who has been with Ys long enough to be more than hired.

During the Swahili Realm, Brod is among those captured by the wolf squad. The protagonist rescues the entire crew using Jack (the wolf leader) as bait. Being rescued by the girl Ys brought aboard -- the prophecy child, the passenger -- likely shifts something in how Brod sees her. She went from cargo to someone who saved his life.
