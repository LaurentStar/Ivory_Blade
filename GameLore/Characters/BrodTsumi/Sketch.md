# Sketch -- The Sketchbook

> *"Under the influence huh? Now whatcha drawin'?..."*

---

## Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Sketch |
| **Nature** | Sentient sketchbook. A living being with two forms. |
| **Owner** | Brod Tsumi |
| **Forms** | Book form (default) and humanoid form |
| **Perceived As** | Brod's imaginary friend -- until the reveal |
| **Awareness** | Captain Ys and the rabbit girl know Sketch is real. Nobody else on the crew does initially. |

---

## Forms

### Book Form (Default)

A blue spiral-bound sketchbook. This is how Sketch appears most of the time -- sitting in Brod's hands, tucked under his arm, or open on whatever surface he is drawing on. It looks like a normal sketchbook to anyone who does not know better. Blue cover, spiral binding, pages full of Brod's drawings.

Brod talks to the sketchbook. The crew assumes this is a quirk -- the anxious artist muttering to his drawings, an imaginary friend, a coping mechanism. Nobody takes it seriously because nobody has a reason to. It is just Brod being Brod.

### Humanoid Form

Sketch can manifest as a humanoid figure -- a girl with white/pale hair, blue eyes, and a mischievous grin. She wears a blue outfit and has a playful, forward energy that is the opposite of Brod's anxious withdrawal. In this form she has what appears to be a large white tail or spine-like appendage behind her, and a blue lightning-bolt marking near her hair.

She looks like something Brod drew and brought to life -- which is essentially what she is. There is a sketch-like quality to her, as if the lines that define her were put down by the same hand that fills the sketchbook's pages.

In humanoid form, Sketch is loud where Brod is quiet, confident where he is anxious, and socially aggressive where he is withdrawn. She leans in, asks questions, gets in people's space. She is the extrovert attached to an introvert, and she does not let him hide.

---

## Personality

**Loud.** Sketch's default volume is too much. She shouts, catches herself, whispers for half a second, then forgets and shouts again. She fills every silence Brod leaves behind and then some. She has the energy of someone who has been stuck in a book for too long and is making up for lost time every second she is manifested.

**Self-appointed wingman.** Sketch saw Brod staring at a girl once and not saying hi. That was enough. She has decided her purpose -- beyond being a magical sketchbook -- is to fix Brod's social life. She pushes him into conversations, nudges him toward people, and treats his isolation as a problem she is personally responsible for solving. She finds his discomfort entertaining but also genuinely believes she is helping.

**Nosy.** She is in everyone's business. She wants to know what you are doing, what you are thinking, who you are looking at, and why. She does not wait for answers before asking the next question. Privacy is a concept she is aware of and has chosen to ignore.

**Chatty to a fault.** Sketch talks too much and knows it. She will monologue, catch herself revealing something she should not, backtrack unconvincingly, and then keep going. Her self-awareness exists but has no effect on her behavior.

**Genuinely warm.** Under the chaos, Sketch cares. She found Brod in a "misery hole" -- anxious, isolated, drawing alone -- and decided to pull him out of it. She calls him "master" with affection and mockery in equal measure. She thinks he is cool. She thinks he is a wreck. Both are true and she holds both without contradiction.

**Jealous of his art.** Sketch cannot draw as well as Brod. This bothers her. She is a living sketchbook who cannot sketch. She can make his drawings become real -- that is her power -- but the art itself is his. She wishes she could do what he does, and this small insecurity is one of the few things that quiets her down.

### Her Power

Sketch can make Brod's drawings become real. This is the core of their partnership and the foundation of his drawing-casting magic. He draws, she animates. The catch: it takes energy from her. She cannot do it indefinitely. The more she brings to life, the more it costs her. This creates a natural limit on Brod's casting -- his magic is not just about his skill with a pen, it is about how much Sketch can sustain.

**Loyal to Brod.** For all her noise, Sketch is Brod's companion. She is his closest bond -- closer than Ys, closer than anyone on the crew. She has been with him through the sketchbook, through the drawing, through the chaos that follows him everywhere. She is the one constant in a life defined by being dragged into situations he did not ask for.

---

## The Secret

The rest of the crew -- the protagonist, the mechanic, the mouse woman -- does not know Sketch is real. They see Brod talking to a sketchbook and chalk it up to his personality. An anxious kid with an imaginary friend. Weird, but harmless.

**Captain Ys and the rabbit girl know.** They are aware of all sentient beings on the ship. Sketch's existence is not hidden from them -- they simply have not announced it to the rest of the crew. Brod did not know they knew until an emergency forced the issue.

### The Reveal

During a crisis, Ys calls for "all hands on deck." He grabs the sketchbook, yells at it to get to work or get thrown out. Sketch manifests. The crew sees her for the first time. Brod's imaginary friend is standing in front of them, very real, very much a person, and apparently being drafted into emergency labor by the captain.

Ys and the rabbit girl confirm, without particular drama, that they are aware of all sentient beings on the ship. They have always known about Sketch. This was never a secret to them -- just something they did not consider worth discussing until it became operationally relevant.

For Brod, the reveal is a different kind of shock. His private companion -- the one thing that was just his, that nobody else could see or judge -- is now visible to the entire crew. The people who bully him and provoke him now know about the sketchbook girl. Whether this changes his dynamic with the crew, and whether Sketch's personality makes things better or worse for him socially, plays out after the reveal.

---

## Relationship with Brod

Sketch is Brod's closest companion. She has been with him longer than Ys, longer than any crew, longer than the mercenary work. She is the sketchbook he has always carried -- the one he draws in constantly, the one he talks to when he thinks nobody is listening.

Their dynamic is complementary opposites: he is anxious, she is confident. He withdraws, she engages. He mumbles, she announces. She is everything he is not, and she lives in the thing he is best at -- his art.

Whether Sketch is something Brod created through his drawing-casting magic, something that was always alive inside the sketchbook, or something else entirely is an open question. The origin matters less than the reality: she is real, she is sentient, and she is his.

## Open Questions

1. **Origin** -- How did Sketch become sentient? Did Brod's drawing-casting magic bring her to life? Was the sketchbook always alive? Did someone else create her?
2. **Combat capability** -- Can Sketch fight? Does she have abilities of her own, or is she non-combat like the mechanic?
3. **The sketchbook connection** -- If the sketchbook is damaged or destroyed, what happens to Sketch? Are they the same entity or is the book just a vessel?
4. **Brod's magic without Sketch** -- Is Sketch connected to his drawing-casting ability? Does his magic work differently when she is manifested vs. in book form?
5. **Post-reveal dynamics** -- How does the protagonist react to Sketch? Does she bully Sketch too, or does Sketch hold her own? Does Sketch get along with the rabbit girl?
6. **Her awareness** -- When she is in book form, can she see and hear everything? Has she been watching the crew the entire time?
