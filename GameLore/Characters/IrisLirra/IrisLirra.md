# Iris Lirra -- The Professora

> *She brought the girl in by mistake. She nearly got her killed by mistake. The decision to save her was the only part that was deliberate.*

## Nature and Position

Iris Lirra is a member of the Black Rose -- a secretive collective of Black elf-human hybrids who built and operate the Blaze Network. Within the group, she functions as a recruiter and instructor. She identifies potential candidates, brings them in, and oversees their integration.

She is good at this. Good enough that when she mistook a fully human girl for a half-elf, nobody questioned her judgment until it was too late.

As a hybrid, Iris lives in the space between worlds that defines the Black Rose's entire reason for existing. She is not a pureblood elf. She does not live a thousand years. She does not pick up magic at the rate elves do. But she has had access to the Blaze Network -- decades of compressed study that brought her to a level of magical competence that her biology alone would never have allowed. She is proof of concept for everything the Black Rose built.

---

## Personality

Iris is an **educator by instinct.** She sees potential in people before she sees anything else. This is both her greatest strength and the flaw that nearly killed the protagonist -- she saw a girl who moved like an elf, spoke like an elf, and carried the weight of someone caught between worlds, and she assumed hybrid rather than human because the alternative did not occur to her. She saw someone who needed the Black Rose, and she acted.

She is **warm without being soft.** Iris cares about her students. She learns their histories, understands their pain, and meets them where they are. But she does not coddle. The Blaze Network is not a gentle experience -- sixty years of isolated study with no physical change, no social life, no experiences beyond learning -- and Iris prepares people for that reality without sugarcoating it.

She is **politically sharp.** When the Black Rose ordered the protagonist's execution, Iris did not throw herself in front of the girl and beg. She advocated. She built a case. She found the option -- targeted memory erasure on a human mind -- that satisfied the group's security requirements while preserving the protagonist's life. This was not just compassion. It was negotiation under pressure, performed by someone who understood the organization's priorities and knew how to work within them.

She is **burdened by what she did.** Iris personally erased the Blaze Network from the protagonist's memory. She did this knowing it was the only way to let the girl live. She did this knowing it would hollow out the protagonist's understanding of their relationship -- leaving the bond intact in feeling but stripped of its context. The protagonist remembers Iris. She does not remember why Iris matters as much as she does. Iris lives with that asymmetry.

---

## The Mistake

Iris brought the protagonist into the Black Rose at age 14-15, believing her to be a half-elf. The mistake was understandable: the protagonist was raised entirely in elven culture by Sirus Vel Ouef, carries elven mannerisms, speaks with elven formality, and navigates elven social customs naturally. She does not act human because she was never raised as one.

When the Black Rose discovered the protagonist was fully human -- adopted, not hybrid -- her execution was ordered immediately. Iris's recruitment error had created an existential security breach. An outsider had seen the group's inner workings, met its members, and potentially learned about the Blaze Network.

Iris carried responsibility for this. Her judgment had put both the protagonist and the organization at risk.

---

## The Bond

During the protagonist's time with the Black Rose, Iris and the girl formed a genuine connection. Iris learned the protagonist's full history:

- The war that destroyed her birth country and family
- The elf siblings who found her as a baby
- The sister's compassion curdling into indifference
- Sirus's quiet, unwavering love
- The years of falling behind elf peers despite constant effort
- Elidyr's three months of imprinted discipline

Iris understood what it meant to be caught between worlds. As a hybrid, she lived a version of that herself -- not elf enough for elves, not human enough for humans. The protagonist's version was more extreme (fully human, raised as elf, belonging to neither), but the shape of the pain was familiar.

The bond was real. Iris was not performing mentorship. She cared about this girl -- cared enough to risk her standing in the Black Rose to keep her alive.

---

## The Memory Erasure

After the spy incident proved the protagonist's loyalty and earned her a reprieve, Iris advocated for a solution: targeted memory erasure.

Because the protagonist was fully human, her mind could be precisely edited. Elf and hybrid minds resist selective memory manipulation. A human mind could be shaped without resistance.

Iris personally performed the procedure. She erased:
- The Blaze Network -- its existence, location, mechanics, everything
- The internal structure and operational details of the Black Rose
- The identities and roles of members beyond surface-level awareness

She did not erase:
- The Black Rose's name
- Herself -- the protagonist remembers Iris Lirra
- The fact that the protagonist was part of the group
- The 60 years of magical knowledge gained inside the Blaze Network

The result: the protagonist left knowing she was part of something called the Black Rose. She remembers Iris. She remembers a bond. But she is an outsider now -- her only real connection to the organization is Iris herself, and even that connection is incomplete because the protagonist cannot recall the full weight of what Iris did for her.

Iris chose this. She gave the protagonist freedom at the cost of being half-forgotten. The protagonist knows Iris matters. She cannot explain why Iris matters as much as she does.

---

## Relationship to the Protagonist -- Present Day

The protagonist is 17 and on a journey to the center of the Mirralata System. Iris is somewhere else -- still with the Black Rose, still operating, still carrying the knowledge of what she did and what it cost.

The protagonist remembers Iris by name. She remembers the feeling of the bond. She knows Iris is her only real connection to the Black Rose. But the relationship exists in limbo: close in feeling, incomplete in understanding. The protagonist cannot reconstruct why this woman shaped her so deeply, because the context -- the Blaze Network, the death order, the spy incident, the memory erasure itself -- is gone or fragmented.

Whether Iris is aware of the protagonist's current journey, whether she would help or stay hidden, and whether the two will cross paths again are open questions.
