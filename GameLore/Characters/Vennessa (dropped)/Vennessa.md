# Vennessa -- The Mouse Woman

> *She is not here for the war. She is not here for the prophecy. She is here because someone took her cat and she wants it back.*

## Appearance

Vennessa is small. Noticeably small -- short, soft-framed, with the rounded ears and fine features of a mouse anthropomorph. She does not command physical presence. In a crew containing a 4'3" muscular teenager who punches soldiers across rooms, a shape-shifting ghost, and a 4'9" alien with a scythe, Vennessa is the one who looks like she should not be in any of these situations.

She is a mouse. She looks like a mouse. There is nothing about her that suggests she belongs on a battlefield.

---

## How She Joined

Vennessa was not part of the original crew. She is not hired. She is not on Ys's payroll.

### The Star Cat

Vennessa had a **star cat** -- a rare breed. A **wealthy collector** saw it and wanted it. He offered money. Vennessa refused. He offered more. She refused again. So he **took it**.

The collector is a man who hoards rare and exotic pets. He lives in the **Mayler Realm** -- a realm notorious for **poaching, animal abuse, and overall terrible conditions for captive creatures**. The Mayler Realm is where rare animals go to be collected, displayed, and mistreated by people with the money to acquire them and the indifference to care for them properly. Vennessa's star cat is in the hands of someone who sees living creatures as trophies, in a realm that enables exactly that kind of ownership.

### The Worm and the Class Divide

Vennessa tried to follow. But the **galaxy-sized worm guardian** passing through the Mirralata System has blocked cross-realm travel -- the same worm that stranded Robert Poole's train.

The critical detail: the travel blockage **only applies to regular people**. The rich and powerful still move freely between realms. They have the ships, the connections, the resources to navigate around the worm's disruption. Ordinary people are stuck. The wealthy collector who took Vennessa's cat had no trouble getting back to the Mayler Realm with his prize. Vennessa, who has none of those resources, was left stranded with no way to follow.

The worm did not just block travel. It created a **class barrier**. The same event that inconvenienced Robert's commute made it impossible for Vennessa to chase the man who stole from her. The rich move. The poor wait. Vennessa refused to wait.

### How She Found Them

Vennessa was eavesdropping. Ys and his crew were discussing how to cross a dangerous Anomaly Realm blocking their route. A **wealthy friend of Ys** -- a resident of the Mayler Realm -- suggested the solution: a rare creature kept by a collector in the Mayler Realm. The friend assured Ys that the caretaker **wouldn't miss or even notice** the creature was gone. The collection is so vast, the neglect so thorough, that one creature disappearing would barely register.

Ys decided to **steal the creature and give it a better home**. The plan was never to buy, trade, or negotiate. It was always a heist -- take the creature from someone who does not care for it, use it to cross the Anomaly Realm, and give it a life better than a trophy case.

Vennessa heard all of this. She heard "Mayler Realm." She heard "collector." She heard the crew was going to the exact place where her star cat was being held. That was all she needed.

### The Stowaway

Vennessa never asked. She never approached Ys, never requested passage, never gave him the chance to say yes or no. She just **got on the ship** and hid.

She knew what the answer would be. Ys was not going to take on another vulnerable passenger on a ship already carrying a prophecy child through an active warzone. He has Robert to protect. He has the protagonist to deliver. He has a crew in a system that is actively trying to kill them. A small, non-combatant mouse woman with a personal quest? He would have said no. So she never asked.

By the time Ys found her, the crew was already in transit. Dumping her was not viable -- they were past the point where leaving her somewhere safe was practical. She was on the ship. She was staying on the ship. Ys accepted this with the cold pragmatism that defines him: if she is here, she works. If she works, she earns her keep. If she earns her keep, he will find a safe place to leave her when the opportunity arises.

He is still looking for that place. He **really** wants to kick her off. The problem is that the Mayler Realm -- the only destination that makes sense for Vennessa -- is **very close to the center of the Mirralata System**. That means there is no convenient early stop. No "safe place along the way" that aligns with Vennessa's needs. Dropping her anywhere before the Mayler Realm means stranding her in a realm that is not her destination, with no way to reach the collector who has her cat.

Vennessa will be with the crew for **nearly the entire journey**. Ys knows this. He does not like it. But the math does not care what he likes -- the only place that makes sense to leave her is almost at the end of the road.

He did not invite her. She did not ask. And she is going to be on his ship for almost the entire 10-game arc whether he wants it or not.

#### The whole crew knows why she is here

**Her motivation is not private and never has been.** The moment Ys found her in the hold she had to explain herself, and the explanation is the entire story above: the cat, the collector, the Mayler Realm, the worm. By the end of that day every person aboard knew all of it.

Write her accordingly. **Nothing about the star cat is a secret, a reveal, or a confession.** There is no scene where Vennessa admits why she is on this ship, because there is nobody left to admit it to. If she raises it, she is raising something everyone in the room already knows.

What is true instead is stranger and worse: **it has been common knowledge for four chapters and not one person has brought it up.** Not to help, not to ask, not in passing. Her reason for existing on this crew was explained once, filed, and never spoken of again. This is the same fact as the nickname -- see § Naming -- and it is why Ys's running "want me to drop you off?" joke works at all. He is needling her about the one thing everybody knows and nobody discusses.

### Why the Crew Goes to the Mayler Realm

Vennessa's mission and the crew's journey eventually converge. In a **future game**, the crew needs to venture into the **Mayler Realm** to find and steal a **rare creature** capable of helping them cross an Anomaly Realm that blocks their path toward the center of the Mirralata System.

The Mayler Realm is not a place the crew would go by choice. It is a realm built on the exploitation of rare creatures -- the exact kind of creature the crew needs. To get one, they will have to navigate a world of collectors, poachers, and black-market animal traders.

This means Vennessa's personal mission -- recovering her star cat from the wealthy collector -- and the crew's strategic need -- acquiring a rare creature for realm traversal -- **happen in the same place**. Vennessa is no longer just a stray passenger Ys is looking to drop off. She is someone who knows the target, knows the realm's culture, and has a personal stake in making the operation succeed. When the crew enters the Mayler Realm, Vennessa becomes essential.

## Drawing-Casting Magic

Vennessa uses the same type of magic as Brod Tsumi -- **drawing-casting**, the ability to bring drawings to life. But where Brod's magic is functional and combat-oriented, Vennessa's is **refined**. She is the better mage of the two by a clear margin.

> **She casts entirely on her own, and she has no connection to Sketch.**
>
> Vennessa animates her own creations by her own power. Sketch is Brod's and only Brod's -- not Vennessa's animator, not her courier, not a shared crew tool. Vennessa does not use Sketch, does not need Sketch, does not speak to Sketch, and does not know Sketch is a person at all.
>
> When Brod and Vennessa work the same job, they are running **two independent techniques side by side**. Brod draws and Sketch brings his lines out. Vennessa paints and her strokes come alive on their own. The outputs meet; the methods never mix. **This mistake has been written into finished scenes three separate times -- Ch2 twice and Ch4 once. Do not write it a fourth.**

### The Tool Gap

Brod and Vennessa are **similarly skilled** in drawing-casting. The difference is not talent -- it is equipment.

Brod's dual swords function as **ink pens**. They are thin, precise, and limited in what they can produce per stroke. He draws with fine lines. His creations are quick, functional, and combat-effective, but they lack the breadth and detail that broader strokes allow. He is an artist working with ballpoint pens -- everything he makes is impressive given the tools, but the tools constrain him.

Vennessa's pen brush is a **big brush**. It covers more surface, lays down more material per stroke, and allows for the sweeping, detailed work that produces creations convincing enough to fool armies. Her fake soldiers pass for real troops not because she is more talented than Brod, but because her tool lets her paint where he can only sketch.

The Brush Blade (later) is the same philosophy taken further: a **big brush with a sword attached**. It amplifies everything the pen brush already did while adding stored magic and self-defense capability.

This means the quality gap between their creations is a **tool gap, not a skill gap**. If Brod had Vennessa's brush, his drawings would be just as refined. If Vennessa had Brod's swords, her work would be just as constrained. They are peers in casting ability working with fundamentally different instruments.

Where Brod pulls ahead is **everything else**. His dual swords are weapons first, ink pens second. He has real sword fighting ability -- he can hold his own in melee, defend himself, and engage directly when his drawings are not enough. Vennessa has none of that. Her pen brush is a tool, not a weapon. The gap in their casting output is tools. The gap in their combat capability is training.

### Chapter 2 -- The Directional Paintings

In the cave system beneath the mountain, Vennessa left **directional paintings** on the walls -- markers to guide scattered crew members toward the rendezvous point. They were clear, well-placed, and easy to follow for anyone who knew what to look for.

The problem: they were easy to follow for anyone, period. The **Wolf Squad** found the paintings and used them to track the crew underground. Vennessa's attempt to help the crew inadvertently brought the enemy to them.

This is a defining moment for her character. She acted with good intention and real skill, and it backfired. The paintings were not a mistake of craft -- they were a mistake of circumstance. She is not a soldier. She does not think about counter-tracking or operational security. She thinks about helping the people around her, and sometimes that help creates new problems.

### Chapter 3 -- The Fake Soldiers

While captured by the Wolf Squad, Ys secretly freed Vennessa and Brod and instructed them to prepare drawing-cast minions for deployment. Brod drew combat creatures. Vennessa drew **soldiers that looked nearly identical to real army troops**.

When Ys gave the signal, these fake soldiers flooded the battlefield. They were convincing enough to **blend with real forces** -- same uniforms, same proportions, same movement. The chaos they created was decisive. In a three-way battle between two armies and a dragon, nobody could tell which soldiers were real and which were Vennessa's creations. The confusion bought the crew time to escape.

This is the clearest demonstration of what separates Vennessa from Brod. Brod can make things that fight. Vennessa can make things that **deceive**. Her art is so refined that her creations pass for real in the middle of active combat. That is not a combat skill -- it is an intelligence and infiltration capability that nobody on the crew except Kat can match in its own domain.

### Chapter 4 -- Bodyguard Duty

Inside the ancient sealed structure, with Kat, Ys, and Vennessa herself injured, she serves as **Robert Poole's bodyguard** while the mechanic repairs the ship. Injured but functional enough to protect someone who cannot protect themselves.

This pairing -- the mouse woman guarding the mechanic -- is their operating dynamic whenever the crew splits. Vennessa is not a frontline fighter, but she can create defensive constructs, decoys, and distractions with her drawings. Paired with Robert, who has no combat capability at all, Vennessa is the layer of protection between the mechanic and whatever is trying to kill them.

## Combat & Abilities

### The Pen Brush

Vennessa's casting tool is a **small pen brush** -- compact, portable, and unremarkable to look at. It is the instrument through which all of her drawing-casting magic flows. Without it, she cannot cast. With it, she can create things that fool armies.

The pen brush is not a weapon in the traditional sense. She does not swing it, stab with it, or channel destructive energy through it. It is a tool for **creating** -- and what she creates is where her power lies.

### Physical Capabilities

Vennessa's physical strength is that of an **average human woman**. She is small, she is not trained for combat, and she has no physical edge that would help in a fight. She cannot punch through armor, she cannot overpower soldiers, and she cannot take the kind of punishment IndigoLLiy absorbs routinely.

She is, however, **tougher than Robert Poole**. Robert is a non-combatant who freezes, seals herself in the ship, and needs protection at all times. Vennessa is not that fragile. She participates in battles. She stands on the field -- from a distance, contributing through her magic -- rather than hiding behind the nearest wall. She is scared, but she does not retreat to the ship and lock the door.

This puts her in a specific tier on the crew:

| Tier | Member | Combat Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Frontline** | IndigoLLiy, Ys, Kat | Direct engagement. Can fight and win. |
| **Combat support** | Brod | Drawing-cast combat creatures. Fights with swords when needed. |
| **Ranged support** | Vennessa | Drawing-cast creations from a distance. Participates but does not engage directly. |
| **Non-combatant** | Robert | Does not fight. Needs protection. Contributes through the ship and engineering. |

### Universal Energy

Vennessa **cannot easily draw on Universal Energy**. In a crew where UE supplements combat ability -- where IndigoLLiy uses it to survive bullets and Brod channels it through his drawings -- Vennessa operates almost entirely without it. The ambient energy of whatever realm they are in does not flow to her the way it flows to others.

This means her drawing-casting magic is **purely magical**, not UE-enhanced. What she creates comes from skill and artistry, not from channeling the universe's raw power. In low-UE realms, this is less of a disadvantage (everyone struggles). In mid-to-high UE realms, it means her creations, while beautifully crafted, lack the raw energy backing that a UE-capable caster could provide.

Her strength has never been power. It has always been **precision and deception**. She does not need Universal Energy to draw fake soldiers that fool real armies. She needs a pen brush and time.

### How She Fights

Vennessa does not fight the way anyone else on the crew fights. She supports **from afar** during battles:

- **Decoy creation** -- Drawing fake targets, fake soldiers, or environmental illusions that misdirect enemy attention and split their forces
- **Defensive constructs** -- Drawing barriers, shields, or obstacles between the crew and incoming threats
- **Battlefield confusion** -- Flooding the field with drawn creations that make it impossible to tell what is real and what is not
- **Bodyguard constructs** -- Creating protective drawings around Robert or other vulnerable crew members when the crew splits

She is not good at direct fighting. If an enemy closes the distance, she is in serious trouble. Her pen brush is not a weapon in melee. Her body is not conditioned for impact. Her only option at close range is to run, hide, or hope someone on the crew intervenes.

Her value is in **never being in that situation** -- staying back, staying quiet, and making the battlefield confusing enough that nobody comes looking for the small mouse woman with the pen brush.

She **cannot pilot the ship**. Unlike Robert (fourth pilot) and the rest of the crew, Vennessa has no flight capability. She is a passenger in every sense when the ship is moving.

### The Brush Blade (Future Game)

In a later game, Ys **buys Vennessa the Brush Blade** -- an **arche blade** that stores a large amount of leftover magic. It functions as both a casting tool and a weapon for basic self-defense.

| Property | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Arche blade |
| **Primary function** | Drawing-casting tool -- significantly more powerful than the pen brush |
| **Secondary function** | Self-defense weapon -- usable as a blade in melee, though Vennessa is not a swordfighter |
| **Special property** | Stores leftover magic, giving Vennessa access to a reservoir of power she cannot generate on her own through Universal Energy |
| **Cast strength** | Much stronger than the pen brush. Her creations gain power, durability, and scale. |

The Brush Blade addresses Vennessa's two biggest weaknesses: her casts are limited by having no UE backing, and she is completely defenseless at close range. The stored magic compensates for the first. The blade form addresses the second -- not by turning her into a swordfighter, but by giving her something between "pen" and "nothing" when an enemy gets close.

### The Ultimatum

Ys did not buy the Brush Blade out of generosity. He bought it with a condition: **if Vennessa did not learn to use it, he was kicking her off the crew.**

This is Ys being Ys. He has carried an uninvited stowaway for multiple games. He has watched her contribute from the back lines with a pen brush while the rest of the crew bleeds. He has accepted her presence because the math did not allow him to drop her off. But the Brush Blade is the line: she upgrades or she is gone. He is done carrying someone who will not grow.

**Brod and IndigoLLiy help her learn.** Brod, as a fellow drawing-caster, helps her adapt her technique to the new tool -- the Brush Blade channels differently than the pen brush, and the transition is not seamless. The stored magic behaves differently from her own casting. IndigoLLiy, who fights with bladed weapons (pike, sword, scythe), helps Vennessa get accustomed to holding and using the blade for basic self-defense.

This is the first time IndigoLLiy and Vennessa have a sustained, purposeful interaction. The protagonist who treats Vennessa as neutral background is now actively training her. Whether this changes their dynamic -- whether teaching someone to fight creates a bond that indifference never did -- is an open thread.

For Brod, helping Vennessa is personal. She is the better drawing-caster. He has always known this. Now he is the one teaching her how to use a tool she has never held. The student helping the master adapt. The dynamic reversal says something about both of them.

## Personality

**Determined.** Vennessa has one priority: her star cat. She joined this crew because their path aligned with her search. She stays because the alternatives are worse. But she has never stopped looking. Every realm they pass through, every stop they make, she is scanning for information, leads, any thread that might bring her closer to her cat. This is not obsession in the frantic sense -- it is quiet, absolute resolve.

**Not a soldier, but not hiding.** Vennessa is small, not physically imposing, and not suited for direct confrontation. She does not pretend otherwise. But unlike Robert, she does not retreat to the ship when fighting starts. She stands on the battlefield -- at the back, at a distance, with her pen brush -- and contributes. Her magic is her tool, and she uses it for support, utility, and deception rather than head-on combat. She is the crew member who creates distractions, not the one who charges in. But she is present. She participates. That counts for more than people give it credit for.

**Normal.** Brod considers Vennessa one of the only **normal people on the crew**, alongside Robert. She does not bully him. She does not provoke him. She is not mysterious, not dangerous, not carrying some dark secret. She is a small woman looking for her cat. In a crew of ghosts, aliens, prophecy children, and sentient sketchbooks, Vennessa is simply a person with a problem.

**Helpful.** The directional paintings in Chapter 2 were not ordered. Nobody told Vennessa to leave markers in the caves. She did it because she saw a need -- scattered crew members who needed guidance -- and addressed it with what she had. She helps because it is in her nature, not because it is her role.

**Quiet.** Like Robert, Vennessa exists at a lower social volume than the louder members of the crew. She is not performing. She is not seeking attention. She does her work, contributes her magic when it matters, and otherwise occupies the calm space on the ship that Brod gravitates toward when the protagonist and Kat are too much.

## Role on the Crew

### Drawing-Caster

Vennessa's primary combat contribution is her drawing-casting magic. She creates minions, decoys, and constructs that serve tactical purposes beyond raw fighting power. Her specialty is **quality over quantity** -- fewer creations, but each one detailed enough to pass as real.

### Robert's Bodyguard

When the crew splits, Vennessa and Robert operate together. The mouse woman guards the mechanic. This pairing makes tactical sense -- neither is a frontline combatant, so isolating them from the fighters would leave both exposed. Together, Vennessa's magic provides defense while Robert works.

### Support and Utility

Vennessa fills gaps. Directional markers in caves. Fake soldiers on a battlefield. A bodyguard for the mechanic. She does not have a single defined role the way Robert (mechanic) or Kat (strategist/medic) does. She adapts to what the crew needs and applies her art accordingly.

## Naming -- "the mouse" and who says it

**This is a writing rule. It holds in every scene, in every chapter.**

Most of the crew does not call her Vennessa. They call her **"the mouse"** or **"the mouse woman"** -- Indigo, Ys, Brod, and Kim all do it, in dialogue and in narration. It is not cruel and none of them intend anything by it. That is the problem.

"The mouse" is what you call someone who has been fully explained to you and then filed away. They know her name. They know her species, her magic, her cat, her collector, and her destination. They know everything, and they still reach for the label, because she was categorised early and nobody has had a reason to revisit it since. **The nickname and the four chapters of nobody asking about the star cat are the same fact wearing two faces.**

**Kat Arlean Mori is the exception.** Kat has never said "the mouse" in any script, in any chapter -- she uses Vennessa's name every single time, and she has never remarked on doing it. This was true before it was a rule and it is now a rule.

- Kat: **always "Vennessa."** Never the nickname. Not once, not as a joke.
- Everyone else: the nickname is fine and correct, and should stay common.
- Vennessa herself: has never objected to it and never will. She notices. She does not consider it worth a fight.

The payoff is quiet and cumulative. If the nickname stays common everywhere else, then one person using her name reads as warmth without anyone having to say anything about it. **Do not retrofit the nickname out of other characters' mouths, and do not let it into Kat's.**

## Key Relationships

### Captain Ys

Ys did not want her. She never asked -- she knew he would say no. She stowed away, and by the time he found her, turning back was not an option. He accepted it with cold pragmatism: she is here, so she works.

He sees Vennessa as a **stray** -- an uninvited passenger he wants gone. He really wants to kick her off. But the Mayler Realm is close to the center of the Mirralata System, which means there is no sensible place to drop her before then. She is stuck on the ship for nearly the entire journey, and Ys is stuck with her.

He did not recruit her. She did not ask. She is going to be on his ship for almost every game.

Ys **jokes about kicking her off. A lot.** It is a running bit -- dry, casual, delivered like it is the most reasonable thing in the world. "We're passing a settlement, Vennessa. Want me to slow down?" The humor lands the way Ys's humor always does: somewhere between funny and unsettling. Vennessa is never entirely sure how much of it is a joke. The unease is constant. She lives on a ship captained by someone who regularly reminds her she was not invited and could be removed at any time.

In a later game, Ys buys her the **Brush Blade** -- but with an ultimatum: learn to use it or get off the crew. This is not a gift. It is an investment with conditions. Ys is done carrying someone who will not evolve. The fact that he bought it at all -- that he spent resources on the stowaway instead of just finding a place to dump her -- says more than any "I'll kick you off" joke ever could.

**She speaks to him exactly once in the first four chapters, and he does not answer.** In the cell in Ch3 1.6, after Brod fobs her off with "She's Indigo," she goes over Brod's head and asks Ys directly what happens to Indigo. It lands closer to the sacrifice than anyone in that cell realises. Ys pauses one second too long and says "Draw."

Vennessa reads this as a captain shutting down chatter during an operation, which is exactly what it is designed to look like, and she goes back to painting without pushing. **She must not suspect anything.** She is structurally the right person to graze the game's largest secret and notice nothing -- her established mode is one soft question followed by reading the room and stopping, set up in Ch1 5.3 with "...Is she okay?" and *(Vennessa blinks. Decides not to ask.)* The audience is meant to catch what she misses.

### Brod Tsumi

Fellow drawing-caster. Brod considers Vennessa one of the only normal people on the crew. He gravitates toward her and Robert as anchors of sanity -- but with Vennessa specifically, it goes further than calm. They are **usually in sync about situations**. When something happens on the crew, Brod and Vennessa tend to read it the same way, react the same way, arrive at the same conclusion. He appreciates being able to have a **normal conversation** -- no bullying from the protagonist, no provocation from Kat, no cryptic non-answers from Ys. Just two people talking about what is happening and agreeing that it is insane.

The magic dynamic between them is notable: they use the same technique at **similar skill levels**, but their tools create a visible output gap. Brod's swords are ink pens -- fine lines, functional results. Vennessa's pen brush is a broad brush -- sweeping strokes, detailed creations. His drawings look like sketches. Hers look like paintings. The difference is the instrument, not the artist.

In a later game, when Ys gives Vennessa the Brush Blade, Brod helps her **adapt her drawing-casting to the new tool**. The Brush Blade channels differently than the pen brush, and the transition is not seamless. Brod's experience adapting his casting to imperfect tools (swords that are really ink pens) makes him the right teacher -- he has always worked with constraints.

### Robert Poole

Bodyguard and protected. They operate together when the crew splits. Vennessa guards Robert while she works on the ship. Their dynamic is practical -- two non-combatants paired together because separating them would leave both vulnerable.

Both are people Brod considers normal. Both exist at the crew's quieter end. Both are temporary passengers with their own reasons for being here that have nothing to do with prophecies or wars.

### IndigoLLiy (The Protagonist)

IndigoLLiy **likes Vennessa**. She messes with her a little -- the same cocky energy she aims at Brod, but lighter, less aggressive. Vennessa allows it. She does not push back the way Brod does, she does not escalate the way Kat would, she just takes it with the quiet acceptance of someone who has bigger things to worry about than a teenager's jokes.

The dynamic is warm but not deep. IndigoLLiy enjoys Vennessa's presence without investing in it emotionally. She is someone Indigo can poke at without consequence -- a safe target in a crew full of people who are either too dangerous (Ys, Kat), too reactive (Brod), or too fragile (Robert) for that kind of casual play.

**Their first real exchange is at the end of Chapter 4, and it is the most Vennessa talks in the game.** Indigo has just come back from the wish machine, which was a lie, and she is asking the crew whether the Mirralata center is another one. Kat answers with evidence, Robert with logistics, Brod with "I'd rather find out than wonder" -- three people all answering *is it real*. Nobody answers the question Indigo actually asked, which is *what happens if it isn't*.

Vennessa is the only person aboard who can answer that one, because she is the only person aboard who is not here for a prophecy, a paycheck, or an order. She raises the star cat -- something everyone has known for four chapters and nobody has mentioned -- and makes the point plainly: she does not know if the cat is still alive, she has never known, and she would go anyway. So would Indigo. That is not stupidity, it is the price, and the question worth asking is whether you can pay it.

**The asymmetry between them is the whole point.** Vennessa's cat is real and she knows exactly where it is; she simply cannot reach it. Indigo does not know whether there is anything at the center at all. Same devotion, opposite odds -- and the one whose object is real is the one this crew calls "the mouse."

This is recognition, not friendship, and it does not make them close. Indigo answers with a single "...Okay." But it is the first time she has looked at Vennessa as a person rather than a safe target, and it reframes the casual teasing that came before it.

In a later game, when Ys gives Vennessa the Brush Blade, IndigoLLiy helps her **learn to use the blade for self-defense**. This is the first sustained, purposeful interaction between them -- and it shifts the dynamic from casual teasing to something with more substance. The Ch4 ending beat is the seed of it.

### Kat Arlean Mori

Neutral but friendly. Kat does not provoke Vennessa the way she provokes Brod. There is no sexual tension, no relentless teasing, no campaign to get a reaction. Kat is simply **helpful** -- she offers advice, shares practical knowledge, and treats Vennessa with the straightforward warmth of someone who does not see her as a target.

Where Kat's energy toward Brod is predatory and playful, her energy toward Vennessa is almost maternal -- a more experienced woman offering guidance to someone who is clearly out of her depth. This is rare for Kat, who defaults to provocation with most people.

The relationship runs through medicine. Vennessa is a civilian who does not fight, cannot take a hit, and gets hurt anyway, so Kat has patched her up more times than anyone else aboard. Kat is one of the only people who regularly touches her and one of the only people who gets time alone with her, because injuries create privacy that nothing else on this ship does.

Kat is also **the only person who notices her work.** Vennessa's magic is force multiplication -- decoys, copies, barriers -- and it is invisible precisely when it succeeds. Kat is the crew's other support caster and knows that problem from the inside. In Ch3 6.2 she points out that Vennessa spent hours in a cell painting an entire army and not one person has said a word about it. She is right, and she is the only one who would notice.

**Kat calls her by her name, always.** See § Naming above. It is the quietest and most important thing in this relationship.

The one exception to the warmth: Kat will occasionally **ship Vennessa with Brod** -- or with others -- specifically to make Brod flustered. This is not her primary method of tormenting him (she prefers direct seduction for that), but when she wants variety, suggesting that Brod and Vennessa are a thing -- or that Vennessa and someone else would be cute together -- gets a reaction out of him that direct flirtation does not. It is rare compared to Kat's usual approach, but effective.

The first instance is Ch3 6.2, while Kat is stitching her arm. Warm voice, completely stoic face, no visible tell, and "I haven't said anything, Brod" -- which is literally true, and which leaves him arguing with an accusation nobody made.

It has a second effect Kat probably intends. **It forces Vennessa to react**, which she otherwise never does. Her default is one soft question and then letting it go. Here that default fails: she deflects twice and then gives up with "I don't want to be in this." Kat is the only crew member who reliably breaks her composure, and watching the composure break is characterisation Vennessa gets nowhere else.

## The Star Cat

Vennessa's entire reason for being on the crew is her **kidnapped star cat**.

| Detail | Status |
|---|---|
| **What is a star cat?** | TBD -- rare breed, valuable enough to attract a wealthy collector's attention. |
| **Who took it?** | A wealthy collector who hoards rare and exotic pets. Lives in the Mayler Realm. |
| **Why?** | He saw the cat, decided it was a rare breed worth owning, and took it when Vennessa refused to sell. |
| **Where is it now?** | The Mayler Realm -- a realm notorious for poaching, animal abuse, and terrible conditions for captive creatures. |
| **When does recovery happen?** | A future game. The crew ventures into the Mayler Realm to steal a rare creature that can help them cross an Anomaly Realm. Vennessa's personal mission and the crew's strategic need converge. |

Vennessa will not stop. The cat is not a plot device she will forget about. It is the reason she is here, and it will remain the reason she is here until it is resolved. The Mayler Realm is where it ends -- one way or another.

### It is a destination, not a mystery

**Do not write this as a search.** Vennessa is not hunting for leads, chasing rumours, or asking Kat to look anything up. She has known the answer since the day it happened: she knows the man's name, she knows the house, she knows the realm. Nothing is missing except the ability to get there, and that is a travel problem created by the worm and the class divide -- not an information problem. There is nothing for anyone on this crew to find out for her.

**And do not write it as a secret.** The entire crew has known all of it since Ys pulled her out of the hold. See § How She Joined -- *The whole crew knows why she is here.*

What she actually does not know is whether the cat is still alive. She has never known and she has no way to find out. It has been a long time, the Mayler Realm is notorious for how captive animals are kept there, and she is riding a warship that will not reach the place for most of a ten-game arc. **She goes anyway.** That is the load-bearing fact about her, and it is the one she finally says out loud in the Ch4 ending beat -- see § Key Relationships -- IndigoLLiy.

## What Makes Her Important

Vennessa is the crew's **deception specialist**. In a story where the protagonist fights with a pike and raw determination, where Ys operates through mystery and command, and where Brod provides functional magic support -- Vennessa's contribution is **illusion and misdirection**. Her fake soldiers changed the outcome of the biggest battle in the game. Her directional paintings, though they backfired, demonstrated initiative and care.

She is also the narrative proof that the crew is not a military unit. Vennessa is a small mouse woman looking for her cat. She ended up on a ship being hunted across an active warzone. She contributes what she can, which happens to be art so good it fools armies, and she waits for the day she can leave and continue her real mission.

The crew has two temporary passengers who are supposed to be dropped off -- Robert and Vennessa. Robert's train got blocked by a worm. Vennessa's cat got taken by someone. Neither asked to be here. Both keep proving useful enough that losing them would hurt.
