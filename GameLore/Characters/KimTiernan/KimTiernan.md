# Kim Tiernan

> *He is still in the earring. Still conscious. Still him. The clock is running.*

---

## Character Profile

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Name** | Kim Tiernan |
| **Species** | Elf |
| **Age** | Same peer group as the protagonist -- grew up together |
| **Status** | Soul sacrificed. Being contained in a failing vessel (the earring). Still conscious. |
| **Role** | The protagonist's oldest friend. In-game hint system. The reason for the entire journey. |
| **Relationship** | The protagonist's deepest bond -- the one person who saw her fully and chose her as family. |

---

## Who He Is

Kim Tiernan is not special. He is not a warrior, not a chosen one, not someone with extraordinary abilities. He is an ordinary elf boy who grew up alongside the protagonist in the elf-controlled nation. His significance comes entirely from two things: the depth of his bond with the protagonist, and the choice he made.

**Funny.** Kim is genuinely entertaining. He is the kind of person who makes a room lighter by being in it -- not through performance or trying too hard, but because he finds humor naturally and shares it easily. He is kinda goofy. He does not take himself seriously enough to be cool, and that is exactly what makes people like him.

**Socially smart.** He reads people well. He knows how to navigate groups, how to defuse tension, how to make introductions that stick. He knows how to **deal with bullies** -- not by fighting them, but by understanding them, outmaneuvering them socially, and making it not worth their time. He built a good group around himself, and he was the glue that held it together.

**Kind.** Not performatively, not strategically. Just kind. He treats people well because that is who he is.

**Moral core.** Underneath the humor and the social ease is a **strong moral code instilled by his mother and father**. This is the foundation everything else sits on. Kim will joke, he will goof around, he will be the lightest person in the room -- but there is a line he will not cross. His parents gave him a code, and he holds it. It is not rigid or preachy. It is quiet and absolute. He knows who he is, and no amount of pressure changes that.

He was **nice to her**. That is where it started. Among the elf children who outpaced her in magic, who saw her as the human kid who did not belong, Kim was the one who was simply kind. He did not make a statement about it. He did not champion her cause. He was just a kid who treated her like a person when most of the others did not.

He was her **social bridge**. Kim was the reason the protagonist was able to enter friend groups at all. Without him, she would have had Sirus and nobody else her own age. The friendships Kim opened for her were limited -- she was still the human outsider, still behind, still different -- but they existed because Kim brought her in. He made the social world survivable.

Like every other elf kid, Kim was **naturally better at magic** than the protagonist. This was biology, not effort. He outpaced her the same way all elf children did -- effortlessly, without trying, without even noticing the gap. The difference was that Kim never made her feel lesser for it. He was ahead of her in ability and beside her in everything else.

That gap only closed when the protagonist entered the Black Rose and the Blaze Network. By the time she came back, the 60 subjective years of study had put her on a level that no elf her age could match. Kim did not know why. Nobody did.

---

## The Almost-Relationship

Their relationship **bordered on love** when they were children. Not a declaration, not a commitment -- just a closeness that felt like it was heading somewhere neither of them named. They were kids. It was the kind of bond that exists in the space between best friend and something more, where neither person pushes it into definition because the current shape already feels right.

When the protagonist got closer to the **Black Rose group**, they distanced a little. Her life was pulling in a direction Kim could not follow -- a secret organization, dangerous training, a world he was not part of. The closeness cooled. Not because either of them chose it, but because she was becoming someone he could not fully reach.

She was gone for roughly **a year** (the Black Rose period -- though the Blaze Network compressed far more time internally). When she came back, Kim **welcomed her into the social circle all the same**. Nothing changed on his end. No grudge, no questioning where she had been, no awkwardness about the distance. He simply opened the door again as if she had never left.

Kim has his own **girlfriend** now. The protagonist is already on good terms with her -- no jealousy, no tension, no unresolved romantic weight. Whatever the almost-relationship was, it settled into something both of them are comfortable with. He is her oldest friend. She is his. The love is there, but it is not the kind that demands a label.

---

## The Sacrifice

Three months before the game begins, a **weapon was fired from all the way across the world**. It created a **soul-destroying wave** -- a tsunami of annihilation that swept outward from the point of detonation, obliterating the souls of every living being it passed through. Not killing them. Erasing them. No afterlife, no peace, no release. Souls unmade entirely.

The warning reached the city with only **20 minutes** to prepare.

The city had a **barrier** -- a protective field capable of shielding its inhabitants from the wave. Those inside the barrier would survive. Those outside would not.

Kim, the protagonist, and their families -- Sirus, Sorain, their children, Kim's parents -- were on the **outer edge of the city**. The outer edge meant less protection, more distance to cover, and less time. Twenty minutes was not enough. They could not reach the barrier in time.

Kim made his choice.

He created a **smaller barrier** using his own body to sustain it. Not a spell he cast and walked away from. A barrier he held open with himself as the fuel. His soul -- his entire being -- was the power source. He fed it into the shield to keep it standing long enough for everyone behind it to survive the wave.

The wave hit. The barrier held. Kim's soul was consumed to sustain it.

He did not just save the protagonist. He saved everyone she considered family, because he considered them his family too. An ordinary elf boy with no extraordinary abilities, burning himself alive from the inside to buy the people he loved a few more minutes of existence.

In the metaphysical framework of the Mirralata System, souls are tangible. Kim gave his away entirely. This is not merely dying. It is **agony beyond death** -- suffering that continues past the point where life ends. The soul is gone. What remains is conscious torment in a void.

### The Capture

Kim should have vanished completely. The wave should have erased him the same way it erased every unprotected soul in its path -- total obliteration, nothing left.

The protagonist **refused to let that happen**. In the moments after the barrier fell and Kim's form began to dissolve, she caught what was left of him before he was completely obliterated. She contained his being in a **vessel** -- a tiny, thumb-sized container. A desperate act of intervention that preserved what remained of who he is.

---

## The Earring

The vessel is a **thumb-sized container** that the protagonist wears as an **earring**. It hangs from her ear at all times. Kim is always with her -- physically, literally, constantly.

Kim is still present within the vessel. Still conscious. Still himself. He is not a ghost, not an echo, not a recording. He is *him* -- compressed into a failing container.

The protagonist **taps the earring** to talk to him. He can talk back. She should do this often -- regular contact helps keep Kim **somewhat stabilized** within the vessel. Silence is not just lonely. It is dangerous. The longer the vessel goes without interaction, the more Kim degrades. Talking to him is not a comfort. It is maintenance. It is the difference between the vessel lasting the full year and giving out early.

The containment is **not permanent**. The vessel is degrading regardless of how often she talks to him -- conversation slows the decay, it does not stop it. She has **one year** from the moment of the sacrifice. If she does not reach the center of the Mirralata System and find a way to restore him before the vessel gives out, Kim will be obliterated completely. No soul. No afterlife. Nothing.

This is the ticking clock that drives the entire journey.

---

## In-Game Role -- The Hint System

Kim serves as the game's **hint system**. He is always with the protagonist -- always in the earring, always able to speak. When the player is stuck, Kim talks. He observes, comments, and nudges without giving direct answers. He is the voice in her ear -- literally -- that sees what she sees and knows her well enough to point her in the right direction.

The tap mechanic ties the hint system to a physical action. The protagonist taps her earring, Kim responds. This keeps the interaction grounded and personal -- she is not talking to the air, she is touching the thing that holds her best friend and asking for help.

---

## What the Crew Knows

**Kim is not a secret and never was.** The crew knows who he is, what he did, and that he is conscious inside the earring the protagonist wears. They know the vessel is failing and they know it is the reason she is going to the center. None of this has to be explained to anyone aboard.

**They have spoken to him before.** Earlier in the journey, before this game begins, Kim was a voice the crew knew. He talked to them. They talked back. He was not a curiosity or a piece of equipment -- he was a person they had met.

**In this game he speaks to nobody but the protagonist.** Not Kat, not Brod, not Ys, not Vennessa or Robert. The crew does not address him and he does not address them. Every line he has in Chapters 1 through 4 is to her and her alone.

The effect is that somebody the crew used to talk to has gone quiet in an earring three feet away, and not one person remarks on it. Play the crew's awareness of him as settled and unspoken rather than curious. When the protagonist asks in **Ch4 End.1** whether the Mirralata center is another lie, the script notes that she "does not say Kim -- she does not need to." That only works because every person on that deck already knows exactly who she means.

**Captain Ys knows the most about the vessel itself.** He has **examined the earring** and understands exactly what Kim is, what state he is in, and how the containment works -- the mechanics, not just the fact of it. He understands that the earring is the only reason the protagonist is on this journey. If the earring is lost, she will not willingly continue toward the center of the Mirralata System. The earring is not just her emotional anchor -- it is the operational necessity that keeps Ys's mission viable.

---

## Everyone Told Her Not to Go

The protagonist did not leave quietly. Almost everyone who cared about her tried to talk her out of the journey.

- **Kim's family** told her to stop. To let him go. Kim was a hero to them too -- he saved them. But they accepted the loss in a way the protagonist cannot. They did not want to watch her destroy herself chasing something that might be impossible.
- **Kim's girlfriend** tried to talk her out of it. She lost Kim too. She understood the protagonist's pain. But she also understood that the journey would likely kill her.
- **Iris Lirra (the Professora)** -- the protagonist's only real connection to the Black Rose -- weighed in against it. Iris knows what the protagonist is capable of, and she knows what the Mirralata System demands. She did not think the odds justified the risk.
- **Sirus and Sorain** -- her own family. Sirus, who has never stopped investing in her, understood the danger. Sorain, whatever her limitations as a mother, did not want the protagonist dead.

And **Kim himself** -- from inside the earring, still conscious, still him -- told her to stop. The person she is trying to save asked her not to save him.

Every person who mattered to the protagonist lined up and told her the same thing: don't do this. Including the one in the earring.

She had her mind made up. She went anyway.

---

## Relationship with the Protagonist

Kim is the protagonist's **anchor**. Before the sacrifice, he was the one constant that was fully hers -- not complicated by biology (like Sirus), not tainted by withdrawal (like Sorain), not shaped by secrecy (like the Black Rose). Kim was clean. He was her friend because he wanted to be, and he stayed because he chose to.

After the sacrifice, the relationship continues in compressed form. She taps the earring. He responds. The dynamic is the same -- the same person, the same bond -- filtered through a thumb-sized vessel and a deadline. He still knows her better than anyone. She still trusts him more than anyone. The difference is that every conversation might be the last one, and both of them know it.

The protagonist's entire journey -- leaving home, joining Ys's crew, crossing realms, fighting wars she does not care about -- exists because of Kim. She is not doing this for glory or prophecy or adventure. She is doing this because her best friend is dying in her earring and she will not let him go.

## Open Questions

1. **The weapon** -- What fired the soul-destroying wave? Who built it? Was it a military strike, an accident, or something else? Is the entity or nation responsible a known factor in the Mirralata System?
2. **The wave's reach** -- How far did the wave travel? Did it hit just the city's outer edge, or were entire regions affected? How many people were lost?
3. **How she captured him** -- How did the protagonist know how to contain Kim's dissolving being in the vessel? Was it instinct, training, magic she half-remembers from the Blaze Network, or something else?
4. **The restoration** -- What does the protagonist believe is at the center of the Mirralata System that can restore a sacrificed soul? Is this a rumor, a prophecy, or something she was told?
5. **Kim's awareness** -- Can he see what's happening around the protagonist, or does he only hear her? Does he know about the crew, the war, the prophecy?
6. **The friend groups** -- Who were the other elf kids Kim brought her into? Do any of them matter, or was the social access limited and shallow?
7. **The barrier** -- Does the city's main barrier have limits? Could it have been extended to cover the outer edges, or was Kim's situation genuinely hopeless?
8. **Other casualties** -- Did anyone else outside the barrier die? Were Kim and the protagonist's group the only ones caught on the outer edge, or were there mass casualties?
9. **Why he stopped talking to the crew** -- He spoke to them earlier in the journey and speaks only to the protagonist now. Is this Kim withdrawing, the decaying vessel narrowing what he can afford to spend energy on, or the protagonist quietly keeping him to herself? Nothing on screen answers it, and the crew never asks.
10. **What the crew thinks about the silence** -- Somebody they used to talk to has gone quiet in an earring in front of them. Has anyone noticed? Does Kat, who notices everything, have a read on it?

*Resolved:* The sacrifice event (soul-destroying wave weapon, 20-minute warning, outer edge of city, Kim's body-sustained barrier). The vessel (thumb-sized earring container, tap to talk, regular contact stabilizes Kim). Kim's personality (funny, goofy, socially smart, kind, strong moral core from his parents). Crew awareness -- they know him, they have spoken to him before, and in this game he speaks only to the protagonist.
