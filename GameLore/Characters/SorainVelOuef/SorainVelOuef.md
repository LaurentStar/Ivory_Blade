> *She saved the baby. That was real. What came after was the slow, honest answer to whether she meant it.*

## The Rescue

Sorain is the one who picked up the baby.

She and Sirus came across a human infant in the aftermath of a war that had destroyed the protagonist's birth country. Sirus argued to leave her behind. Sorain refused. She chose to bring the baby along when they relocated to an elf-controlled nation using their family connections.

This decision was genuine. Sorain was not performing compassion or making a political gesture. She saw a baby in a warzone and could not walk away. In that moment, she meant it completely. The rescue was real. The love that followed -- at first -- was real too.

---

## The Early Years

Sorain acted as the protagonist's mother figure. She fed her, sheltered her, raised her through infancy and early childhood. The protagonist's first years were shaped by Sorain's care. Whatever came later, the foundation of the protagonist's survival is Sorain's choice to carry a human baby out of a war.

She was young -- barely an adult by elven standards, trying to raise a child of a species she did not fully understand. Human babies grow fast. They change every month. They need constant attention in ways that elf children, who develop over decades, do not. Sorain experienced parenthood at human speed before she was ready for it at elven speed.

---

## The Shift

Sorain had her own biological child. A full-blooded elf.

Raising the human baby had made both siblings want families of their own sooner than any elf normally would. Watching a child grow at human speed -- urgent, visible, every year different -- accelerated the instinct. Sorain found a partner and had her child early by elven standards.

When her biological child was born, something changed. Not overnight. Not cruelly or dramatically. But steadily, visibly, and irreversibly.

The biological child was **fully elf**. Same species, same lifespan, same magical potential, same timeline. Sorain could raise this child on elven time -- decades of patience, centuries of shared life. The biological child would be with her for a thousand years.

The protagonist would be with her for maybe eighty.

Sorain did not decide to stop loving the protagonist. She did not wake up one morning and choose cruelty. What happened was quieter and more honest than that: she had a child who was hers in every way -- blood, species, future -- and the gap between that bond and what she felt for the adopted human girl became impossible to ignore.

The protagonist faded into the background of Sorain's attention. Not abandoned. Not mistreated in any obvious way. Just... deprioritized. Second. The sister saw the adopted baby increasingly as something temporary -- a brief chapter in a thousand-year life. More of a pet than an actual daughter. The affection was still there, but the investment was not. Sorain was raising her biological child for a shared millennium. She was housing the protagonist until the protagonist's short human life ran its course.

---

## Who She Is

Sorain is not a villain. She is not cruel. She is an honest answer to a difficult question: what happens when compassion is tested by time?

She saved a baby because she could not walk away. She raised that baby with real love. And then she had a child of her own species, and the biological reality of what she was -- an elf, a thousand-year being -- reasserted itself. The human child was temporary. Her elf child was permanent. The math was cold, but Sorain did not experience it as a calculation. She experienced it as a feeling: one child felt like her future, and the other felt like a guest.

She is not hostile to the protagonist. She does not insult her or exclude her from the household. She simply stopped investing. The attention, the focus, the energy that a parent pours into a child they believe in -- Sorain redirected all of it to her biological child. What the protagonist received was maintenance, not devotion.

Sorain is capable of warmth. She showed it when she rescued the baby. She showed it in the early years. She is not empty or cold by nature. She is an elf who made a compassionate choice and then discovered, over years, that her compassion had limits she did not know about until they were tested.

---

## Relationship with Sirus

Sorain and Sirus do not agree about the protagonist, and they do not fight about it.

Sirus sees what Sorain does to the protagonist -- the fading attention, the deprioritization, the slow withdrawal of real parental investment. He does not confront her. He does not lecture her. He does not make it a war between siblings. He simply becomes the counterweight. Where Sorain withdraws, Sirus doubles down. Where Sorain stops seeing the protagonist as a real daughter, Sirus starts.

Whether Sorain notices this -- whether she sees her brother quietly filling the role she vacated -- is unclear. If she does, she does not comment on it. The siblings coexist around the protagonist without ever directly addressing the elephant in the room: one of them rescued a baby and stopped raising her, and the other argued to leave the baby behind and never stopped.

---

## Relationship with the Protagonist

The protagonist understands what happened. She is not a child anymore. She knows Sorain loved her once and stopped -- not out of cruelty, but out of something worse: honest limitation. Sorain reached the edge of what her compassion could sustain and went no further.

The protagonist does not hate Sorain. She does not resent her the way she might resent someone who was deliberately cruel. What she feels is something more complicated: the knowledge that the person who saved her life would not do it again if given the choice today. Not because Sorain is evil, but because Sorain now knows what it costs, and the cost is more than she is willing to pay twice.

Sirus is the protagonist's real parent. Sorain is the person who made that possible by making a choice she could not sustain.

## Love Life -- The Traveler

The siblings did not reach the elf-controlled nation alone. A **male elf friend** helped them travel there. He was a traveler by profession -- always moving, always somewhere else -- and he provided the connections and guidance that got two young elves and a human baby safely into elven society.

Over the course of **five years**, he fell in love with Sorain. He resisted it at first -- at **year four** he was still against the idea, not willing to let the relationship become what it was becoming. Half a year later, he gave in. By the time the protagonist was **four and a half years old**, Sorain and the traveler were together.

Their love is **pure**. He loves Sorain as much as she loves him. They are soulmates in the truest sense -- two people who found each other through circumstance and chose each other completely. Unlike Sirus's compromised, transactional love life, Sorain's relationship is clean and genuine. Whatever her failings as a mother to the protagonist, her partnership is not one of them.

His relationship with the protagonist is **minimal**. He is polite and nice to her -- not cold, not hostile, not dismissive. Just not invested. She is Sorain's adopted child from before he arrived, and he treats her with the courtesy you give someone in your household who is not quite yours. He does not need a full profile -- he is a good man who loves Sorain and is not particularly relevant to the protagonist's story beyond being the father of the elf child who replaced her.

This is the partner whose biological child eventually displaced the protagonist in Sorain's attention. He was not the cause of the shift -- Sorain's withdrawal from the protagonist would have happened regardless. But his presence gave Sorain the family structure she needed to have the elf child that changed everything.
