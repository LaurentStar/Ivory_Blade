## Appearance

Nassor is big. Not imposing in the way a soldier is imposing -- big in the way a piece of architecture is big. The humanoid half of her sits atop a serpent body that coils rather than stands, which puts her head around eight feet up when she bothers to rear, and leaves a great deal of her trailing behind wherever she goes.

Her head is reptilian: a long snout, a mouth full of teeth, horns capped in worked gold. White feathered plumes burst from her shoulders and recur down the length of her tail. Her scales are grey-green and matte. Everything else about her is loud -- purple and green silks, gold filigree, a heavy ornamented belt, jewelry on both arms.

She is dressed like someone who expects to be received. Her default posture is declamatory: one hand raised, index finger up, mid-point. She holds the Ram Dao one-handed and overhead with no apparent effort, which is the first and clearest signal that the self-image does not match the body.

**She is constantly mistaken for male.** This happens to her everywhere, it has happened to her for her entire life, and it is a genuine wound rather than a joke she is in on. In Game 1 it pays off at the ending, where Indigo -- who assumed wrong in Chapter 2 and never thought about it again -- works it out mid-conversation.

## The Kingdom and the Goal

Nassor comes from a kingdom with one law that matters: **create a trend, and you become King or Queen.** Fashion, art, manner, anything. You hold the throne until someone else creates the next trend and displaces you. The reign can last a decade or an afternoon.

The length does not matter. What matters is that **every monarch is written into the history of the kingdom permanently**, no matter how briefly they held the crown. That record is the entire prize, and it is Nassor's only goal.

To get it she left on a journey to find **two legendary swords** said to make everything beautiful beyond belief. With those, the trend is guaranteed and the throne follows.

### Where the kingdom is

**Not established. Do not place it.** It belongs to a realm that has not been written yet, and nothing in Game 1 requires knowing where. She is simply very far from home and bad at admitting it.

The one fixed point: **Kat has heard of it.** At the Ch4 ending Kat can place the kingdom by name, which is what turns Nassor from an infiltrator into a person with an address. Kat is canonically the crew's intelligence source, so this costs nothing and does not require the realm to exist on the page.

### The swords are not here

Her information was wrong. She heard the swords were somewhere around the Swahili Realm and they are not, and they never were. There is no conspiracy, no planted lie, nobody misled her -- it is bad intel and she acted on it immediately because she always does.

**This is deliberately disconnected from the demon's deception of Indigo.** Both women crossed a realm chasing something that was not there. Indigo's was a lie told to her on purpose; Nassor's was a rumour she did not check. The rhyme is the whole point and it is ruined if the two are linked. **Nothing about the swords connects to the ancient structure, the archive, or the seal.**

## Personality

**The gap is the character.** Nassor's self-image and Nassor are two different people, and she has no idea. Hold both in every scene:

| She believes she is | She is |
|---|---|
| Delicate | Eight feet of muscle that shrugs off things that would kill a human |
| A stylish mage whose power is refined | Enormously powerful and unable to control it |
| A fair maiden | Read as male by nearly everyone she meets |
| Gracious, above it all | Egocentric, spiteful, and keeping score |

**Petty.** This is her most-used register and the one that drives her Game 1 appearances. She performs outrage she does not feel over harm she did not suffer, and she will carry the resulting grudge for months. The Chapter 2 encounter runs entirely on this: she is never in any danger at any point, and she is furious about it for four chapters.

**Vengeful, spiteful, elitist.** She shows no real interest in other people and uses them as suits her. When something bad happens to someone who has wronged her, she enjoys it openly and does not help.

**Aristocratic, with a sharp tongue and the charisma to back it up.** She is genuinely good at talking. The grandeur is not hollow -- it works on people, and in Chapter 2 it is literally what saves her life, because Indigo listens to thirty seconds of it and reclassifies her from threat to nuisance.

**Short temper.** Her plans fail because she makes hasty decisions, not because she lacks the ability to execute them. She is her own worst enemy and the story should keep proving it.

**Not actually evil.** She can behave nobly, especially when there is something in it for her or when her survival depends on it. Both conditions are met at the Ch4 ending, which is why she pleads before she lectures.

**Determined.** Underneath everything, she is entirely capable of achieving what she set out to do. The goal is ridiculous. Her ability to pursue it is not.

### Who she respects

Only three categories: **royalty, important people, and lizards.** Nobody on the crew qualifies for the first two as far as she can tell.

**The lizard reverence has no outlet in Game 1.** The only chapter with a dragon in it is one she spends in a cargo hold. Recorded here for a later game. Note also that she is reptilian herself, so the trait reads less as reverence and more as self-regard -- play it that way if it ever comes up.

### Dancing

She is a genuinely excellent dancer. This is one of the few things she is right about herself. No outlet in Game 1.

## Power

**She is extremely strong and the story never says so.**

Her power is established exactly once, in Chapter 2 Level 3, and entirely by implication: the player fights the cave animals themselves with real exertion in the same scene, and the same animals are climbing all over Nassor and doing nothing whatsoever to her. The player's own effort is the measuring instrument. She never comments on it and never appears to notice.

**She is never measured against the power ladder.** She is never put opposite huskies, wolves, tigers, the guardian, or the demon, and no character ever ranks her. She is hidden for every fight in the game that matters. This is a hard constraint: the moment she wins something, the Ch4 L4 thesis that weak people working together can beat one powerful person alone takes damage.

### Magic

Powerful and **uncontrolled**. She cannot reliably aim it or scale it. In Chapter 2 this gets one demonstration -- a spell that goes off wrong and removes a significant piece of cave wall -- which establishes extreme power and zero control in a single beat, so the audience laughs at her and is wary of her at the same time.

**Her magic needs no in-world excuse.** The Swahili Realm is an Anomaly Realm where magic may behave unexpectedly, and the Mirralata System penalizes beings who cross Universal Energy tiers. Both already apply to her.

### The Ram Dao

A large, heavy, ornate curved blade, carried openly and handled one-handed. She never uses it on anyone in Game 1. Its only function in this game is to make her read as armed at the Ch4 ending, which is part of why Ys and Kat attack on sight.

## Her Role in Game 1

She appears in four places and wins nothing.

- **Ch2 L3 -- the encounter.** Indigo rounds the last bend expecting the hidden ship and finds Nassor coiled at the sealed hatch, pounding on it and berating Robert through the hull for cowardice, with cave animals all over her doing nothing. Indigo clears the animals, nearly runs her through, listens to her talk, downgrades her to an unrelated nuisance, and tells her to leave. Nassor walks in behind her.
- **Ch2 L3 -- the ejection.** She comments on the volcanic activity from behind Indigo and Robert on the bridge. Hard cut: Indigo throws her bodily off the ship. She scolds them both for leaving her to burn alive, and the scene moves on. **She gets back on entirely off screen and the player never sees it.**
- **Ch3 2.4 -- the punchline.** One shot of her watching the tiger captain choke Robert, delighted, declining to help. This is where the player learns she is still aboard. The crew still does not know.
- **Ch4 End.8 -- found.** Ys and Kat sweep the ship for the first time, find her, and attack on sight. She is pinned, has a shotgun put to her head, pleads, lectures, insults Robert, and is ultimately granted conditional passage by Ys.

Between those she is in the cargo hold, doing nothing, for thirteen levels.

### Why nobody finds her

Not luck. **Ys and Kat are the only two who could have found her and neither is aboard** -- their shared awareness of all sentient beings on the ship is established in three separate profiles, and it is precisely why both have to stay off it until the ending. Kat, the detection specialist, is coordinating remotely, then captured, then outside, then injured. Brod is in a separate tunnel system and then at the fortress. Indigo is underground alone. Robert lives aboard and is nearly magicless, so she finds physical evidence and nothing else -- and says nothing about it.

### Why she never forced the hatch

It did not occur to her. **Being admitted is the entire point.** Cutting the door open would concede that she was not owed entry in the first place.

## Key Relationships

### Robert Poole

The only real relationship she has in Game 1, and it is entirely one-sided.

Robert sealed the hull against the cave animals -- correctly; they would have killed her -- and refused to open it for a stranger she had never seen. Nassor has treated this as a grave personal injury ever since, despite never having been in a moment's danger. She calls Robert a coward through the door in Chapter 2, blames her for the lava after the ejection, gloats when the tiger captain chokes her in Chapter 3, and runs her down again at the ending.

**Neither of them is wrong and that is the joke.** The animals were lethal to Robert and nothing to Nassor, simultaneously.

At the ending she does not address Robert directly. She disparages her to someone else's face while Robert is standing there -- and Robert, who does not argue, simply starts opening the airlock faster.

Her term for Robert is **"boney human."** It is a snob's insult, aimed at a woman canon describes as slender and lanky, and it establishes Nassor as species-ist rather than cruel.

### IndigoLLiy

Threw her off the ship. Nassor does not appear to hold this against her with anything like the energy she reserves for Robert, which is itself characterisation -- Indigo is strong and decisive, and Nassor's contempt does not attach to people like that.

Indigo registers her as an irrelevance twice, which is worse than being hated. The height gap does comedy work in both scenes: Indigo is 4'3", almost exactly half Nassor's height, and entirely unimpressed.

### Captain Ys

Puts a shotgun to her head and then decides she can stay. His ruling is narrow and deliberate: **not malicious.** He offers no opinion on how dangerous she is, because that is not the question in front of him and not something he can verify.

The passage he grants is temporary -- until the ship can set down somewhere in the Swahili Realm outside the civil war for repairs. It is the identical arrangement Robert already has, and it will fail the identical way, because the crew keeps nearly dying before they can drop anyone anywhere.

### Kat Arlean Mori

Pins her, prepares to space her, and is perfectly matter-of-fact about both. Kat does not know her, does not find her interesting, and treats removing an intruder as routine work.

Kat is also the one who places her kingdom, which is what gets her out of the airlock.

### Brod Tsumi

**They have never met.** Brod is off-ship for the entire game and does not recognise her at the ending. If she ever shares a frame with Sketch or Tablet, the existing rules hold without exception: Sketch is a book and speaks only to Brod, and Tablet reads as equipment.

## Delivery and Voice Direction

**Beep signature:** loud, over-articulated, metrically regular. The rhythm of someone announcing themselves. Per the beep system, rhythm carries personality -- hers should sound like a herald who was not asked to speak.

**No `[VOICED]` lines in Game 1.** Voiced lines are reserved for peak moments and she does not get one. The whole point is that she is never important enough for the story to stop.

**Internal monologue is restricted.** `[INTERNAL]` is scoped to the protagonist in [Script_Style_Methods.md](../../Story/Script_Style_Methods.md). Nassor's single internal line in Ch3 2.4 is a deliberate extension of the category, and it is contained by one rule: **she only gets internal monologue in parallel cutscenes where Indigo is absent.** If Indigo is in the scene, Nassor does not think out loud.

**Register:** grandiose at all volumes. She does not shout to be heard, she declaims. The raised index finger from the reference art is her default gesture and should recur.

## What Makes Her Important

Nothing, and that is the design. She contributes nothing to the plot of Game 1, solves nothing, saves nobody, and is physically the strongest thing on the ship. Every appearance is a joke at her expense, and the audience is given exactly enough evidence to understand that the joke could stop being funny at any time.

She also does something for Robert that no other character can. The cowardice accusation is unfair, it is delivered by someone who was never in danger, and it lands on a fear Robert already has about herself. It gives the quietest crew member a four-chapter spine that costs the story almost nothing.

## Open Questions

1. **Age** -- Unknown. Does her species age differently?
2. **The kingdom's realm** -- Deliberately unplaced. Which realm, and what is its Universal Energy tier?
3. **The two swords** -- Do they exist at all? Where are they actually? Is the rumour she chased traceable to anyone?
4. **How she got to the Swahili Realm** -- She crossed realms alone during a period when travel is officially discouraged. By what means, and does she still have it?
5. **What she ate for thirteen levels** -- Partly answered by Robert's missing-supplies seeds. Worth deciding how much of the ship's stores she went through.
6. **Her magic's failure mode** -- Is the lack of control a training problem, a species trait, or a consequence of crossing Universal Energy tiers?
7. **The lizard reverence** -- No outlet in Game 1. Does it ever become plot-relevant, and does she extend it to dragons?
8. **After Game 1** -- The passage Ys granted expires at the next safe landing. What keeps her aboard after that?
