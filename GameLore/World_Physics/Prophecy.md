# The Prophecy

> *The prophecy is real. It happens. What comes after is fair game.*

---

## Overview

| Field | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | A chosen child who will overcome all challenges, travel to the center of the Mirralata System, and activate a special event |
| **Status** | Real. The prophecy will be fulfilled. |
| **The Chosen Child** | Not the protagonist. A separate, unknown individual. |
| **The Protagonist's Role** | Unwitting pawn of a rabbit god. Her journey exists to slow the chosen child's progress and position her to defy the new order after fulfillment. |

---

## What the Prophecy Says

A chosen child will:

1. **Overcome all challenges** placed before them across the realms
2. **Travel to the center of the Mirralata System**
3. **Activate a special event** upon arrival

The prophecy does not specify what the special event is in terms that outsiders can easily interpret. What matters is the sequence: challenges overcome, center reached, event triggered. The prophecy is a roadmap, and the chosen child is the one meant to walk it.

---

## What Everyone Believes

Captain Ys, the two armies in the Swahili Realm, and anyone else who has heard the prophecy believe the protagonist is the chosen child. She matches enough of the criteria -- or appears to -- that the assumption sticks. She is young, she is on a journey toward the Mirralata System's center, and she is overcoming challenges that would kill most people.

The protagonist herself does not believe she is the prophecy child. She does not care about the prophecy. She is traveling to the center to save Kim Tiernan's soul. Her motivation has nothing to do with fulfilling ancient predictions.

**She is correct to be skeptical. She is not the chosen child.**

---

## The Real Chosen Child

The actual chosen child of the prophecy is a separate individual. Their identity, their location, and their own journey have not been established in detail. What matters is the relationship between their journey and the protagonist's.

The protagonist's adventures across the realms -- her fights, her escapes, her survival -- have a specific unintended consequence: **they make the path harder for the real chosen child.**

### How the Protagonist Slows the Chosen One

The protagonist does not know she is doing this. She is not sabotaging anyone on purpose. She is simply surviving, fighting, and moving forward on her own mission. But the ripple effects of her journey compound:

- **The Wolf Squad survived their encounter with the protagonist.** They were defeated and humiliated, but they lived. That experience made them more prepared, more cautious, and more dangerous. When the real chosen child eventually faces them, the wolves are not the unit they would have been without the protagonist's interference. They are harder. They have learned. The chosen child defeats them, but it takes longer.

- **The Tiger Unit survived.** The tiger captain took a laser and lived. He is critically injured, furious, and obsessed. When the real chosen child crosses his path, the tiger captain is not an unknown quantity -- he is a wounded predator with experience fighting someone who used indirect tactics. He adapts. The chosen child defeats him too, but again, it takes longer.

- **Every enemy the protagonist fights and does not kill becomes a harder enemy for the next person.** Her cleverness -- the same trait that defines her and keeps her alive -- leaves behind survivors who learn from the experience. She is training the obstacles that the chosen child will face.

The cumulative effect: the chosen child's journey to the center of the Mirralata System is **significantly delayed** by the protagonist's prior passage through it. Not blocked. Not stopped. Delayed. The prophecy still happens. The chosen child still arrives. But they arrive later than they would have without the protagonist's interference.

---

## The Rabbit God

Behind the protagonist's journey is a **rabbit god** -- an entity that selected her for a specific purpose.

The protagonist does not know she is working for the rabbit god. She believes she is on a personal mission to save Kim. Every decision she makes is her own. Her motivations are genuine. Her suffering is real. But the trajectory of her life -- the encounters she stumbles into, the path that keeps aligning just well enough to keep her moving -- has been nudged by something that wants her exactly where she is.

The rabbit god chose the protagonist because of her **selfish antics** -- her single-minded focus on saving Kim, her refusal to care about prophecies or wars or anything that is not her immediate goal. A selfless hero would have stopped to help. A selfless hero might have resolved conflicts cleanly, killed threats permanently, or stayed to fix problems. The protagonist does none of that. She fights to survive, takes what she needs, leaves chaos in her wake, and moves on. That selfishness is the tool.

### The Rabbit God's Plan

The plan has two phases:

**Phase 1: Slow the chosen child.**
The protagonist's journey through the realms leaves behind harder, more prepared enemies. The chosen child is delayed. The prophecy still happens, but later. This delay is the point. Every day the chosen child is slowed is a day the rabbit god uses for something else -- preparation, positioning, accumulating advantage.

**Phase 2: Defy the new order.**
The prophecy is fulfilled. The chosen child reaches the center, activates the event, and the new order is established -- whatever that looks like. The prophecy is technically complete.

But what comes after the prophecy is not covered by the prophecy.

The rabbit god has set the protagonist up to **defy the new order** immediately after it is established. The protagonist -- battle-hardened, clever, driven by her own unresolved mission, carrying the weight of everything she has survived -- is positioned to challenge whatever the prophecy's fulfillment creates. She is the chaos that follows the conclusion. The wrench thrown into the machine the moment it starts running.

The prophecy technically happens. The chosen child does what they were meant to do. And then the rabbit god is free to manipulate the aftermath as it pleases, with the protagonist as its instrument of defiance.

---

## The Rabbit God vs. The Rabbit Girl

These are **not the same entity** and should not be confused.

The **rabbit god** is an actual rabbit -- a divine or god-tier being that happens to be a rabbit. It is the architect of the protagonist's manipulation, the entity that selected her to slow the chosen child and defy the new order.

The **rabbit girl** on Captain Ys's crew is an **alien** with a rabbit-like anthropomorphic appearance. She is not the rabbit god. She is not an agent of the rabbit god. She is an alien woman who looks rabbit-like and happens to be Ys's hidden partner. The visual similarity is coincidence, not connection.

The rabbit girl knows the protagonist is meant to be a sacrifice because Ys told her -- not because she has divine knowledge. Her warmth toward the protagonist comes from her own character, not from a god's agenda.

---

## What This Means for the Series

The protagonist's 10-game journey toward the center of the Mirralata System is real. Her motivation -- saving Kim -- is real. Her struggles are real. But the meta-narrative running beneath everything is that she was chosen not to fulfill the prophecy but to **obstruct it just enough** and then **break what it builds.**

She is not the hero of the prophecy. She is the aftermath.

## Open Questions

1. **The rabbit god** -- What is it? Where does it come from? What does it want with the new order? Why does it need the protagonist specifically?
2. **The real chosen child** -- Who are they? Do they appear in the game series? Does the protagonist ever meet them?
3. **The special event** -- What does the prophecy's activation actually do? What is the new order that is established?
4. **The rabbit girl** -- What is her connection to the rabbit god? Is she aware of the full plan, only the sacrifice, or something else entirely?
5. **Kim's sacrifice** -- Was Kim's soul sacrifice a natural event, or did the rabbit god engineer it to give the protagonist a reason to travel to the center?
6. **Captain Ys** -- Does Ys know about the rabbit god? Does he believe in the prophecy sincerely, or is he also a piece on the board?
7. **The protagonist's awareness** -- Does she ever learn she is being used? If so, when, and what does she do about it?
8. **Defying the order** -- What does defiance look like? Is the protagonist meant to destroy what the prophecy creates, corrupt it, redirect it, or simply create enough chaos for the rabbit god to act?
