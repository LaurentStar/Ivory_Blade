# Mecha Suits

> *When magic fails and Universal Energy dries up, you summon something that doesn't need either.*

---

## What They Are

Mecha suits are mechanical constructs that a user can summon to bridge the gap between what their body can do and what a realm demands. They are force multipliers -- weapons, armor, and mobility platforms compressed into a deployable form.

Mechas are not magic and they are not powered by Universal Energy during operation. The summoning itself costs stored magic, but once deployed, a mecha operates on its own construction. This is what makes them valuable in low realms where both magic and UE are scarce: you spend the magic to bring it in, and then the mecha handles the rest on its own mechanical merits.

---

## How Summoning Works

A user saves up and stores enough magic to perform one critical action: the summoning. In a low realm where casting is otherwise impractical, this stored reserve represents a significant investment -- potentially all the magical energy a user can accumulate over an extended period.

The summoning is the expensive part. Once the mecha materializes, it does not require ongoing magical input to function. It runs on its construction, its materials, and whatever power systems were built into it.

This means the decision to summon is a commitment. The magic spent on summoning is gone. If the mecha is destroyed or the situation changes, that reserve cannot be recovered quickly.

---

## Construction Determines Realm Viability

Not all mechas are equal, and the most important variable is not raw power -- it is where the mecha can survive.

### Sturdy, Robust Mechas

Built for durability over performance. These mechas prioritize structural integrity using materials and engineering that do not depend on Universal Energy or ambient magic to hold together.

- Can survive and perform decently across **multiple realm levels**, including lower ones
- Sacrifice raw power, speed, and flashy capabilities for reliability
- In a low-UE realm, they still function because their construction is self-sufficient
- They are the safe choice -- they will not crumble, but they will not dominate either
- Ideal for uncertain conditions, realm transitions, or prolonged deployments where resummoning is not an option

### Lighter, Faster, More Powerful Mechas

Built for peak performance. These mechas maximize offensive output, speed, and capability by leveraging materials and systems that benefit from or require ambient energy.

- Hit harder, move faster, and outclass sturdy variants in direct combat
- Can only function in **higher-ranking realms** where sufficient ambient energy exists to sustain their advanced construction
- In lower realms, they **crumble apart** -- their materials lose cohesion, their power systems fail, their structural integrity collapses without the energy they were designed to draw from
- They are the aggressive choice -- devastating where they work, useless where they don't

### The Tradeoff

Power vs. versatility. A mecha built to dominate one realm may be scrap metal two realms down. A mecha built to survive anywhere may not be powerful enough to win anywhere.

This is a strategic decision, not a simple upgrade path. The "best" mecha depends entirely on where you are going and what you expect to face.

## Implications for the Protagonist

In the Swahili Realm (mid-tier low UE, Anomaly Realm), the protagonist has enough ambient magic to potentially summon a mecha, but it would represent a significant expenditure of her reserves.

A sturdy mecha would function reliably in the Swahili Realm's conditions. A high-performance mecha might work in some areas but could behave unpredictably due to the anomalous physics -- the strange gravity and broken spatial rules could interact badly with systems designed for standard physics.

The anomaly factor adds an additional layer of uncertainty to the already-existing realm-viability question: even a mecha rated for the correct UE level might malfunction if the physics themselves are unreliable.

## Open Questions

1. Where do mechas come from? Are they crafted by specialists, bought commercially, looted from battlefields, or found as artifacts?
2. Can the protagonist build or modify her own mecha, or does she rely on pre-existing ones?
3. How rare are mechas? Is summoning one a common tactic among capable fighters, or is it unusual?
4. What does a mecha look like in this world? Full-body exosuit, giant robot, something in between?
5. Can magic enhance a mecha after summoning -- buffing its armor, sharpening its weapons, augmenting its systems?
6. How does the stored magic work? Is it held in an internal reserve, a physical container, or an enchanted object?
7. How long does a summoned mecha last? Indefinitely until destroyed, or does it have a deployment timer?
