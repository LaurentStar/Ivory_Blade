## Realm Classification

The Swahili Realm is an **Anomaly Realm** -- a realm where the standard laws of physics do not apply in expected ways. Unlike standard realms where physics merely scale with Universal Energy level, Anomaly Realms introduce unique, localized distortions that defy conventional logic.

The Swahili Realm's anomaly is **gravity.** It does not behave predictably. It shifts between planetoids, reverses without warning in certain zones, and creates navigational hazards that cannot be mapped permanently because they change.

---

## Terrain

The realm is not a planet. It is a vast three-dimensional space filled with **planetoids** -- floating landmasses of varying sizes. Some are large enough to support small cities. Others are barely big enough to stand on.

### Planetoid Structure

- Planetoids range from continent-scale to boulder-scale
- They are scattered through open space at various distances and orientations
- Each planetoid has its own localized gravity -- "down" on one planetoid may be "sideways" or "up" relative to a neighboring one
- The spaces between planetoids are traversable but dangerous -- gravity transitions can be sudden and violent

### Gravity Behavior

- **Localized pull:** Each planetoid generates its own gravitational field. Step off the edge of one and you may fall toward another, float in a dead zone, or be pulled sideways
- **Transition zones:** The boundaries between gravitational fields are unpredictable. Some transitions are smooth. Others are instantaneous -- one step you're standing on solid ground, the next you're falling in a completely different direction
- **Shifting zones:** Some areas of the realm experience gravity changes over time. A corridor that was safe yesterday may pull you into open space today
- **Dead zones:** Pockets of near-zero gravity exist between planetoid clusters. Getting stuck in one without propulsion or a grapple point is potentially fatal

### Environment Types

The planetoids are not uniform. Terrain varies across the realm:

- **Rocky barren planetoids** -- bare stone and mineral surfaces, no vegetation, exposed to the open space between bodies
- **Vegetated planetoids** -- planetoids large enough to sustain ecosystems. Forests, grasslands, and bodies of water exist on the larger ones
- **Urban planetoids** -- settled areas with structures, fortifications, and infrastructure built by the realm's inhabitants. Cities, military installations, and trading posts
- **Debris fields** -- shattered planetoids, wreckage from the war, and natural rock formations that create dense obstacle zones

## Universal Energy Level

The Swahili Realm sits at **mid-tier low** on the Universal Energy scale.

### What This Means for Combat

- **Magic is functional and relevant.** The protagonist can cast, sustain spells, and use magic as a primary combat tool. It is her most versatile option in this realm.
- **Universal Energy is accessible but limited.** The protagonist can draw on UE to supplement her physical abilities -- modest increases to speed, durability, and resilience. She can survive a bullet. She cannot move at superhuman speeds or tank artillery.
- **The protagonist is not superhuman.** She is enhanced. She is durable. But she is still recognizably human in capability. She bleeds. She tires. She can be overwhelmed by numbers or outclassed by a sufficiently powerful opponent.
- **The inhabitants live at this level.** The soldiers, the wolf squad, the tiger unit -- they all operate within the same UE constraints. The wolves are dangerous because of training and equipment, not because they are UE-enhanced demigods. The tigers are more dangerous because they are simply better -- more powerful within the same constraints.

### The Anomaly Factor

As an Anomaly Realm, the Swahili Realm does not follow standard UE-to-magic scaling perfectly. Magic may behave unexpectedly. Spells may interact with the gravitational anomalies in ways that are difficult to predict. The protagonist's magical knowledge (60 subjective years of study) gives her an advantage in adapting, but the realm will surprise her.

## The War

A civil war is being fought in the Swahili Realm between two factions: the **Swahili East Axel Legion** and the **People Swahili Brigade and Electives**. The war predates the protagonist's arrival and will continue after she leaves. She is not here to resolve it.

### The Factions

| Faction | Role in Story |
|---|---|
| **Swahili East Axel Legion** | Primary antagonist faction. Deploys the Husky Squad, Wolf Squad, and Tiger Unit against the protagonist. |
| **People Swahili Brigade and Electives** | Secondary antagonist faction. Converges on the protagonist in Ch3 after a spy within the Legion tips them off. |

### The Conflict

The war is a civil conflict -- both factions are from the Swahili Realm, fighting over their own home.

- Both factions field **human soldiers** and **anthropomorphic creatures** -- the realm's population includes both, and both serve in military structures
- The conflict is serious enough to require dedicated military units, organized command structures, and ongoing campaigns across the planetoid system
- The war creates the conditions for the protagonist's hunt -- military infrastructure, patrols, checkpoints, supply lines, and communication networks all exist and are obstacles for the crew

Full profiles: `GameLore/Groups/SwahiliEastAxelLegion.md` | `GameLore/Groups/PeopleSwahiliBrigade.md`

### The Prophecy

Both armies believe in a prophecy: a figure who can end the war in decisive victory for whichever side claims them. Both armies believe the protagonist is this figure.

Whether the prophecy is real, what it actually says, where it originates, and why both sides believe the protagonist fulfills it are open questions. What matters for the story is the consequence: she is hunted by everyone.

## Inhabitants

The Swahili Realm is populated and civilized. It is not a wasteland -- it is a place where people live, work, and are currently killing each other over a war the protagonist has no stake in.

### Humans

Present as regular inhabitants and soldiers. They fill the rank-and-file positions in both armies -- standard infantry, logistics, support roles. In the wolf squad's structure, human soldiers report to the wolf elites and carry out supporting missions.

### Anthropomorphic Creatures

Humanoid beings with animal characteristics. They are people, not monsters. They hold positions in society and military hierarchies, often at elite or command levels.

Known types in this realm:

| Type | Role |
|---|---|
| **Wolf anthropomorphs** | Elite military unit (Wolf Squad). Hunter-killer specialists. Gun-equipped. Command human soldiers. |
| **Tiger anthropomorphs** | Elite military unit (Tiger Unit). Far more dangerous than the wolves. Dispatched only when conventional elites fail. |

Other anthropomorphic species likely exist in the realm's population but have not been established.

### Coexistence

Humans and anthropomorphic creatures coexist within the realm's civilizations. The military hierarchies are mixed -- anthropomorphic elites commanding human troops is an established pattern, not an exception.

## Strategic Significance for the Crew

The Swahili Realm is not a destination. It is an obstacle. The crew needs to pass through it to continue toward the Mirralata System's center via the Apotheosis Track.

### Navigation Challenges

- The anomalous gravity makes standard navigation unreliable
- The war creates military zones, patrols, and restricted areas that must be avoided
- The crew's ship must be hidden or secured -- it is their only way out
- The planetoid terrain provides cover but also creates ambush opportunities for both the crew and their hunters

### Combat Terrain

The gravity anomalies define how fights play in this realm:

- **Wall running and surface transitions** become critical movement skills -- the protagonist's ability to move across surfaces that would be walls or ceilings in a normal realm is a core advantage
- **Verticality is constant.** Fights happen across multiple gravitational orientations. An enemy above you might not be above you for long.
- **Environmental hazards are everywhere.** Gravity dead zones, transition boundaries, unstable surfaces, and open space gaps between planetoids are all potential weapons or traps
- **Ranged combat is complicated.** Bullets and projectiles are affected by gravitational transitions. A straight shot may curve or accelerate unpredictably crossing between gravitational fields.
