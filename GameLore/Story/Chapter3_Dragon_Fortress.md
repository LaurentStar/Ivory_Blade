# Chapter 3: Dragon Fortress

> *The storm keeps everyone grounded. Everyone except the girl with the plan.*

---

## Context

Chapter 2 ended with the protagonist defeating and capturing the Wolf Squad leader in a storm. She reached the ship at the top of the giant tower with the mechanic. The rest of the crew -- Ys, Kat, Brod, and the mouse woman -- are still captured by the Wolf Squad on the frozen lake.

A **powerful and deadly storm** has rolled in across the region. This is not the wind and lightning from the boss fight -- this is worse. A full-scale storm that forces everyone to take shelter. The general army and the Wolf Squad cannot operate in it. They hunker down.

The **Tiger Unit** is still en route. The storm does not stop them. They are coming regardless.

The protagonist has the captured wolf leader, the ship, the mechanic, and a narrow window created by the storm. She needs a plan to free the crew before the storm passes and the Tiger Unit arrives.

## The Plan

### Secret Contact with Kat

The protagonist is able to **secretly contact Kat** via magic signals. Kat is captured but not helpless -- she has been gathering information while in custody.

Kat recovered **records** about the local area while captured. Among them: there is a **floating military fortress** nearby. It was abandoned by the army because a **dangerous dragon** made a nest there. The dragon is territorial, aggressive, and powerful enough that the military gave up the fortress rather than fight it. The fortress still floats, still has infrastructure, and the dragon still lives in it.

### The Bargaining Problem

The protagonist wants to use the captured wolf leader as a **bargaining chip** to free the crew. She has the leverage. But she cannot take on the entire army and the Wolf Squad -- even sheltering from the storm, they outnumber her by orders of magnitude. Walking up and offering a trade means walking into a situation she cannot fight her way out of if the trade goes wrong.

She needs a distraction. Something that forces the army to deal with a bigger problem than her.

### Ys's Suggestion

Captain Ys -- communicating through Kat's signal relay -- suggests **provoking the dragon**. If the dragon attacks the shelter where the army and the Wolf Squad are hunkered down, the resulting chaos creates the opening the protagonist needs. The army fights the dragon. The protagonist frees the crew.

The dragon is not an ally. It is not tamed. It is not controlled. It is a weapon pointed in a direction -- provoked into attacking a specific location. What happens after that is chaos, and chaos is what the protagonist needs.

### Ys Prepares from the Inside

While the protagonist climbs the fortress, Ys acts from within captivity. He **shape-shifts** -- using his ghost-like nature to slip free without the wolves noticing -- and quietly **unties Brod and the mouse woman**.

He does not free everyone. He does not start a fight. He instructs them to **draw magic minions** and stay hidden until the time is right. This is preparation, not escape.

**Brod** draws combat-ready creatures -- quick, functional, ready to deploy when chaos hits.

**The mouse woman** draws **soldiers that look nearly identical to the real army troops** -- convincing copies, except they are loyal to their creator. When the moment comes, these fake soldiers will blend into the chaos, follow orders from the crew, and create confusion about who is on which side.

Ys tells them to wait. The dragon has not been provoked yet. The protagonist has not reached the fortress yet. When the plan triggers, they need to be ready to move instantly. Until then -- stay hidden, stay quiet, keep drawing.

## Levels

### Level 1: The Climb

The storm makes flying impossible. The mechanic cannot take the ship to the floating fortress -- the winds, the lightning, and the visibility are too dangerous. The ship stays grounded at the tower.

The protagonist has to reach the fortress on foot. She climbs from the **lower underside** of the floating structure -- approaching from below, scaling the exterior, working her way up through the fortress's underbelly.

The environment is **white and foggy**. Visibility is short. The fortress looms above her, half-swallowed by cloud and storm haze. She cannot see the top. She cannot see the ground below. She climbs blind, feeling for handholds, navigating architecture that was built to keep people out from exactly this direction.

The underside of the fortress is industrial -- exposed supports, maintenance hatches, structural ribs, rusted scaffolding. This is not the part of the fortress anyone was meant to see. It is cold, wet, and the wind pulls at her constantly.

The captured wolf leader is secured at the ship. The mechanic is with the ship. The protagonist is alone on the climb. Kim is in the bottle.

**Gameplay establishes:** Vertical traversal, climbing mechanics, navigation in low visibility. The fortress introduced from below -- the player sees its scale and construction before seeing its interior. The storm as an environmental obstacle, not just a backdrop.

### Level 2: Inside the Fortress

The protagonist breaches the fortress interior. What she finds is not a military installation anymore.

**Strange trees** have grown through the complex. They are massive -- **coiling giants that resemble redwoods**, but they do not grow straight. They spiral and wrap around the fortress's structures, threading through hallways, coiling around pillars, punching through floors and ceilings. The trees have taken the architecture and made it theirs. Metal walls are cracked apart by roots. Walkways are split and tilted. Entire sections of the fortress are held together by the trees as much as by the original construction.

The structure is **badly damaged**. Floors are broken, collapsed, or angled wrong. Gaps open into empty air below. The fortress was abandoned for a reason, and the dragon's presence and the tree growth have made it worse. Traversal requires good footwork -- the protagonist needs her agility, her wall-running, her spatial awareness to navigate a space that was built for soldiers walking on flat ground and is now a maze of broken metal and coiling wood.

The trees create a strange atmosphere. They are alive, growing, red-barked and enormous in a place that should be sterile military architecture. The fortress feels less like an abandoned base and more like something that was reclaimed by nature -- or by whatever these trees are.

The dragon is somewhere deeper in the fortress. The protagonist is getting closer.

### Parallel -- The Tiger Unit Arrives

While the protagonist navigates the fortress interior, the **Tiger Unit** reaches the Swahili Realm.

The Tiger Unit is able to **locate its subordinate units** -- they track the Wolf Squad's position and status. They know the wolf leader is missing. They know he was last reported with the prophecy child. They head straight to the **ship's hidden location**.

They find the **mechanic** and the **wolf leader tied up**.

The **Tiger Unit captain** grabs the mechanic by the neck and **threatens her life** to force her to reveal the protagonist's location. The mechanic is terrified -- she has already been through the cave animals, the volcanic activity, and being left alone. Now the most dangerous unit in the army has her by the throat.

The tiger captain **unties the wolf leader** and **scolds him**. The Wolf Squad -- the army's elite hunter-killer unit -- was beaten by a teenage girl. Their youngest member is dead. Their leader was captured and tied up at a ship. The tiger captain does not hide his contempt.

The wolf leader is **visibly upset** but backs down. He does not challenge the tiger captain. The hierarchy is clear. The wolves answer to the tigers when the tigers are deployed, and the tigers have been deployed because the wolves failed.

The protagonist does not know any of this is happening. She is inside the fortress, climbing through coiling trees, getting closer to the dragon. Her ship, her hostage, and the mechanic are now in the Tiger Unit's hands.

**Gameplay establishes:** Interior traversal in a destroyed environment. Platforming through broken architecture wrapped in coiling trees. The fortress as a space that tells its own story -- military order overtaken by something organic and strange. The dragon's territory established through environmental detail before the creature itself appears. The Tiger Unit's arrival as a dramatic escalation the player witnesses but the protagonist does not -- the situation at the ship has changed completely while she is away.

### Level 3: Dragon Nest

The protagonist reaches the heart of the fortress. The dragon is here.

It is **sleeping**, curled around an **egg**. The nest is built from torn metal, uprooted tree wood, and debris from the fortress itself. The dragon is massive -- the kind of creature the military abandoned an entire floating fortress to avoid fighting. And it has something to protect.

The protagonist contacts Kat to discuss the finer details of the plan. The egg is the key. Steal the egg, lead the dragon toward the army's shelter, and the chaos begins. The dragon will follow its egg anywhere. The plan is set.

### Something Is Wrong

The protagonist tries to contact the mechanic at the ship. No response. The signal is dead.

She is high up -- the top of a floating fortress above the storm clouds. She has line of sight. She uses magic to **summon a pair of binoculars** and looks down toward the ship's location.

She sees the **Tiger Unit**. They have a **powerful airship** -- a military vessel built to handle this storm. It is already airborne and flying **straight toward the fortress**. Straight toward her.

A **marksman on the airship** fires. The shot nearly takes her head off. She ducks. The Tiger Unit knows where she is.

### Panic and Decision

The protagonist contacts Kat in a panic. The Tiger Unit is here. They have the ship. They have the mechanic. They are coming for her right now. What does she do?

**Ys** overhears the situation through Kat's relay. He makes the call instantly. The dragon ambush needs to happen **now** -- not as a planned operation, but as an emergency. He commands the protagonist to **steal the egg immediately** and lead the dragon toward the shelter where the crew is being held.

Ys also **activates the crew's ship remotely from afar** -- calling it to him. The ship responds. The mechanic is not piloting it. Ys is pulling it toward the crew's position through whatever method his nature allows.

### The Chase

The protagonist grabs the egg. The dragon wakes up.

What follows is a **chase sequence** through and off the floating fortress. The protagonist is running with a dragon egg while being pursued by:

- **The dragon** -- enraged, enormous, tearing through its own fortress to get its egg back
- **The Tiger Unit's airship** -- firing on her from the air, the marksman tracking her through the storm

She has to dodge both. The dragon's fury and the Tiger Unit's firepower are not coordinated -- they are two independent threats that do not care about each other. The dragon will attack anything between it and the egg, including the Tiger Unit's ship. The Tiger Unit will shoot at the protagonist regardless of the dragon.

The protagonist needs to survive the chase, keep the egg, and lead the dragon toward the army's shelter below. If she can get the dragon pointed in the right direction, Ys's plan works. If she drops the egg or gets killed, everyone dies.

**Gameplay establishes:** The dragon as a force of nature, not a boss to fight. The egg as a held objective the player must protect while running. The Tiger Unit's airship as a new persistent threat. Chase sequence with dual threats -- dodge the dragon, dodge the gunfire. Ys's ability to act remotely (activating the ship) as a display of his mysterious capabilities.

### Level 4: Eye of the Storm

The protagonist makes it to the shelter with the dragon egg. She has led the dragon here.

The weather reports are deceptive -- the skies above the shelter are **calm**. The storm has not passed. They are in the **eye of a hurricane**. It will get much worse when the wall hits.

The dragon **rains down on the shelter**, attacking everything and everyone. It does not discriminate. Soldiers, structures, vehicles -- the dragon wants its egg and it will destroy anything in its path. The army scrambles to respond to a threat they abandoned a fortress to avoid.

**Ys gives the signal.** Brod and the mouse woman activate their prepared drawings. Brod's combat creatures deploy into the chaos. The mouse woman's **fake soldiers** -- copies of real army troops, loyal to their creator -- flood the battlefield, blending in with the real forces and sowing confusion about who is on which side.

The crew's ship is flying in on **autopilot**, called by Ys's remote activation. It is heading to their position.

### Ys Rescues the Mechanic

The **mechanic is on the Tiger Unit's airship**. She was taken when the tigers seized the crew's ship.

Ys sets out to get her. He uses his **special move** to bring down the Tiger Unit's airship. The ship goes down -- **none are killed**, but the airship is grounded and cannot fly again. The Tiger Unit is forced to operate on foot.

Ys **maneuvers past the elite tigers** to reach the mechanic. He does not fight them head-on -- he moves through them, around them, using whatever his nature allows. He reaches the mechanic. They make it to the crew's ship as it arrives on autopilot. They reboard. The ship is back in the crew's hands.

### The Second Army

The Tiger Unit had a **spy**. This spy alerted the **opposing army** about the prophecy child's location earlier -- before the dragon assault, before the chaos. Both armies now know where the prophecy child is.

**Two armies converge on the shelter** despite the dragon, despite the storm. The prophecy child is worth more than the risk. Flying away is dangerous -- the hurricane wall is closing in and the airspace is filled with a dragon, military fire, and storm winds.

### The Survival Fight

Everyone is on the ground fighting. The protagonist, the crew, Brod's creatures, the mouse woman's fake soldiers -- all of them are in the middle of a three-way war:

- **The dragon** -- attacking indiscriminately, chasing its egg, destroying everything
- **Army 1** -- the army that deployed the Wolf Squad and Tiger Unit, converging on the shelter
- **Army 2** -- the opposing army, arriving from the other direction, also hunting the prophecy child

The protagonist is not trying to win. She is trying to **survive for a duration** -- hold the ground, keep the crew alive, and wait for the ship to be ready for departure. The crew fights. The drawing-cast minions fight. The dragon fights everyone. The two armies fight each other AND the crew AND the dragon.

This is the most chaotic battlefield in the game. Three factions plus a dragon plus the crew in the eye of a hurricane with the storm wall closing in.

**Gameplay establishes:** Survival combat -- endure waves of enemies from multiple factions for a set duration. The dragon as an environmental hazard that attacks all sides. The drawing-cast minions (Brod's creatures and the mouse woman's fake soldiers) as allied combatants on the field. Ys's special move and his ability to move through elite fighters. The escalation from a local hunt to a full-scale convergence of two armies. The hurricane eye as a ticking clock -- when the wall hits, everything changes.

### Level 5: The Sealed Gate

The dragon is getting angrier. The battle, the armies, the chaos -- it has been pushed past territorial aggression into pure fury. It rears up and fires a **powerful white fireball** straight down at the ground.

The shot hits the shelter. But the shelter is not just a shelter.

The structure the army had been using as cover is an **ancient structure** -- sealed away for centuries, locked, forgotten. The armies used it as a shelter without knowing what it actually was. The dragon's white fireball hits the **gate** in exactly the right way to **unseal it**. The gate opens.

This is a **hidden secret of the Swahili Realm**. The key to opening this ancient structure was always a dragon -- a specific kind of force, a specific kind of fire. The dragon did not know this. The armies did not know this. Nobody planned for this.

The **native inhabitants** of the realm recognize what has been uncovered. They are **shocked**. This structure exists. It was sealed for a reason. The discovery is reported to **both armies** immediately. This is no longer about the prophecy child. This is bigger. Both armies signal **all hands on deck** -- every available unit is redirected to this location.

### The Escape Inside

Ys sees it on the radar. **Ships are approaching fast** -- not scouting parties, not elite squads, but full military fleets from both armies converging on the opened gate. Flying the ship out of here would be suicide. The airspace is about to be saturated with military vessels, the hurricane wall is closing, and the dragon is still raging.

Ys makes the call. He instructs the **entire crew, including the protagonist**, to go through the gate the dragon opened. Get inside the ancient structure. It is the only direction that is not filled with armies, storm, or dragon fire.

### Level 5 Gameplay -- The Moving Structure

The ancient structure is **massive and moving**. It is not a static building -- its interior components shift, rotate, and rearrange. Giant platforms, walls, and architectural segments are in constant motion, cycling through positions like the mechanisms of a colossal lock that has just been opened.

The gate is **closing**. The crew has a limited window to get inside before it seals. The protagonist must **time her advancement** across the giant moving structures to proceed -- jumping between shifting platforms, crossing gaps that open and close, navigating architecture that was not designed for people to walk through while it is active.

Ys **flies the ship through** a larger opening in the moving structure, threading it between shifting segments. The crew follows on foot, the protagonist leading the way through the moving gauntlet.

The **gate closes** behind them. The armies are locked out. The dragon is locked out. The storm is locked out.

But they are not alone inside.

The **Tiger Unit captain** managed to **slip through the gate** before it sealed. He is inside with the crew. Alone. Separated from his unit, his airship, his support. Just him.

He is **extremely dangerous even when alone.** The Tiger Unit captain is not like the wolf leader. The wolf leader was the best of an elite squad. The tiger captain is something beyond that -- the kind of fighter that gets deployed when elite squads fail, and he does not need his squad to be a lethal threat. One tiger, alone, inside a sealed ancient structure with the entire crew.

**Gameplay establishes:** Timed platforming on giant moving architecture -- precision traversal under a closing deadline. The ancient structure as a world-building revelation -- the Swahili Realm has secrets older than the war. The dragon as an accidental key. Ys's tactical decision to go inside rather than flee. The gate sealing as a point of no return -- the crew is safe from the armies but trapped with the tiger captain. The tiger captain as an immediate, personal, overwhelming threat.

### Level 6: The Puzzle Room

The crew finds themselves in a **giant room** deep inside the ancient structure. The moving architecture has stopped. The gate is sealed behind them. The tiger captain is somewhere inside, but not here. Not yet.

The crew is hurt. **Kat, the mouse woman, and Ys** are all injured from the battle outside -- the survival fight, the dragon chaos, the scramble through the moving gate. Kat is tending to wounds, including her own. She is the crew's medic, and right now she has too many patients and not enough time.

The room is a **puzzle**. The ancient structure does not let them pass without solving it. The architecture responds to interaction -- mechanisms, symbols, spatial logic embedded in the walls and floor. This is not a fight. This is a test the structure was designed to give.

**The protagonist, Brod, and the mechanic** solve the room. These three are the ones on their feet, functional, able to move and think. The protagonist brings her magic and tech knowledge. Brod brings his creativity and drawing. The mechanic brings her engineering mind -- she may not know magic, but she understands how things are built, how systems connect, and how mechanisms work.

### The Dialog

This level is **heavy on conversation**. With Kat, Ys, and the mouse woman sidelined by injuries, the three puzzle-solvers talk. This is the first extended downtime the crew has had since the ship was attacked. The adrenaline is fading. The walls are quiet. There is space to breathe and speak.

The protagonist, Brod, and the mechanic talk while they work -- about the situation, about each other, about what they have been through. The dynamic between these three has not been explored in depth until now. The protagonist who bullies Brod. Brod who considers the mechanic one of the only normal people on the crew. The mechanic who has been terrified, protected, and underestimated for the entire game. They are solving a puzzle together, and the conversation is as important as the solution.

**Gameplay establishes:** Puzzle mechanics -- environmental interaction, spatial logic, ancient mechanisms. Character development through dialog. The crew's injuries as a cost of the previous levels. The mechanic's intelligence on display in a non-combat context. The protagonist and Brod's dynamic shifting in a quieter moment. A change of pace from combat to problem-solving.
