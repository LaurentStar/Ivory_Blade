# Chapter 1: Snow

> *Face down. Cold. Alone. The earring is still intact. Get up.*

---

## Context

The crew is traveling through the Swahili Realm aboard Captain Ys's ship when they are hit by a brutal attack. The protagonist is thrown from the ship and crashes into a snowy mountaintop planetoid. She is separated from everyone.

The armies have not focused their attention on her yet. She has not beaten their elites. She has not proven herself a threat worth hunting seriously. The Husky Squad is not a targeted response -- they are a low-tier patrol unit that happens to be in the area and stumbles into the opportunity.

---

## Level 1: Crash into the Snow

The protagonist wakes up face down in the snow on a mountaintop planetoid. She is alone, disoriented, and the Meta Morph Blade is nearby. Kim is in the earring. He is still intact. She gets up.

The Husky Squad is already in the area. They are a mixed unit -- huskies, humans, and foxes -- weaker than the Wolf Squad, a low-priority unit, not elites. They operate in this snowy mountain terrain and have spotted the crash. They are closing in.

The protagonist fights through husky patrols, navigates the mountain, and establishes her bearings. She has no contact with the crew. She does not know the ship's status. She does not know if anyone else survived the attack.

**Gameplay establishes:** Movement, melee combat with the Meta Morph Blade (pike form), environmental navigation in snow and mountain terrain. The protagonist is 4'3" and alone against armed soldiers in freezing conditions. She handles it.

---

## Level 2: Contact with Kat

The protagonist reaches a viable location -- high ground, defensible, with enough open space for a magic signal to travel. She sends magic signals to Kat Arlean Mori to establish contact.

Communication is remote. Magic signals, not face-to-face. The protagonist's proficiency in both magic and tech is on display -- she builds and maintains the signal using her dual training. Kat responds from the ship (or wherever the crew has regrouped).

Kat provides status updates: the ship's condition, the crew's situation, and the local threat landscape. She scans the area and feeds the protagonist intel on the Husky Squad -- their numbers, their positions, their equipment. They form a plan to regroup.

Kat identifies that **Brod Tsumi** has been captured by the Husky Squad. He is not on the ship. He is somewhere on the mountain.

**Gameplay establishes:** Magic signal mechanics, Kat's role as strategist and intel source, remote crew coordination. The player learns to use the protagonist's magic/tech versatility for non-combat purposes.

---

## Level 3: Controlled Tsumi

The Husky Squad has captured Brod Tsumi and fitted him with a mind control device. Their goal was interrogation -- they wanted information about the crew, the ship, and why a group of outsiders crash-landed in their patrol zone.

Brod is hostile. Under the device's control, he attacks the protagonist on sight.

He is not much of a threat. Without his drawing-casting ability and Sketch, Brod is a mediocre fighter at best. The protagonist handles him easily. She already knows how to free him -- she identified the mind control device and its vulnerability before or during the fight (Kat's intel, her own observation, or Kim's hint from the earring).

### The Magic Tutorial

This fight teaches the player magic combat. The critical lesson: **the player can accidentally kill Brod with regular weapons.** If they use the Meta Morph Blade or physical attacks to finish him, he dies. Game over.

To free him, the player must use **magic** to deliver the final blow. The protagonist conjures a **sword of light** -- a magical attack that is painful but will not cause lethal bleeding. It breaks the mind control without killing the host. 

The tutorial teaches: magic is not just another damage type. It is the precision tool for situations where killing is not the answer.

### After the Fight

Brod is freed. The mind control device is removed or destroyed. He is shaken, hurt, but alive.

The Husky Squad's units have stopped responding -- the protagonist and Brod have cut through enough of them that command notices the silence. A **nearby mech** is dispatched to support the missing units.

### The Separation

Brod's gear is missing. When the Husky Squad captured him, they confiscated his equipment -- including his **sketchbook** (Sketch) and his **tablet** (Tablet). Both are at the Husky Squad's field camp. Without them, Brod has his dual swords and nothing else. No drawing-casting magic (Sketch is what brings his drawings to life), no bodyguard (Tablet is his lethal protector in close-quarters situations). He is a mediocre fighter with no support.

**Kat suggests Brod go back for the gear.** Via magic signal, she scans the area and locates the husky camp where Brod's confiscated equipment is being held. She provides surveillance: the camp is **small -- about four troops**. Not a fortress. A manageable target for one person.

Kat relays this to both of them. The camp is still out of the way -- off the protagonist's route -- but it is a realistic solo mission for Brod.

The protagonist is against it. She does not want to split up so Brod can go back for a stupid book and a robot. The mech is coming. They need to move, not run errands.

**Kat mentions the vessel.** She reminds the protagonist of what she carries -- Kim's earring. The thing that matters most to her in the world. If someone told her to take it off because it was just an earring, she would not listen either. That shuts the protagonist up. She does not argue further.

Brod goes alone. The protagonist stays on the mountain. The mech is close.

**Gameplay establishes:** Magic as a precision tool (kill vs. free), consequences for carelessness, the protagonist's knowledge advantage over raw power. The separation has a real reason -- Brod has a mission (recover Sketch and Tablet) and the protagonist has a fight (the mech).

---

## Level 4: Coordinated Pursuit

The Husky Squad **listened in on the magic signal conversation** between the protagonist and Kat. They intercepted enough to know her general plan, her position, and that she has crew support somewhere off the mountain. They report everything up the chain. Their commanding officer takes notice.

The disorganized patrols from Level 1 are gone. The remaining huskies reorganize with new intel. They set up ambushes on the routes she discussed with Kat. They tighten the perimeter. They are still weaker than the protagonist -- individually, she handles them -- but they are coordinated now. They know where she is trying to go.

The protagonist's surprising physical strength begins showing here. Her punches send soldiers and debris flying. She does not treat it as special -- it is just part of how she fights. A husky catches a fist and goes airborne. She does not comment on it. She keeps moving.

The Husky Squad's report reaches their boss. Their boss contacts the **Wolf Squad**. The wolves are the army's elite hunter-killer unit. This is no longer a patrol incident. The protagonist just became a flagged target.

**Gameplay establishes:** Consequence for the Level 2 magic signal (the enemy was listening), environmental awareness, the protagonist's raw physical power as a combat factor. Enemy AI is noticeably smarter -- ambushes, flanking, blocking exits.

---

## Level 5: The Wolf Squad Is Coming

Kat picks up new signals. Something bigger than the Husky Squad is moving toward the snow planetoid. She feeds the protagonist the intel: the **Wolf Squad** has been dispatched. They are en route. She does not know exactly when they will arrive, but they are coming.

The Wolf Squad is not the Husky Squad. They are an elite hunter-killer unit -- seven to ten wolf-like anthropomorphic soldiers, professional, gun-equipped, commanding lower-ranking human soldiers. The protagonist has not encountered them yet, but Kat's scans and the Swahili Realm's intelligence networks make it clear: these are the ones the army sends when patrols are not enough.

The calculus changes. The Husky Squad was manageable. The Wolf Squad is a different problem entirely. The protagonist needs to be off this mountain before they arrive.

This level is about urgency. She is still fighting huskies, still cutting through their coordinated patrols, but the clock is no longer just Kim's earring. There is a second timer now: the wolves. Every minute she spends fighting the huskies is a minute closer to a much worse fight.

**Gameplay establishes:** Escalation through information rather than direct combat. The player feels the approaching threat before seeing it. Kat's role as intelligence source deepens. The level teaches the player that winning every fight is not always the goal -- sometimes the goal is to move.

---

## Level 6: The Mech

The mech that was dispatched after the Husky Squad's units stopped responding arrives on the mountaintop. It is a military-grade combat walker piloted by a husky operator -- armored, armed with missiles, and designed for this terrain. A Metal Gear Solid-style machine on a frozen peak.

The protagonist is 4'3", carrying a pike, staring down a missile-equipped mech. She handles it.

Her physical strength matters here. She cannot pierce the mech's armor with the pike alone, but she can punch hard enough to dent panels, rip at exposed components, and send debris into its joints. The mech is built to fight soldiers and vehicles. It is not built to fight someone who can close the distance, dodge missiles at close range, and tear at it with her hands between pike strikes. The anomalous gravity of the Swahili Realm adds chaos -- the mech's footing is unreliable on the strange terrain, and the protagonist's agility lets her exploit the shifts.

She defeats the husky piloting it. He is alive but beaten.

### The Radio Deception

The protagonist takes control of the pilot's **radio and communication equipment**. She uses **magic to mimic the pilot's voice** -- a precise application of her training, not a raw power move. She contacts the incoming Wolf Squad.

She feeds them false intel. Wrong coordinates, fabricated status updates, a convincing performance as a shaken mech pilot reporting that the target has moved to a different sector. She directs the Wolf Squad away from her actual position.

This is not a permanent solution. The Wolf Squad will figure it out -- they are professionals, and the deception has a shelf life. But it creates a window. Hours, maybe less. Enough time to move.

### The Underground River

With the Wolf Squad misdirected, the protagonist contacts Kat one more time. They form an exit plan.

There is a path underground -- a river that runs beneath the mountain and feeds into a **lava lake** deeper in the planetoid's interior. The water is cold at the entrance, still carrying snowmelt, but it warms gradually as the tunnels descend toward the volcanic system below. It is a way off the snow.

The protagonist takes the underground path, following the river down and away from the mountaintop. She does not regroup with the full crew. She is still separated, still alone with Kim's earring. But she is off the mountain, out of the snow, and ahead of the wolves.

**Chapter 2 begins at ground level** on the Swahili Realm.

**Gameplay establishes:** Boss fight against military hardware, the protagonist's physical strength as a real combat tool, magic used for deception (voice mimicry), strategic thinking under pressure. The chapter ends with the player understanding: she does not always win by fighting harder. Sometimes she wins by being smarter.

## Open Questions

1. **The attack** -- What hit the ship? Was it the Husky Squad, a larger military force, or something else that caused the crash?
2. **Brod's path** -- Where does Brod end up after splitting from the protagonist in Level 3? Does he regroup with the ship crew, or is he still separate when Chapter 2 begins?
3. **The lava lake** -- What is waiting at ground level? Is the underground river exit near civilization, a battlefield, or empty terrain?
4. **The Wolf Squad's reaction** -- How quickly do they realize the radio deception? Do they pursue to the mountain or begin tracking her new route?
5. **Kim's earring** -- How much has the vessel's condition deteriorated by the end of Chapter 1? Does Kim comment on the time pressure?
