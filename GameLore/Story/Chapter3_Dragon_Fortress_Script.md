### 1.6 -- Ys Prepares from Inside (Parallel)

```
[CUTSCENE]
(Cut away from the protagonist. Interior of the army's shelter -- dark, cramped. The crew is being held in a makeshift cell. Nemo's wolf squad guards outside. Storm sounds through the walls.)

(Ys is seated against the wall. Still. Eyes closed. He looks like he is resting.)

(He is not resting.)

(His body shifts -- subtly, almost imperceptibly. His outline blurs. He slips free of his restraints without breaking them. His ghost-like nature allows him to pass through the bindings as though they are not there.)

(He moves to Brod. Quietly unties him. Brod's eyes open. He says nothing. Ys hands him Sketch. Beside Brod, Tablet is powered down -- slumped against the wall, eyes dark. Ys touches her power interface. Her blue eyes flicker on. She stands, recalibrates, and locks onto Brod.)

(Brod opens Sketch. Begins drawing. Quiet, focused, his pen moving across the pages in the dim light. Tablet positions herself beside him -- watching the cell entrance.)

(Ys moves to Vennessa. Unties her. She looks at him, confused. He puts a finger to his lips.)

YS (whisper): Draw. Quietly. Vennessa -- soldiers. Copies of theirs. Convincing. When I give the signal, deploy everything. Not before.

(Vennessa does not reach for her brush yet. She is looking through the cell bars -- past the guards, past the tents, out at the storm.)

(Brod is already working. He does not look up.)

VENNESSA (barely a whisper): She's out there. On her own.

BROD: Yeah.

VENNESSA: Doing what?

BROD: Something that makes all of this necessary.

(He tips his head at the page under her hand. At the army she has not started yet.)

VENNESSA: That isn't an answer.

BROD: It's the one I've got. She'll be fine. She's Indigo.

(He says it the way he always says it. It is not reassurance. It is a door closing.)

(Vennessa looks at him a moment longer. Then she does something she has never done.)

(She turns to Ys.)

VENNESSA (whisper): Captain.

(Ys stops. One hand against the wall, halfway back to his position. He does not turn around.)

VENNESSA: What happens to her?

(Storm against the walls. A guard shifts somewhere outside. Nobody in the cell moves.)

(Ys is still for exactly one second too long.)

YS: Draw.

(Vennessa pulls out her brush pen. She studies the army soldiers visible through the cell bars -- their armor, their posture, their gear. She begins painting. Her copies need to be convincing.)

(Ys moves back to his position. His body solidifies. He closes his eyes again. To anyone looking, he never moved.)
```

**Notes:** Ys's shape-shifting is the first real display of what he can do. It's understated -- he doesn't fight, he doesn't make a scene. He slips through restraints and gives orders. The audience sees that Ys is something other than human, and that his capabilities go far beyond what has been shown.

**Vennessa's question is the other half of this scene.** She asks Brod first and gets the answer he always gives -- "She's Indigo" is the same shrug he handed her in Ch1 5.3, and she reads it the same way. Then she does the thing nobody expects: she goes over his head. **These are her first words to Ys in the entire game**, and they are a question he will not answer.

The setting does the work. They are in a cell with wolves on the other side of the bars and everything has to be whispered, so "Draw" reads as a captain shutting down chatter during an operation. That is exactly what Vennessa hears, and she goes back to painting without pushing. **The audience should hear something else.** The one-second pause before he speaks is the whole performance -- Ys knows precisely what happens to Indigo, and he has just been asked by the one person on this ship with no stake in the prophecy, no rank, and no reason to be lied to.

Play the pause honestly. Do not let him look at her, and do not let Vennessa suspect anything. Her established mode is one soft question followed by reading the room and stopping. She stops.

### 2.4 -- Tiger Unit Arrives (Parallel)

```
[CUTSCENE]
(Cut away from the protagonist. The crew's ship -- grounded at the giant tower from Chapter 2. Robert is inside. The captured Jack is restrained with the stone bindings Indigo made.)

(A shadow passes over the ship. Heavy. The sound of engines -- military grade, built to handle this storm.)

(The Tiger Unit captain steps out last. He is bigger than the rest. He moves like he owns the ground beneath him.)

(He walks to the crew's ship. Opens the hatch like it isn't locked. Robert is inside.)

(He grabs her by the neck and forces her down -- knees hit the floor. She's tall, so he brings her to his eye level by driving her into the ground.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: Where is the girl?

(Robert can barely breathe. His grip is crushing. She points -- toward the sky, toward the floating fortress barely visible through the storm clouds.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: I won't ask twice.

(He lets go. She collapses forward, gasping.)

(He moves to Jack. Looks at the stone restraints. Breaks them with one hand. Jack stands -- free, rubbing his wrists.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: Your squad was beaten by a child. Your youngest is dead. And you were tied up at a ship as a prisoner

(Jack says nothing.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: If you weren't useful for tracking, I'd leave you here.

(Jack follows the tiger captain onto the airship. He does not argue. He does not look back at Robert.)
```

**Notes:** The tiger captain is introduced through violence and contempt. He chokes Robert without hesitation. He breaks Indigo's magical restraints with one hand -- the same restraints Jack couldn't budge. And his dismissal of Jack is total: "tied up at a ship as a prisoner" reduces everything Jack went through to nothing. The audience should feel the power gap immediately.

### 4.2 -- The Dragon Hits

```
[CUTSCENE]
(The protagonist reaches the shelter area. She has the egg. The sky above is deceptively calm -- the eye of the hurricane. Blue sky, still air. The storm wall churns in a massive circle around them.)

(The dragon descends. It does not care about the calm. It cares about the egg.)

(It hits the shelter like a bomb. Structures collapse. Soldiers scatter. Fire, debris, screaming. The army scrambles to respond to a threat they abandoned a fortress to avoid.)

(In the chaos -- Brod's drawings deploy. Combat creatures spring from Sketch's pages, engaging soldiers in the confusion. Vennessa's fake soldiers flood the field -- copies of real army troops, loyal to Vennessa, blending in with the panicking forces.)

(The protagonist lands in the middle of it. Egg still in hand. The dragon circles above, raging.)

(She looks at the egg. She looks at the nearest Legion tent -- canvas half-collapsed, soldiers pouring out of it.)

(She throws it in.)

(No ceremony. No aim. She gets rid of it the way you get rid of something that is done being useful. The egg disappears into the canvas. Whether it breaks in there, whether it survives, whether anyone in that tent even understands what just landed on them -- none of it is shown, and none of it matters to her.)

(She does not watch where it goes. She is already moving.)

(Above them, the dragon has lost track of it. That does not calm it down. It makes everything worse.)
```

**Notes:** The egg has done its job. It got the dragon here, and now the protagonist is holding a beacon in the middle of a battlefield -- the single worst object to be carrying. Throwing it is the correct tactical call and it is also completely callous, which is the point. She stole a living thing to use as bait and she discards it without a second look. No line, no hesitation, no glance back.

**Its fate is deliberately unresolved.** Do not show the egg breaking and do not show it intact. The camera does not follow it. What matters is that the dragon can no longer track it, that the dragon is now enraged with nothing to focus on, and that it is tearing apart everything in reach for the rest of the level. Leaving the outcome open keeps the option available and keeps the moral weight on the protagonist rather than on the outcome.

### 4.3b -- Deployment (Parallel)

```
[CUTSCENE -- PARALLEL]
(Inside the fortress. The dragon's impact shakes the walls. Dust falls. The army scrambles toward the breach.)

(Ys stands. Brod is beside him, sketchbook full, drawings ready to deploy. Tablet is behind them both -- still, alert, blue eyes scanning the breach.)

(Brod hesitates -- looking out at the chaos through the fortress openings. Explosions. Soldiers running. The dragon roaring.)

YS: Do what I paid ya for boy!

(Ys grabs Brod by the jacket and hurls him out into the fighting chaos. Brod tumbles, rolls, and hits the ground mid-battle. Tablet follows -- she doesn't need to be thrown. She steps out into the chaos on her own, moving to Brod's flank. Sketch snaps open. Drawings deploy -- ink constructs flood out, combat creatures materializing around him.)

(Vennessa's fake soldiers pour out from behind cover -- blending with real troops, spreading confusion across the battlefield.)

(Brod hits the ground. Gets up. Looks back at where Ys threw him from.)

BROD: ...EVERY TIME.

(A soldier charges at him. Brod sidesteps, slashes with both swords -- clean, fast. The soldier drops.)

BROD (muttering, furious): I draw. That's what I do. I sit in the back and I draw. I don't do... THIS.

(Another soldier. Brod blocks, counters, sends him sprawling. Sketch deploys a combat creature beside him -- it rushes into the crowd.)

BROD: I'm gonna charge him double for this!


(Brod sees the angry dragon and the storm clouds. The situation has escalated sharply.)

(Brod stops. Mid-battle. Swords down. Chest heaving. He stares at nothing -- not at the soldiers, not at the chaos, not at where Ys threw him from. He's done. His body language says walk away.)

BROD: fuck this!

(The shadows around Brod darken. Not the battlefield -- just him. The light pulls away from his body like something is standing between him and the sun. Frost crackles across his sleeves, his jacket, the hilts of his swords. His breath becomes visible -- white vapor in air that should be warm from dragonfire and explosions. The sounds of battle dull. Distant. Muffled.)

AVOLIN (ambient -- everywhere, nowhere, crisp corporate audio): Brod! Our systems have detected your motivation to fulfill your payment obligations has dropped below the contracted threshold. This is a friendly reminder that your Movator agreement remains active and non-negotiable. Please continue generating income... or we will be forced to initiate early collection. Thank you for being a valued Movator participant!

(The shadows recede. The frost fades. Battle audio slams back to full volume -- explosions, screaming, the dragon roaring overhead.)

(Brod is no longer angry. He is pale. His jaw sets -- not from resolve. From something colder.)

(He doesn't decide to fight. His body is already fighting. Swords up, positioning exactly where the crew needs cover deployed, drawing creatures that complement the formation -- all without a single word from Ys. His mouth was ready to quit. His hands never stopped working.)

(More enemies close in. He does his job the way he always does: pale, quiet, and good enough to survive.)
```

**Notes:** Ys doesn't give speeches. He doesn't motivate. He pays Brod and expects results. The physical throw is his version of a pep talk. Brod's reaction is real -- he is genuinely pissed about being forced to the front line. "I sit in the back and I draw" is who he is. He's a support caster being used as a frontliner and he knows it's wrong. "I'm gonna charge him double" -- he processes everything through the paycheck. But when the rant peaks and he almost walks away, **Avolin checks in.** The perky corporate voice, the frost, the shadows -- this is the first time the player sees and hears what "Avolin Credit" actually means. The name was dropped in Ch2 ("Avolin Credit?" / "........") as a joke that made Brod go silent. Now the audience knows why. The paycheck from Ys isn't a preference -- it's the only thing between Brod and literal hell. The demon doesn't threaten. It reminds. A billing department from damnation. And notice: the demon gets Brod moving, but what Brod does with that movement is perfectly calibrated to Ys's needs. The debt forces him to stay. The training tells him what to do. He'd call it "just doing the job." The audience sees it's more than that.

### 4.4b -- Brod and the Tiger Captain (Parallel)

```

(Soldiers move out of the way. Both armies. Nobody wants to be standing where he is walking.)

(Brod looks up.)

SKETCH (from the book, quiet -- just to Brod): ...Master.

BROD: I see him.

(The tiger captain does not slow down. Brod is simply in the line he is walking.)

(Brod does the only thing he knows how to do. He draws -- fast, nothing elaborate, no time -- and deploys. Three constructs, ink hardening into muscle and teeth, all of them between him and the tiger.)

(The tiger captain goes through them in one motion.)

(Not three motions. One. They come apart into loose ink before any of them lands anything.)

(Brod gets his swords up. It does not matter. The impact takes him off his feet and puts him in the dirt on his back, both blades gone from his hands.)

(He cannot breathe. He is trying to and it is not working.)

(A shadow falls across him.)

(Tablet arrives.)

(She hits the tiger captain from the side at full speed -- silent, precise, no warning. It is the fastest Brod has ever seen her move. It buys two seconds.)

(The tiger captain catches her. Not easily. But he catches her, and he puts her down, and it costs him almost nothing.)

(He looks at Brod on the ground. Half a second. Maybe less.)

(Brod looks back up at him. Winded, one sword knocked out his hand, surprised.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: No.

(That is the entire assessment. Not a threat. Not a taunt. A conclusion.)

(He walks on. Toward the fighting. Toward the girl.)

(Brod stays down a moment longer than he needs to.)

(Tablet picks herself up. Comes back to his side. Offers a hand. He takes it.)

(Neither of them says anything.)
```

**Notes:** This is the encounter Brod refers to in Ch4 1.4 -- "I fought the tiger captain." / "He's... kinda tough." 

The point is that the tiger captain does not continue to attack him. His goal is the prophency child. He want to secure the package in the chaos first.

**Tablet is the only reason he is alive, and nobody in the crew will ever know it happened.** She hits the tiger captain harder and faster here than anyone has ever seen. She is simiplily protecting Brod.

**Indigo does not witness this.** She is fighting on the far side of a battlefield with three factions and a dragon on it. That matters for Ch4 1.4: when Brod warns her and she brushes him off, she is dismissing a report from the only member of the crew who has actually been hit by this man.

**Brod does not learn the tiger captain later slips through the gate.** Kat detects two signatures at the end of L6 and cannot identify them, and the crew stays in the dark. All Brod knows is that he met something on that field that was stronger than usual

### 4.5 -- Ys Rescues Robert (Parallel)

```
[CUTSCENE]

## Script Notes

### Voice Direction -- IndigoLLiy
- Same as previous chapters. Cocky, direct, short sentences.
- This chapter adds a leadership quality -- she's making calls, coordinating with Kat, running the plan. She's not the captain, but she's the one doing the work.
- "That's a big lizard" -- she deflects fear with understatement.

### Voice Direction -- Kim
- Quieter in this chapter. The plan is dangerous and he knows it. His humor is still there but more muted.
- "I heard a terrible plan" is honest. He doesn't pretend to be confident when he isn't.

### Voice Direction -- Kat
- Still the smartest person in any conversation. She coordinates from captivity, feeds intel, and maintains composure.
- "Don't let it catch you" -- dry, practical. She knows it's not helpful. She says it anyway because there's nothing else to say.
- "Just noting it for the record" -- Kat's way of saying "this is a bad idea and I want it documented."

### Voice Direction -- Captain Ys
- Minimal words, maximum impact. "Now." is an entire command.
- His remote ship activation and phasing through tigers are shown, not explained. The crew doesn't question it. They're used to not understanding Ys.

### Voice Direction -- Robert (Mechanic)
- This is her chapter. The puzzle room is where Robert proves she belongs on this crew.
- "Machines talk. You just have to listen." -- her defining line. She has been underestimated the entire game. The puzzle room changes that.
- "Nobody listens to the mechanic until something breaks." -- earned frustration, not bitterness.

### Voice Direction -- Brod
- Growth. He gives Vennessa credit unprompted. He pushes back on Indigo without backing down.
- His drawing is shown as fast, functional, combat-ready -- he's good at what he does and he knows it.
- **In 4.4c he is terrified for the entire scene and it never stops him.** Play the fear openly -- shaking hands, a symbol that comes out wrong, "don't stop don't stop don't stop" muttered to nobody. None of it is bravery and it should not be performed as bravery. His mouth is ready to quit. His hands never do.
- The turn in 4.4c is one line of stage direction -- "He stops trying to fight him." Everything before it is Brod doing what he thinks a fighter is supposed to do. Everything after it is Brod doing what he is actually good at.
- "We did not win that." -- no self-deprecation in it, and no joke. He is stating a fact and he is right. He does not hear the other fact, which is that he did not lose it either.

### Voice Direction -- Tiger Captain
- Violence. Contempt. He chokes Robert without hesitation and dismisses Jack's entire arc in three sentences.
- He does not look concerned about being sealed in an unknown structure alone. That's the scariest thing about him.

### Voice Direction -- Jack
- Silent. Defeated. He follows the tiger captain without arguing. The contrast with Chapter 2 Jack -- the relentless hunter -- should be stark.
- **He speaks exactly twice in this chapter, both words, both to Brod, in 4.4c.** Nowhere else. Not to the tiger captain who humiliates him in L2, not at the sealed gate in L5. If he sounds like a man with anything left to say, it is wrong.
- **4.4c is where the silence comes from.** He is not passive in that fight -- he is the best fighter on the field with the girl absent, and he is trying to take back something the tiger captain removed from him. The stillness everywhere else in the chapter is what is left after that does not work either.
- "Not you." -- flat, quiet, no weight on either word. He is not making a point. He is closing a file. Do not let him sound wounded, and do not let him sound merciful.

### Voice Direction -- Avolin Credit Demon
- **No beep.** The Avolin demon does not use the character beep system. Its voice plays as clean corporate audio -- crisp, high-fidelity, uncomfortably polished compared to everything else in the game. It sounds like it's being played through speakers that shouldn't exist in this world.
- **Spatial audio:** Ambient -- comes from everywhere. Not directional. The player cannot locate the source.
- **Visual cue:** Shadows darken around Brod's body. Frost appears on his clothing and skin. The surrounding environment dims. These effects onset before the voice and fade after it finishes.
- **Tone:** Perky, helpful, customer-service. The kind of voice that says "your call is important to us" while describing damnation. Think corporate hold music energy applied to existential threats.
- **First appearance:** Chapter 3, section 4.3b. The name "Avolin Credit" was dropped in Chapter 2 as foreshadowing. This is the first time the player hears the demon speak and sees the ambient effects.

### Bark Volume
- Dragon-related barks from soldiers should sound panicked. The army feared this thing enough to abandon a fortress.
- Vennessa's fake soldiers should be indistinguishable from real army chatter -- the confusion is the point.
- During the survival fight, barks from all three factions overlap. Chaos is the soundscape.
