## Consensus: What All Three Agree On

### Universal Strengths
1. **Escalation is exceptional.** All three reviewers scored P3 at 4/5 or 5/5. The Husky → Wolf → Tiger → Demon power ladder is the story's structural backbone -- every rung is a qualitative leap caused by the previous one's failure.
2. **Theme is expressed through action, not dialogue.** T2 scored 4-5/5 across all reviewers. "Average talent, extraordinary persistence" is never spoken -- it is demonstrated through the wolf captain (intelligence over violence), the tiger captain (persistence over power), and the magic tutorial (precision over lethality).
3. **Gameplay and narrative are deeply integrated.** All reviewers praised the anomalous gravity as a combat/traversal/puzzle mechanic, the magic tutorial as ludonarrative harmony, and boss fights as story events rather than pure challenges.
4. **The protagonist has a strong, clear identity.** C1 scored 4/5 from all three. IndigoLLiy's motivation (save Kim), method (find the angle), and personality (cocky socially, cold in combat) are immediately legible.

### Universal Weaknesses
1. **The two armies are undefined.** W5 scored 2/5 from all three reviewers. The story's central conflict is a war, and neither faction has a name, ideology, culture, or distinguishing feature. They are "Army 1" and "Army 2."
2. **Kim is the premise but barely the plot.** All three flagged that Kim's earring conversations -- the story's stated emotional core -- have almost no on-page content across four chapters. His deterioration is not tracked, his reactions are not shown, and the ticking clock is asserted rather than felt.
3. **The final act introduces too much without foreshadowing.** The wish-granting machine, demon god, planet demon, and golden child all appear in Ch3-4 with no setup in Ch1-2. The dragon's fireball unsealing the gate is a coincidence driving the entire third act.
4. **Dialogue cannot be fully evaluated.** D1-D4 scored lowest across all reviewers (11-14/20) because the chapters are outline-prose, not scripted dialogue. Character voices are strong in concept but unproven on the page.
5. **Resolution is incomplete.** P7 scored 2-3/5 from all reviewers. The game ends mid-event with too many threads stacked simultaneously: demon, golden child, tiger captain's fate, Kim, the protagonist's reaction to being used.

1. **The Two Armies Are Undefined (W5: 2/5)** -- Neither faction has a name, ideology, or distinguishing feature.

1. Define the two armies with names, cultures, and ideologies.

6. Ground the planet demon in UE system logic so it belongs in the same world as wolf squads.

## Combined Recommendations (Priority Order)

These are the issues flagged by all three reviewers, ranked by how many reviewers raised them:

### Flagged by All 3 Reviewers
1. **Define the two armies** -- Names, ideologies, cultures, visual identity. The war needs factions the audience can distinguish.
2. **Write Kim into every chapter** -- Track his deterioration, show his reactions, establish his voice. The story's emotional core needs on-page content.
3. **Foreshadow the ancient structure and Ch4 reveals in Ch1-2** -- Anomalous readings, carvings, military references, native rumors. The biggest narrative turns need runway.
4. **Write actual dialogue** -- At least one sample scene per chapter to test character voices before locking the script.
5. **Improve the ending** -- Resolve or explicitly address at least one major thread (protagonist's reaction to being used, tiger captain's fate, Kim's status).
