**Notes:** Brod's "kinda tough" is the most important two words he says in this chapter. He is not a fighter. He does not rate opponents. Brod is understating a fight he lost in seconds, and he is understating it because that is how he talks about everything.

Indigo does not take it seriously because Brod's combat opinion has never carried weight with her. She did not see 4.4b -- she was on the other side of the battlefield -- so she is dismissing a firsthand report as an anxious kid being anxious. She will regret this.

### 1.5 -- Wish Machine Discovery

```
[CONVERSATION]
(Kat and Brod are working an ancient terminal. Tablet stands at the console with one hand flat against it, interfaced directly -- her eyes running data, holographic archive text unspooling in the air above her palm. Sketch is closed under Brod's arm. She has nothing to offer here and she knows it.)

(Tablet does not speak. She projects. Brod reads what she surfaces and says it out loud.)

BROD: There's a whole archive in here. Digital records -- old, but intact. The structure has its own data system and it's still running.

KAT: I can read the magical signatures layered into the records. Cross-referencing with what Tablet is pulling out of the hardware.

BROD: First layer. This place is a containment system. The entire structure was built around something.

KAT: Confirmed. The magical architecture is concentric. Everything points inward. Whatever is at the center, the structure was designed to keep it there.

(Beat. The crew processes this.)

BROD: Second layer. There are records describing what's at the center. Detailed. Consistent. Cross-referenced across multiple archives.

(Tablet lifts her palm off the console. The hologram blooms outward -- pages of archive text turning slowly in the air, dense with a language nobody in the room reads. Kat leans in.)

KAT: A sealed machine that grants a single wish to whoever completes the trials within.

(Silence.)

INDIGO + VENNESSA: A wish!?

KAT: The trials are built into the structure itself. The corridors, the chambers, the launch system I detected -- they're tests. Complete them, prove yourself worthy, earn the wish.

BROD: The records go back centuries. Every entry says the same thing. Different authors, different time periods, same story. It isn't one person planting a lie. It's baked into the structure's entire history.

KAT: The gate was sealed and buried. The Legion pitched their camp directly on top of it -- tents, vehicles, the whole position -- and sheltered from the storm behind the structure itself without ever knowing what they were standing on. The natives didn't know it existed either. Whatever is at the center of this place, people went to extraordinary lengths to keep everyone out -- and then made sure everyone forgot there was anything to keep out.

(Indigo is quiet. Processing.)

BROD: ...That sounds too good to be true.

KAT: It does. It also checks out. Hundreds of years of entries and not one of them disagrees. 

KAT: The only thing that isn't in here is the dragon. No mention of it anywhere. Not as a guard, not as a warning, not as anything.

BROD: It nested in the fortress. Up in the sky, north of here. It never came near this place until we pulled it down on top of us.

KAT: So the one thing that actually opened the door is the one thing nobody wrote down.

(Camera: rotating between speakers. The information lands in pieces -- each one more convincing than the last.)
(Trigger: after the crew settles in the main chamber.)
```

**Notes:** The wish machine reveal is the chapter's hinge. Everything Indigo does from this point is driven by what she heard here. The information is presented as credible -- multiple sources, consistent records, centuries of documentation. The player should believe it. Brod's skepticism is noted but overwhelmed by the evidence. The audience needs to buy the lie before the lie breaks.

**Tablet is the tech half, not Sketch.** Tablet was a digital device before she was a person -- interfacing with a still-running data system is what she natively is. Sketch's power is animating Brod's drawings; she has no relationship to archives or hardware, and she stays closed under his arm for this whole scene. Using Tablet also costs nothing in secrecy: the crew already reads her as Brod's equipment, so a robot with her hand on a console projecting holograms is the least remarkable thing in the room. Nobody registers that she is choosing what to show them.

This sets up two payoffs. Tablet interfacing with the structure here is why she can relay gravity-gate solutions to Indigo remotely in Level 2. And she does the quiet backbone work of the entire chapter -- archive, puzzles, containment nets -- without one person thanking her or speaking to her directly, which is the ground End.6 stands on.

Kat is the one who certifies the records as consistent. She reads the magic, she translates the archive, she vouches for it out loud. That is what makes her reassurance to Indigo in End.2 cost her something.

```
VENNESSA: (nods)

```

### 1.7 -- Indigo's Decision

```
[CONVERSATION]
(Indigo has been quiet since the wish machine reveal. She approaches Ys. Not to ask. To inform.)

INDIGO: I'm going deeper. The tiger captain is hunting us and the wish machine is down there. Same path, both objectives. I'm not abandoning anything -- I'm handling two problems at once. I need Kat on remote support mapping the structure and tracking the tiger captain. Robert on comms for anything the ship's systems pick up.

(She is requesting support, not permission.)

YS: No.

(Indigo does not argue. Does not justify. Does not explain Kim.)

INDIGO: Anyone coming along?

(A beat. Nobody moves. The crew looks between Indigo and Ys.)

INDIGO: I guess not. Good.

(She turns and walks into the corridor.)

VENNESSA: I hope it's down there.

(Indigo stops. Turns halfway....)

INDIGO: ... I'll wish the kitty back if possible.

(She keeps walking. The structure swallows her. The crew watches her go.)

(Ys does not stop her. He could. He does not.)

(Camera: wide shot as Indigo walks into the structure alone. The corridor stretches ahead -- metallic, polished, unknown. The crew is framed behind her, getting smaller.)
(Trigger: after Ys assigns roles.)
```

**Notes:** Indigo presents her plan and requests support in one statement. She is not negotiating. Ys objects -- one word. She does not argue back. "Anyone coming along?" is not a question. It is the period at the end of a sentence she already finished.


"I hope it's down there" should sound like nothing. Indigo does not understand it and does not have time to. **The audience should not understand it yet either** -- it only resolves in the ending beat, once the wish machine has turned out to be a lie and Vennessa explains what she meant. Do not have anyone react or comment. Let it sit.

### 2.2 -- The Encounter

```
[CUTSCENE]
(Indigo enters a corridor junction. The metallic walls stretch in three directions. She pauses, scanning.)

(Movement. Fast. From her left.)

(The tiger captain hits her before she can react. Not a strike -- a full-body collision that drives her into the wall. The metal dents behind her.)

(She pushes off, pike out. He is already swinging. She blocks -- barely. The force drives her back. Her feet slide on the polished floor.)

(He does not stop. He does not pause between strikes. Every movement flows into the next -- claws, fists, elbows, kicks. There is no rhythm to read. There is no pattern to exploit. He fights like an avalanche: constant, escalating, indifferent to resistance.)

(Indigo adapts. She always adapts. She reads the spacing, finds the gaps, tries to create distance for the pike. It does not work. Every gap she finds closes before she can use it. Every distance she creates, he erases in a step.)

(She switches to short swords. Close quarters. His territory. She lands two cuts -- shallow, across his arms. He does not acknowledge them.)

(He catches her by the throat. Lifts. Drives her into the floor. The metal cracks beneath her.)

(She kicks free. Rolls. Gets up. He is already there.)

(This is not a fight. This is a lesson.)
```

---

### 2.3 -- Jack Arrives

```
[CUTSCENE]
(Jack appears at the far end of the corridor. He sees the beating in progress.)

(He does not intervene. His hands stay at his sides. His body leans forward -- the instinct to fight is there. He does not move.)

(The tiger captain notices. He does not stop. He drives a knee into Indigo's ribs, sends her skidding across the floor. She coughs. Blood.)

(When there is a pause -- when Indigo is on the ground and the point has been made -- the tiger captain looks at Jack.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: Good. You're learning.

(Jack says nothing.)

(Indigo is already moving. Not toward them. Away. Deeper into the structure. Bleeding. Running. Putting distance between herself and something she cannot beat.)

(The tiger captain lets her go. He watches her flee the way a predator watches prey it has already decided is not worth chasing yet.)

(Camera: the final shot holds on Jack. Standing in the corridor. Hands at his sides. The tiger captain's words still hanging in the air.)
```

**Notes:** "Good. You're learning." is the first backhanded compliment. Jack has never been a soldier who holds position. He leads from the front, he does not delegate the hardest fight, he went back down into collapsing lava caves for Bassual's body, and he peeled off from his own squad to hunt Indigo personally. Standing still is the one thing he has never done. The tiger captain treats Jack not jumping in as a minor miracle of discipline. It is condescending. Jack staying out of the fight is not a tactical choice -- it is a dog learning to sit. And the tiger captain praises him for it. The audience should feel the weight of what it cost Jack to stand there.

Brod was right. The tiger captain is kinda tough.

### 3.3 -- Stand Down

```
[CUTSCENE]
(The tiger captain sees Indigo enter. He does not react immediately. He looks at Jack.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: Stay out of it.

JACK: I can--

TIGER CAPTAIN: You can what? Help? You would get in the way. You would need saving. Stand down and let me finish what I started.

TIGER CAPTAIN: If she kills me -- which she will not -- you can run... Until then, you watch. Don't die stupidly

```

**Notes:** This is the second time the tiger captain has put Jack in his place. The first was a backhanded compliment for staying out of the mauling. This is a direct order paired with a statement of inadequacy. Jack -- the squad leader who hunted Indigo across an entire chapter, who fought her one-on-one, who has never once let someone else take the hard fight for him -- listens. The tiger captain's authority is absolute. The cost of that authority is about to arrive.

### 3.4 -- The Guardian Emerges

```

[CUTSCENE]
INDIGO: (Draw her pike)

INDIGO: BRING IT YOU NOSY BASTARD!


(Before the fight begins, the chamber shakes. The floor cracks. Something beneath them stirs.)

(A form rises from the center of the arena. Massive. Skeletal. Black bone, ancient and dense. Red eyes ignite in a skull the size of a small building.)

(A guardian. Sealed within the structure. Dormant for centuries. Awake because someone reached the arena.)

(The tiger captain turns. He does not retreat. He adjusts.)

TIGER CAPTAIN: Deal with this first!

(He engages the guardian. Full force. Claws tearing at the skeleton's legs, dodging sweeping arms, striking at joints. He fights the way he fought Indigo -- constant, overwhelming, without pause.)

(Jack engages too. Despite the order. The guardian is too massive, too ancient -- he cannot stay on the sideline when something this powerful is in the room. He attacks from the flank, drawing attention, creating openings.)

(It is not enough.)

(The guardian backhands the tiger captain into the wall. The impact cracks the metal. He does not get up.)

(Jack is hit next. A sweeping arm catches him mid-dodge and sends him spinning across the floor. He lands hard. Conscious. Broken.)

(The guardian turns toward Indigo.)
```

**Notes:** The hierarchy is established instantly. The tiger captain -- who mauled Indigo in a cutscene -- is dispatched by the guardian without ceremony. The player watched the tiger captain demolish Indigo in Level 2 and now watches the guardian demolish the tiger captain. The power scaling is clear: Indigo < Tiger Captain < Guardian. And Indigo has to fight the guardian alone.

```
(Soft-lock degree: tight.)

INDIGO: Don't look at me with those puppy dog eye. Heh Heh.

(Jack says nothing.)

INDIGO: I don't kick puppies while their down, espically weak ones. Step aside mutt, I got a wish to make.

(Nothing.)

INDIGO: Good boy (smiles)

(Nothing.)

INDIGO: You are one well trained little B boy.

(Jack's jaw tightens. Barely. It is the first thing he has given her.)

INDIGO: There it is. I see you getting mad -- step up!

(She waits for it. Pike loose in her hand. Ready.)

(The jaw loosens. He goes back to nothing.)
```

**Notes:** Indigo could kill the tiger captain and Jack. She does not. She kills in a fight -- she does not finish opponents who are already down. Same call she made with the husky mech pilot in Chapter 1 and with Jack in Chapter 2. Her mockery is not cruelty for its own sake. She is needling the person who hunted her, fought her, and tracked her across the Swahili Realm. The taunt has to account for what the player just watched: Jack *did* break the stand-down order for the guardian and got swatted for it. What he never did was come after her. "Why didn't you come after me? I'm right here." is the direct hit. The effect is devastating because it lands on top of the tiger captain's dismissal. Two people who should be enemies agree on the same thing: Jack moved for the wrong fight and it did not matter.

### 3.8 -- The Wish

```
[CUTSCENE]
(The arena shifts. The metallic walls begin to glow -- warming from cold silver to gold. Light fills the space. The guardian's bones dim. The wreckage fades into the brightness. It is beautiful.)

(A voice fills the arena. Not from speakers. Not from a person. From the structure itself.)

VOICE (warm, resonant, sacred): You have endured the trials. You have defeated the guardian. You have proven yourself worthy.

(The light intensifies. The arena feels holy.)

VOICE: You have earned your wish. Speak what you desire, and it shall be granted.

(Indigo stands in the light. Bleeding. Exhausted. Four chapters of fighting, bleeding, and losing people. The wolf squad. The tiger unit. The dragon. The gate. The beating. The guardian. All of it led here.)

(She does not hesitate.)

INDIGO: [VOICED] Kim.

(The word hangs in the golden light.)

INDIGO: Restore him. Heal what was done to him. Give him back.

(Silence. The light holds. The arena waits.)
```

**Notes:** "Kim." is voiced. One word. The first time the audience hears Indigo say his name with her actual voice. Everything she has done in this chapter -- defying Ys, taking the beating from the tiger captain, dragging herself through the red corridors, fighting the guardian alone -- was for this moment. The wish voice speaks in the arena -- the same room where the guardian fell, where the tiger captain lies unconscious, where Jack lies broken. No separate chamber. The golden light transforms the space around her. The player should feel it. The audience should believe the wish is real.

### 3.9 -- The Lie Breaks

### C.4 -- Keep Talking

```
[CONVERSATION]
(Indigo has not moved. Behind her the other two groups carry on -- Kat's voice pitched to carry, Robert laughing at something, the dry scrape of a chart being rolled up. Nobody comes over. Nobody needs anything from her.)

(She is alone in the middle of her own crew.)

(She taps the earring.)

INDIGO: You've been quiet.

KIM: You've been busy.

INDIGO: That's not what I asked.

KIM: It wasn't a question, either.

(Beat.)

KIM: I heard all of it. The voice. The trials. The thing at the end.

INDIGO: Yeah.

KIM: You okay?

INDIGO: I got a planet killed.

KIM: That's not what I asked.

(She almost smiles. It does not quite arrive.)

KIM: Can I ask you something and you don't get weird about it?

INDIGO: No.

KIM: Great. Why'd you go down there?

(She does not answer straight away. Above them the golden child drives the demon back through a collapsing arc of light, and neither of them looks up.)

INDIGO: It was on the way.

KIM: ...Was it.

INDIGO: It was on the way, Kim.

(He lets it go. He is the best in the world at letting things go, and it has never once meant he believed her.)

KIM: Okay.

(Beat.)

KIM: The mouse girl was good.

INDIGO: Vennessa.

KIM: ...Vennessa. Sorry.

KIM: She's right, you know.

INDIGO: I know.

KIM: I'm going to be extremely annoying about it if you forget.

INDIGO: You're already extremely annoying my friend

KIM: I'm dead. You have to be nice to me.

INDIGO: You're not dead.... yet

(It comes out faster and harder than she meant it.)

(Silence in the earring.)

KIM: ...No. I'm not.

(She takes the earring between her fingers. Not tapping it. Just holding it.)

KIM: Hey.

INDIGO: Yeah.

KIM: Keep talking to me.

INDIGO: I know how it works, Kim.

KIM: Remember you gotta keep me entertained while you save me

(Beat.)

INDIGO: ...I know. (chuckle) I know.
```

**Notes:** **The gap holds.** Kim asks the direct question -- why did you go down there -- and she lies to him, and they both know she is lying, and he lets her. He still does not know the wish machine was for him. This is the chapter's stated emotional engine and it is worth more carried into the next game than spent in a credits scene. Everything here is written to press against that line without crossing it.

**"It was on the way" is the same lie she told Ys.** She framed the wish machine to the captain as two objectives on one path. She is now using the operational excuse on the one person it was never meant for, and it is the only dishonest thing she does in the entire sequence.

**Kim heard everything.** He is in her ear at all times -- he heard the heavenly voice, the demon, End.1, and every word of Vennessa's speech. Which is exactly why he is the one person on this ship who does not have to reassure her. That conversation already happened without him, so he gets to skip it and just be her friend. His register here is the one from his profile -- funny, goofy, quick -- and it is the first time in the chapter he has been allowed it. See the Ch4 voice direction: he is quieter than any previous chapter right up until this scene.

**"You're not dead."** Kim jokes about everything, including this, and it is the one thing she cannot take as a joke. She snaps. He stops instantly and agrees with her, because he is socially smarter than she is and because he is not going to make her carry that on today of all days. This is the emotional center of the vignette and it is four lines long.

**The last exchange is not sentimental, it is medical.** Conversation is what slows the vessel's decay -- silence degrades him. "Keep talking to me" is Kim asking her to keep him alive and framing it as company so she does not have to hear it as a request. She hears both. Her "...Okay" is the third instance of that word as her private signal that she has decided to do something hard, after **Ch3 1.3** and **End.4b**.

**Indigo corrects Kim to "Vennessa."** Small and deliberate. Kat has been the only person aboard who uses her name -- see `GameLore/Characters/Vennessa/Vennessa.md` § Naming. End.4b ends with Indigo "looking at Vennessa like she is seeing her for the first time," and this is the one-word evidence that it stuck. **If this change is unwanted, cut the two lines and nothing else in the scene depends on them.**

### C.5 -- Last Look


**Notes:** The final image is the thesis. The prophecy -- the thing both armies died over, the thing Indigo was told she was -- resolves in the sky behind a crew who are not watching it. What they are doing instead is bandaging each other, arguing about routes, and keeping a boy alive by talking to him.

Hold on the wide long enough that the player has to choose what to look at, and make sure the golden child is not in the center of frame.

---

## Script Notes

### Voice Direction -- IndigoLLiy

- Her cockiness is stripped back in this chapter. The wish-machine lie guts her emotionally. She enters Level 1 as the cocky fighter from Ch3. She exits Level 3 as someone who made a catastrophic mistake with full confidence.
- "Kim." -- voiced. One word. The most important line she delivers. No cockiness, no deflection. Just his name.
- Post-lie Indigo does not collapse. She does not scream. She stands. The quiet is worse than any reaction.
- "Then what am I?" -- the question she cannot answer. Not self-pity. Genuine confusion.
- Her mockery of Jack is sharp but purposeful. She is needling someone who hunted her. The cruelty is incidental.

### Voice Direction -- Kim

- Quieter than any previous chapter. He heard the wish machine discovered -- he is in her ear, he hears what she hears -- but he never brings it up, and she never tells him it is why she is going. He does not know she is doing all of this for him.
- "You're pushing harder than usual." -- he sees the pattern but not the reason. He trusts her when he probably should not.
- His concern is personal, not tactical. He does not give combat advice during the tiger captain mauling or the guardian fight. He worries about her, not the mission.
- The gap between what Kim knows and what Indigo knows is the chapter's emotional engine. The player sits in that gap the entire time.
- **In the credits (C.4) he gets his real voice back.** Funny, fast, goofy -- the register from his profile that this chapter has kept in a box. He is the only person aboard who does not owe her a reassurance, because that conversation happened without him, so he can just be her friend.
- He asks the direct question and accepts a lie. "...Was it." is not an accusation. It is him deciding not to make her say it today.
- "I'm dead. You have to be nice to me." -- thrown away, light, a bit he has clearly done before. When she snaps at him for it, he does not defend the joke and does not apologize for it. He simply agrees with her and drops it. He is the socially smarter of the two and always has been.
- "Keep talking to me." -- no weight on it. He is asking her to keep him alive and dressing it as wanting company, because he knows what it does to her to hear it the other way.

### Voice Direction -- Kat

- Surveillance specialist mode for most of the chapter. Calm, precise, feeding intel. "He's not pursuing. He's moving east." Professional.
- Her reassurance in the ending is the most important factual statement in the scene. She checked the Mirralata data before they left. That is who she is.
- The moment she catches Ys's joke: her face does not change. It never does. But the camera holds on her one beat too long, and the audience should feel the weight shift. Stoic face, bubbly voice -- but not now. Now she is silent.
- **In the credits (C.2) she is back to full volume and it is a performance.** Warm voice, completely stoic face -- her established mode. The gear change out of tactical kit is the cue that she has gone off duty, and it is also the exact thing that makes Brod unable to look at her.
- Her hands stay clinical the entire scene no matter what her mouth is doing. She is genuinely treating him. The medicine is not a pretext for the flirting; the flirting is riding on top of real work.
- **The two-line quiet in the middle is the only true thing she says.** She stops talking, Brod notices, and she covers it with "Careful. That almost sounded like you know me." Play the cover as effortless. The audience should only register what happened on a second viewing.
- She is doing all of this roughly four minutes after her husband told her, in a joke nobody else could parse, that he is still going to hand Indigo over. Nobody aboard knows they are married. There is nowhere for it to go, so it goes into Brod.

### Voice Direction -- Captain Ys

- Skeptical of the wish machine. "That is a story in an old archive." He does not buy it, and he says so out loud in 1.6 before he assigns anyone anything. His stated priorities are the two things he can verify: a tiger captain hunting them inside the structure, and a ship that does not fly.
- He lets Indigo go. He could stop her. He does not. The prophecy child needs to be strong enough, and stopping her means explaining why.
- His objection is one word: "No." He does not argue. Indigo does not argue. The entire exchange is two people who both know what is going to happen.
- The dark joke: flat delivery. No change in tone. The double meaning is invisible to everyone except Kat.
- **The credits (C.3) are the one place in the game he is funny, and that is canon rather than a liberty.** His humor is reserved for the outer circle -- Robert and Vennessa specifically. Brod and Kat never receive it. The register shift should read as a reveal, not a slip.
- The humor is still bone dry and still delivered flat. "You keep being useful. It is an awkward combination" is affection, and it is the most he is structurally capable of.
- **"Keep it."** -- two words, no eye contact, said while marking a chart. This is him telling Vennessa the ship is going to Mayler. Do not let him soften it, explain it, or look at her. Anything warmer breaks it.
- The pen stopping on "getting people where they're going" is the only involuntary thing he does all chapter. A fraction of a second. He recovers immediately and Robert never notices.

### Voice Direction -- Brod

- His warning about the tiger captain is the most important thing he says. "Kinda tough." Understated. He is not a good fighter and he knows it -- so for him to rate someone is significant.
- Functional throughout. Drawing. Fighting. Augmenting the cannon. No complaints. The Avolin demon stays quiet this chapter because Brod has a reason to keep going that does not require threats.
- "Worth a look." -- his honest assessment of the Mirralata center. Not a speech. Not a promise. Just a kid giving his take and going back to work.

### Voice Direction -- Tiger Captain

- Violence and contempt. Every line is either a command or a dismissal.
- "Good. You're learning." / "Good. You didn't die a needless death being stupid." -- both are sincere. He genuinely believes he is being kind. The cruelty is not intentional. He is incapable of seeing Jack as an equal, so his praise is always condescending.
- He does not stop fighting when the world ends around him. The armies are destroyed, his mission is meaningless, and he still comes after the crew. That is the scariest thing about him.

### Voice Direction -- Jack

- Silent for most of the chapter. Contrast with Chapter 2 Jack -- the relentless hunter, the squad leader, the wolf who leads from the front and never delegates the hard fight.
- He obeys the tiger captain twice, and both times it costs him. He stays out of the mauling in the corridor. He accepts the stand-down in the arena -- and holds it right up until the guardian rises, then breaks it and gets swatted across the floor. Obeying earns him a backhanded compliment. Disobeying earns him a broken body and proof that the tiger captain was right. Both outcomes tell him the same thing: he was not needed.
- Indigo's mockery lands on top of the tiger captain's dismissal. Two enemies agree on the same thing: Jack moved for the fight that did not matter and stood still for the one that did.
- He does not follow to the final fight. The wolf learned to sit. He hates what it taught him.

### Voice Direction -- Robert

- "Clear." -- voiced. One word. Steady. The mechanic pulls the trigger. Her first contribution to a kill.
- Practical reassurance in the ending. She does not understand prophecies or wish machines. She understands ships. "We're still flying." That is what she can offer.
- Her confidence with the cannon is the same confidence she had with the puzzle room mechanisms in Ch3. Same logic, different scale.
- **Her laugh in the credits (C.3) is the first genuine one in four chapters.** Small, surprised, out before she can stop it. She is not a person who laughs on this ship and she should sound faintly startled by it.
- When Ys asks how the shot is sitting with her, she does not deflect, because she is not capable of it. She thinks about the question properly and answers it. "I don't feel bad about it" is the truth arrived at honestly, not bravado.
- Low social volume throughout, as always. She drifts into the scene because there is a chart, not because she wants to talk.

### Voice Direction -- Tablet

- Silent throughout the chapter except for her one sentence at the end.
- **She is the chapter's tech backbone.** She interfaces with the ancient archive in 1.5, relays gravity-gate solutions to Indigo remotely in Level 2, and projects containment lock-points for the tiger captain in 4.5. Three separate jobs, all load-bearing, all done without a word. She was a digital device before she was a person -- plugging into a still-running data system is the closest thing she has to a native language.
- Communicates via holographic projections -- archive text, puzzle sequences, confirmation glyphs, trajectory calculations. The crew sees tech. The player should start to see intent. She is not dumping everything she finds; she is deciding what matters and surfacing that. Nobody in the room notices the difference.
- Nobody thanks her. Nobody speaks to her. Brod reads her projections aloud and the crew responds to Brod. This is not cruelty -- it genuinely does not occur to them that there is anyone to address. That accumulated silence is what End.6 pays off.
- "You did enough." -- see options below. Private to Brod. Blunt. No cushioning. The first time all chapter that the flow reverses and she is the one offering something unprompted.

About Ys -- referencing the fact that Brod augmented the cannon, fought, and contributed without being told. Tablet noticed. She is saying what Ys would never say: that Brod's loyalty does not need commands. Reveals sentience through observation and judgment.

### Voice Direction -- Sketch

- **Book form for the entire chapter.** Sketch has never manifested her humanoid form in front of the crew and does not do it here. The only person who has ever seen it is Brod, alone, at the husky camp in Ch1 L4. Ys and Kat know she is sentient and have chosen not to say so. Everyone else reads the sketchbook as a piece of Brod's kit.
- Loud, animated, and constantly talking -- but pitched low, to Brod only, the way she has spoken since Ch1. Anything the crew needs to hear, Brod says himself. To Indigo, Robert, and Vennessa, this looks like a mercenary muttering at his sketchbook. They have long since stopped asking.
- **She sits out the archive scene (1.5).** Her power is animating Brod's drawings. She does not read data, interface with hardware, or process archives -- that is Tablet's function, and Sketch knows it. She stays closed under his arm. If she says anything at all it is a private, unhelpful aside to Brod.
- Silent during the emergence (E.3) -- not spent yet, just shocked. She has no comment for a planet-sized demon eating two armies.
- **The cannon augmentation in 4.4 is her chapter.** That is where her actual power carries the crew: Brod draws, Sketch makes his half real and holds it together long enough to fire. **Vennessa's half is not hers to touch** -- Vennessa has always cast her own work, and Sketch has no relationship to her magic whatsoever. Sketch speaks up in this scene because Brod needs her, then goes quiet for good.
- Fully spent by the ending beat. Animating that much, at that scale, at that density, costs her everything she had. The exhaustion should read as earned by the cannon, not by paperwork.
- Her absence from the ending beat is deliberate. Sketch is always talking. When Sketch is silent, things are bad.
- **Her silence in the credits (C.2) is the opposite kind of silence and must read differently.** Kat is present, so the book stays a book -- but Sketch is the Ch1 L4 wingman who coined "she likes you" and "your face is red," and Kat kneeling in front of Brod with her hands on him is the funniest thing that has ever happened to her. She is not spent here. She is gagged. Play it entirely through the page corner lifting and Brod's elbow coming down on it.

### Voice Direction -- Vennessa

- Minimal dialogue for almost the entire chapter. Injured but functional. Small, quiet, holding her arm, present.
- Her contribution to the cannon augmentation is critical -- her brush strokes are more refined than Brod's sword-pen lines. Same talent level, better tools. **She casts her own work; Sketch has nothing to do with it.** See the Sketch block above.
- **She breaks that silence exactly twice inside the chapter proper, and both are with Indigo.** One line at 1.7 as Indigo leaves for the machine, and the End.4b speech. Everything in between is silence, and the silence is what makes the ending beat land. Her credits scene (C.3) sits outside that count -- the chapter is over by then and the rules relax.
- **1.7 is a throwaway and must be played as one.** "I hope it's down there" gets no reaction from anyone including Indigo. The audience is not meant to understand it yet.
- **End.4b is the most she talks in the game and she is bad at it.** Halting, plain, no rhetoric, no rising cadence. She is not delivering a speech, she is failing to not say something. The power is entirely in the content. If the performance sounds practiced, it is wrong.
- **The star cat is the only subject that gets this out of her.** Nowhere else in four chapters does she raise it, because it is not a secret -- the whole crew has known since Ys caught her in the hold, and not one of them has asked about it since. She is not confessing. She is mentioning something old.
- Her relationship to Indigo is recognition, not friendship. She is not comforting Indigo and she is not on her side against Ys. She is the one person aboard who has organised her whole life around reaching one being at an unreasonable price, and she says so once.
- **Kat calls her by name. Everyone else calls her "the mouse."** That holds for the whole of this chapter and every chapter before it. See `GameLore/Characters/Vennessa/Vennessa.md` § Naming.
- **One deliberate exception, at the last possible moment.** In C.4 Kim says "the mouse girl" and Indigo corrects him to "Vennessa." It is the only time in four chapters that anyone other than Kat uses her name, it is placed after End.4b so that it reads as consequence, and Vennessa is not present to hear it. **This is a flagged change, not an established rule.** If it is unwanted, cut the two lines in C.4 and this bullet; nothing else depends on either.
- She is also the one who lands the accidental knife in C.3 -- "You do a lot of this. Getting people where they're going." She means it as an observation about a competent captain. Play it completely innocent. She has no idea what she has just said.

### Voice Direction -- Heavenly Voice / Demon God

- **Heavenly Voice:** Warm. Resonant. Sacred. It should feel earned. The player should believe the wish is real when this voice speaks. The tone is gentle, almost parental -- "You have proven yourself worthy" delivered like a benediction.
- **Demon God:** The laugh is the transition. The same voice, curdled. Ancient cruelty. "Thank you" is the most terrifying word because it is sincere -- the demon is genuinely grateful to Indigo for freeing it.
- The demon's voiced lines in E.2 should hit like the ground shifting under the player's feet.

### Bark Volume

- Tiger captain combat: heavy impacts, brutal strikes. His attacks should sound like structural damage, not combat.
- Guardian fight: massive scale. Each swing should shake the audio. The player should feel the size difference.
- Phase 2 crew preparations: the cannon's charge building should be an escalating hum. Ys powering the ship should sound like something straining -- energy forced through circuits not designed for it.
